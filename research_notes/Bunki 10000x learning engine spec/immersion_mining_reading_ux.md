## KQ1. Mining pipelines end to end (Yomitan + Anki, asbplayer, Language Reactor, Migaku, mokuro, Textractor/Agent/GSM, Jimaku/kitsunekko, subs2srs) and the "perfect card"

### Takeaway
The community-standard pipeline is: text made selectable (subtitles, texthooker, OCR'd manga, ebook reader) -> Yomitan popup lookup -> one-click AnkiConnect note with dictionary fields -> a second tool (asbplayer / mpvacious / GameSentenceMiner / mokuro-reader) *updates the last card* with sentence audio, screenshot and source metadata. The "perfect card" is a one-target (1T) sentence card with expression, reading/furigana, glossary, pitch accent (graph + position), frequency, sentence with furigana, native word audio, sentence audio, screenshot, source/URL/title, and tags.

### Inferences
- A spec-ready "perfect card" schema, derived from the Yomitan marker set plus what asbplayer/mpvacious/GSM add: `expression, reading, furigana (ruby), sentence, sentence_furigana, cloze_prefix/body/suffix, glossary (full + brief/first), pitch_accent_graph, pitch_accent_position, pitch_category (heiban/atamadaka/nakadaka/odaka), frequency_harmonic_rank, word_audio, sentence_audio (opus, padded), screenshot (webp/avif, optionally cropped), source_title, source_url, media_timestamp, tags, conjugation/part_of_speech`. Word audio and screenshot are widely treated as optional; sentence + target + reading + source are the non-negotiables.
- The two-phase pattern (Yomitan creates, media tool "updates last card") exists because no single tool owns both the dictionary and the media; a native app can collapse this into one gesture, which is the main UX win available.
- VAD-trimmed sentence audio, screenshot black-bar removal and history-based "mine the previous line" (GSM) are the current bar for game/video mining polish.

## KQ2. Reader UX that works (lookup ladders, furigana policies, pitch coloring, vertical text, page vs scroll, audio sync, known-word gestures, coverage indicators)

### Takeaway
Best-in-class readers converge on: per-word status coloring driven by an SRS/known-word DB (jpdb, Migaku, LingQ, asbplayer); furigana shown only for kanji the learner does not know (Satori Reader's "According to my knowledge", Yomu's "adaptive furigana"); a pre-reading coverage/difficulty indicator (jpdb stats pages, Migaku difficulty, Manabi familiarity %); vertical/horizontal and paginated/continuous modes with per-session character-count and chars/hour stats (ttsu); and hover/tap lookups with a modifier or long-press to avoid accidental popups on touch.

### Inferences
- Furigana policy spec: three modes (all / none / by-knowledge), where by-knowledge resolves at kanji granularity (Satori) and optionally by JLPT level (Yomu); a fourth "first occurrence only" mode is common in printed graded readers but I found no shipping app documenting it (see Gaps).
- Known-word gestures in the wild are keyboard-first (Shift-hover, Alt+P, right-click) on desktop and tap/long-press on mobile; a native app should give a single-tap lookup + swipe/long-press "mark known / ignore / track" with statuses at least {unknown, seen, learning, known, ignored, tracked}.
- Coverage indicator should be shown on the content card before opening (jpdb/Migaku pattern) and computed as % of running tokens known, with the 95/98% thresholds as color bands.
- Reading stats (characters read, chars/hour, heatmap) are expected by the ttsu/mokuro community and double as immersion-time tracking.

## KQ3. AI-generated graded content in shipping products (Duolingo, Speak, LingQ, Migaku vs Satori's human approach): level control, vocabulary verification, audio, user perception, failure modes

### Takeaway
As of 2026, Migaku ships a known-word-driven AI short story generator and Duolingo ships LLM conversation (Video Call) that "adapts to your level", while Satori Reader explicitly markets human-written, human-recorded content. Academic work shows LLMs default to near-native complexity (bad for A1-A2), produce 13-16% unusable example sentences, and have measurable kanji-reading (yomi) and honorific-register failures, so any generated Japanese needs a vocabulary-constrained generation loop plus deterministic reading/segmentation checks.

### Cited Findings
- Migaku (snippets): "Migaku has introduced a new AI Short Story Generator feature... Users can create stories based on their known words via the 'Wand' button"; recent versions "lean harder into AI-generated cards, automatic definitions, and example sentence suggestions". — [Migaku EA Google Play (snippet)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.migaku.android.alpha&hl=en_NZ); [immit Migaku review 2026 (snippet)](https://immit.co/blog/migaku-review-2026-is-it-worth-it-for-japanese-learners)
- Duolingo Video Call (snippets): "Designed to simulate natural dialogue, the feature provides a personalized, interactive experience tailored to your skill level"; on iOS available for Japanese and Korean, Android "Japanese and Korean coming soon" (at press-release time); "Duolingo has leveraged GPT-4". Lily "adapts to your level, and even remembers what you've said in past calls." — [Duolingo investor release (snippet)](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-ai-powered-video-call-android); [duoplanet (snippet)](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-video-call/)
- Satori Reader (snippet): stories "written by humans rather than generated"; all content "written, edited, recorded, and annotated by people". — [Lexirise (snippet)](https://lexirise.app/blog/article/satori-reader-alternative)
- LingQ (snippet): "thousands of hours of lessons generated from podcasts, books, interviews... all with matching text"; tracks known/learning/mastered words. — [LingQ Play (snippet)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.linguist&hl=en_US)
- Difficulty control research (snippet of arXiv 2506.04072): "Most LLMs generate text at a near-native level of complexity, making them ill-suited for first and second-year beginner learners (CEFR: A1-A2)." — [Controlling Difficulty of Generated Text for AI-Assisted Language Learning (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04072)
- Example-sentence generation for L2 Japanese (snippet of arXiv 2506.03580): rejection rates for "unnatural sentences and those with confusing errors" were 13% (LLM-jp) and 16% (GPT-3.5); "segmentation errors appeared in generation baselines, such as sentences starting with punctuation or with fragments." — [Automatically Suggesting Diverse Example Sentences for L2 Japanese Learners (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03580)
- Kanji reading (snippet of YOMI-Bench, arXiv 2607.00664): "Even models that achieve high performance on kanji reading tasks show low accuracy on rhyme selection, and knowledge of readings is not necessarily linked to their use"; Japanese-specific open models "almost incapable of generating rhymes". — [YOMI-Bench (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00664)
- Register/honorifics (snippet): "mistakes in the direction or level of politeness of honorifics can result in unnatural sentences... it becomes difficult to use in actual applications." — [Human Science: Which LLM is best for Japanese, 2026 (snippet)](https://www.science.co.jp/en/nmt/45726/)
- Japanese-tuned models (snippet): "Japanese-tuned models like LLM-jp-4 able to rephrase sentences at your level, generate example sentences for grammar points". — [Migaku blog: Learning Japanese in 2026 (snippet)](https://migaku.com/blog/japanese/learning-japanese-in-2026-what-actually-works)
- jidoujisho already integrates "ChatGPT integration for grammar explanations and examples" alongside corpus sources (Massif, Tatoeba, ImmersionKit). — [lrorpilla/jidoujisho](https://github.com/lrorpilla/jidoujisho)
- G2P benchmark exists for Japanese LLM kanji-reading accuracy (title only; content not fetched). — [Benchmarking LLMs for Grapheme-to-Phoneme Conversion: A Japanese Case Study (title only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22009)

### Inferences
- Production pattern implied by Migaku + the research: generate from an explicit allowed-vocabulary list (learner's known set + N target words), then post-check with a tokenizer (MeCab/Sudachi) that every content token is in the allowed set or is a whitelisted target, regenerate or rewrite failing sentences, and never let the LLM emit furigana/readings directly; derive readings deterministically (dictionary/tokenizer) and only use the LLM for disambiguation when multiple readings exist.
- Expected failure modes to test for: near-native complexity drift; unnatural collocations (13-16% reject rate baseline); segmentation artefacts (leading punctuation, fragments); wrong kanji readings (especially names, heteronyms); wrong honorific direction/level; hallucinated dialogue register for characters.
- Audio: shipping products use TTS for generated text (Duolingo, Migaku - unverified specifics) while Satori uses human recordings; a quality bar is per-sentence audio with sentence-level alignment for karaoke highlighting.
- User perception: reviews and community blogs consistently list "human-written, natural audio, per-sentence notes" as Satori's differentiator, which suggests users notice and penalize generic/unnatural generated text; no quantitative user-rating data was found (gap).

## KQ4. Listening UX (transcript-synced audio, speed, shadowing, dictation, pitch-accent visualisation)

### Takeaway
Listening tools in this ecosystem are subtitle-centric: asbplayer/mpvacious/GSM give auto-pause at subtitle boundaries, condensed audio, subtitle-list seeking and sentence-audio clipping; jidoujisho does subtitle-synced local/YouTube playback and lyrics lookup; pitch visualisation for learners is dominated by OJAD's accent-above-kana and Suzuki-kun's Fujisaki-model pitch contours with TTS playback. Dedicated shadowing/dictation modes were not found in the immersion toolset.

### Cited Findings
- asbplayer: "Condensed playback (skip unsubtitled sections)", "Fast-forward playback (accelerate through unsubtitled sections)", "Auto-pause (pause at subtitle boundaries)", "Seek through subtitles using a navigable subtitle list", subtitle offset adjustment. — [asbplayer README](https://github.com/killergerbah/asbplayer)
- jidoujisho: video player for local files, YouTube and Jellyfin with SRT/ASS/SSA subtitles, "Audio playback with subtitle synchronization", background playback with media notifications, "Lyrics lookup for music streaming apps". — [lrorpilla/jidoujisho](https://github.com/lrorpilla/jidoujisho)
- GameSentenceMiner: VAD "to automatically record and trim voice lines" and replay of previous lines for mining. — [bpwhelan/GameSentenceMiner](https://github.com/bpwhelan/GameSentenceMiner)
- Language Reactor (snippet): auto-pause, dual subtitles, and Pro "speech recognition for all audio tracks and clear subtitles for dubbed content". — [Language Reactor FAQ (snippet)](https://www.languagereactor.com/help/faq)
- Satori Reader (snippet): "natural audio" for every episode. — [Lexirise (snippet)](https://lexirise.app/blog/article/satori-reader-alternative)
- Yomu Yomu (snippet): every story "includes audio". — [Yomu Yomu Play (snippet)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.sinamon.yomuyomu)
- OJAD (snippets): "The pitch curve of each form is drawn on its hiragana representation using the Fujisaki model"; offers "visual accent pattern of verb forms (auxiliary verb + postpositional words)", "intonation pattern (high/low) of any sentence together with the positions of kernel accent"; ~423,000 accents for 12 forms of 3,500 words; Suzuki-kun lets users select "Pitch Contour" or "Accent Above Text", then "listen to audio playback with male or female voice options". — [OJAD Suzuki-kun usage (snippet)](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/usage_suzukikun); [ERIC technical analysis of OJAD (snippet)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1127234.pdf); [ISCA Interspeech 2013 OJAD paper (snippet)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2013/nakamura13_interspeech.pdf)
- Yomitan renders pitch as `'text' | 'graph' | 'position'` with nasal/devoiced marks via the `pronunciation` helper; asbplayer adds "Accent annotation" on subtitles. — [yomitan templates.md](https://github.com/yomidevs/yomitan/blob/master/docs/templates.md); [asbplayer README](https://github.com/killergerbah/asbplayer)

### Inferences
- A listening screen spec consistent with the tooling: transcript list with active-line highlight, tap-line-to-seek, auto-pause per line, A-B repeat of the current line, "condensed" mode that skips silence, 0.75x/1.0x speed, and one-tap "mine this line" that clips audio with padding and grabs a frame.
- Pitch visualisation options with precedent: (1) per-word accent number/graph (Yomitan-style), (2) accent marks above kana (OJAD), (3) sentence-level H/L contour curve (Suzuki-kun). Sentence contours require a phrasing model; word-level graphs can be derived from pitch dictionaries (NHK/Daijirin-style data used by Yomitan pitch dictionaries).
- Shadowing and dictation are not features of the mining ecosystem; they are opportunities rather than table stakes for immersion learners.

### Gaps
- No primary sources reached for karaoke-style word highlighting, shadowing or dictation modes in Satori, Migaku, LingQ or Todai; the OJAD site itself was blocked (snippets only).
- Speed control ranges and TTS voices in shipping graded readers not verified.

## KQ5. Rights-clear immersion sources (NHK Easy, Wikinews/Wikipedia, Aozora, Tatoeba, CC podcasts/YouTube) and community norms for copyrighted media

### Takeaway
Tatoeba text is CC BY 2.0 FR (partly CC0) with attribution required, but Tatoeba audio is per-contributor licensed and most commonly CC BY-NC 4.0, so audio needs per-clip license checks; NHK Easy is scraped by unofficial clients but its terms could not be read here; the community treats fan-ripped subtitles (kitsunekko/Jimaku) as volunteer-collected files with no stated license, used for personal mining rather than redistribution inside products.

### Cited Findings
- Tatoeba (snippets from tatoeba.org and its wiki): "Tatoeba files are released under CC BY 2.0 FR, with a part of sentences also available under CC0 1.0"; "The license covering an audio file is chosen by the contributor... The most popular license at this time on tatoeba.org is CC BY-NC 4.0"; attribution: "write somewhere that some/all of your sentences are from Tatoeba, with a link to https://tatoeba.org, and mention that Tatoeba's data is released under CC-BY 2.0 FR"; for audio "mention the username of each member whose audio you are reusing, as well as the license they chose." — [Tatoeba terms of use (snippet)](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use); [Tatoeba downloads (snippet)](https://tatoeba.org/en/downloads); [Tatoeba wiki: audio licenses (snippet)](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/choose-audio-license)
- NHK NEWS WEB EASY provides simplified news "with furigana, built-in dictionary gloss for certain words, and even an audio recording of the article"; an unofficial client ecosystem (nhk-easy-mobile, nhk-easy-api, nhk-news-fetcher-go) exists under MIT for the client code, and its org contains a repo named "nhk-easy-mobile-terms-and-conditions". — [livingjapanese.com (snippet)](http://livingjapanese.com/pages/nhk-news-web-easy.html); [nhk-news-web-easy GitHub org](https://github.com/nhk-news-web-easy)
- NHK's current Easy news URL is news.web.nhk/news/easy/. — [NHK NEWS WEB EASY (search result)](https://news.web.nhk/news/easy/)
- manga-ocr is Apache-2.0 ("permitting commercial and private use with appropriate attribution"); jimaku is AGPL-v3; jidoujisho GPL-3.0. — [manga-ocr](https://github.com/kha-white/manga-ocr); [jimaku](https://github.com/Rapptz/jimaku); [jidoujisho](https://github.com/lrorpilla/jidoujisho)
- kitsunekko-mirror hosts volunteer-collected .srt/.ass files with no license statement for the subtitle content. — [Ajatt-Tools/kitsunekko-mirror](https://github.com/Ajatt-Tools/kitsunekko-mirror)
- Community mining is framed as personal use: users "supply subtitles yourself" (asbplayer) and Netflix/YouTube subtitles are auto-detected in the user's own browser session rather than redistributed. — [asbplayer README](https://github.com/killergerbah/asbplayer); [Lexirise (snippet)](https://lexirise.app/blog/article/language-reactor-alternative)

### Inferences
- For a product: Tatoeba sentences are safe to ship with an attribution screen; Tatoeba audio must be filtered by license (CC BY / CC0 only if commercial) and credited per contributor. Aozora Bunko public-domain texts and Wikimedia (CC BY-SA) are standard choices in this space, but their terms could not be verified in this session (see Gaps).
- Fan subtitles should stay user-supplied (import from file / Jimaku via the user's own API key) rather than bundled; streaming subtitles should be read from the user's own session (asbplayer/Language Reactor/Migaku model).

### Gaps
- NHK terms of use (nhk.or.jp) unreachable; whether NHK Easy articles/audio may be reproduced in an app is unverified. From prior knowledge (unverified): NHK's site terms prohibit reproduction beyond private use without permission.
- Aozora Bunko usage rules (aozora.gr.jp, and the aozorabunko GitHub README) unreachable; prior knowledge (unverified): expired-copyright works are free to use with the Aozora notation kept, while some works are contributed under CC or "with permission" and are not PD.
- Wikinews/Wikipedia Japanese license pages unreachable; prior knowledge (unverified): Wikipedia is CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikinews is CC BY 2.5.
- CC-licensed Japanese podcasts and YouTube channels allowing reuse: no sources retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## KQ6. Native mobile constraints (iOS Safari extension limits, Android accessibility overlays, share-sheet capture, PWA vs native for offline dictionaries)

### Takeaway
Yomitan does not run on iOS: Safari forks report "Popup doesn't work in Safari" and "Adding cards to Anki doesn't work in Safari", and the Orion-based port loses text scanning and replaces AnkiConnect with the AnkiMobile URL scheme; iOS learners use purpose-built Safari extensions (Yomikiri, 10ten) that add cards via AnkiMobile. Android has richer options: accessibility-service OCR overlays (InstantJPDict, PopLingo), process-text/share popups (Rin, Popup Japanese Dictionary), and full native suites (jidoujisho) that import Yomitan-format dictionaries and export to AnkiDroid with audio/images. PWAs (ttsu, mokuro-reader) offer offline reading but not system-wide lookup.

### Cited Findings
- yomichan-safari fork: "Safari Web Extension" compiled through Xcode for iOS/iPadOS/macOS; known limitations "Popup doesn't work in Safari" and "Adding cards to Anki doesn't work in Safari"; workaround setting "Use a native browser window instead of an embedded popup". — [Michael-128/yomichan-safari](https://github.com/Michael-128/yomichan-safari)
- yomitan-ios fork: runs Yomitan as a Chrome extension inside the Orion iOS browser; "AnkiConnect does not exist on iOS, so this fork uses the Anki URL scheme to add cards via AnkiMobile instead"; text scanning (popup on hover/selection) does not work "due to Apple API constraints"; install by building the extension zip and sideloading into Orion. — [tysonwu/yomitan-ios](https://github.com/tysonwu/yomitan-ios)
- Yomitan issue "Support for Safari?" (#66) is still open with 48 comments and was updated 2026-09-25; labels include "platform/mac-only" and "triage/not-our-bug". — [yomidevs/yomitan#66](https://github.com/yomidevs/yomitan/issues/66)
- Search snippet: "Yomitan doesn't run on iOS, so there's no Yomitan in Safari on an iPhone or iPad"; alternatives: Yomikiri ("a Safari Extension that lets you look up words you don't know with just a tap... supports Anki integration... add them to AnkiMobile") and 10ten Reader (works in "Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and iOS Safari"). — [Yomikiri App Store (snippet)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yomikiri/id6479743831); [Lexirise Yomitan manga article (snippet)](https://lexirise.app/blog/article/yomitan-manga-images)
- InstantJPDict (Android): "On-device OCR" with PP-OCRv6 small models (PaddleOCR), runs "as an Android Accessibility Service", triggered "via the accessibility button or volume-down double-press", screen-capture or camera mode, lookups against "imported Yomitan-format dictionaries", deinflection, and OCR-error correction via "the text recognition model's own prediction ratings, plus a lightweight n-gram model and kanji decomposition table". — [vgf89/InstantJPDict](https://github.com/vgf89/InstantJPDict)
- PopLingo (snippets): "a system-wide overlay you can use inside any app", "built on the open-source dictionary standard compatible with Yomitan", marketed for "Netflix, manga, VNs, games". — [PopLingo Play (snippet)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.aktaris.chattranslator&hl=en_US); [WaniKani community thread (snippet)](https://community.wanikani.com/t/poplingo-system-wide-japanese-dictionary-overlay-on-android-netflix-manga-vns-games/73679)
- Popup Japanese Dictionary (snippet): "From any app that allows you to select and share words... can display the definition in a window overlay on top of your screen." — [Amazon Appstore listing (snippet)](https://www.amazon.com/Nifty-Gnomes-Popup-Japanese-Dictionary/dp/B00LGUN8OI)
- Rin (Android): "minimal and lightning fast Japanese popup dictionary", imports Yomichan dictionaries including "Frequency and pitch accent dictionaries", auto de-inflection, recursive lookups. — [kamui-fin/rin](https://github.com/kamui-fin/rin)
- jidoujisho (Android, Flutter): Yomichan/Migaku/DSL dictionary import with pitch and frequency, tap-to-lookup, ttsu-based EPUB/HTMLZ reader, mokuro manga, YouTube/local/Jellyfin video, AnkiDroid export with audio/images, WebSocket texthooker support; "Requires updated Android WebView". — [lrorpilla/jidoujisho](https://github.com/lrorpilla/jidoujisho)
- ttsu reader is a PWA with offline install and cloud sync; mokuro-reader supports installation and offline use. — [ttu-ttu/ebook-reader](https://github.com/ttu-ttu/ebook-reader); [ZXY101/mokuro-reader](https://github.com/ZXY101/mokuro-reader)
- Browser extension reader tools note only experimental mobile support (Kiwi Browser). — [max-kamps/jpd-breader](https://github.com/max-kamps/jpd-breader); [Kagu-chan/anki-jpdb.reader](https://github.com/Kagu-chan/anki-jpdb.reader)
- asbplayer produces a Firefox-for-Android extension build. — [asbplayer README](https://github.com/killergerbah/asbplayer)

### Inferences
- iOS: a native app cannot offer system-wide popup lookup; the practical ladder is (1) in-app reader/browser with tap lookup, (2) a Safari Web Extension for tap-lookup in Safari (Yomikiri/10ten pattern), (3) Share Sheet / clipboard capture into the app, (4) AnkiMobile URL scheme for card export. Bundled offline dictionaries need native storage rather than relying on extension IndexedDB.
- Android: an accessibility-service overlay with on-device OCR (InstantJPDict/PopLingo pattern) plus ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT/share targets gives near-Yomitan reach across apps, at the cost of Play policy scrutiny for accessibility usage (not verified in sources) and DRM black frames on protected video (not verified).
- Yomitan-format dictionary import is the de facto interchange standard on mobile (jidoujisho, Rin, InstantJPDict, PopLingo all consume it); supporting it lowers switching cost.

### Gaps
- Specific Apple constraints (Safari extension storage quotas, background limits, popup positioning on touch) were not documented in any fetched source; the #66 thread comments could not be retrieved (API 403).
- Google Play accessibility-service policy text and DRM behaviour for overlays not verified.
- Yomikiri's exact capabilities (offline dictionary size, share-sheet) come only from the App Store snippet.
