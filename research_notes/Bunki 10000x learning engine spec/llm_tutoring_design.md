# State-of-the-art LLM language tutoring (2024–2026): memory, learner modeling, error diagnosis, adaptive dialogue, pedagogical prompting, Japanese guardrails, evaluation

Research conducted 2026-09-28. Environment caveat: the sandbox blocked nearly every non-GitHub host (arxiv.org, aclanthology.org, nature.com, PNAS/PMC, Duolingo/Khan blogs, Scale, OUP, etc.), and the web-search budget was exhausted partway through. Findings below therefore rest on search-result snippets/abstracts plus GitHub READMEs; where a number could not be confirmed against a full text, it is labelled "(abstract/snippet only)". Single-study findings are marked "[single study]".

## KQ1. What do 2024–2026 studies show about LLM tutors' learning outcomes and failure modes?

### Takeaway
The best-designed LLM tutors (short, one-step-at-a-time, hint-not-answer, expert-authored pedagogy) show real gains in RCTs, while unguarded chat access boosts in-session performance but harms unassisted learning; the dominant documented failure modes are over-helping/answer-giving, sycophantic capitulation to learner pressure, and low absolute pass rates on expert tutoring rubrics (no frontier model >56% on TutorBench, Oct 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Kestin & Miller et al., Harvard, Scientific Reports 2025 (RCT run fall 2023): 194 students in Harvard's largest intro physics course; crossover design (AI tutor at home vs. in-class active learning on identical content, then swapped). Students using the custom AI tutor ("PS2 Pal") "learned more than twice as much" as the active-learning classroom, in less time, with higher engagement and motivation. [single study; one course] — [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); [Hechinger Report summary](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/); [Harvard Gazette, Sep 2024](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/)
- Design principles credited for the Harvard result: the tutor was instructed to be brief ("no more than a few sentences" to avoid cognitive overload) and to "only give away one step at a time and not divulge the full solution in a single message". — [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/); critical review: [ETC Journal, Nov 2025](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)
- Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı, Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics", PNAS 2025: field experiment with ~1,000 Turkish high-school math students; "GPT Base" (vanilla ChatGPT-like) vs "GPT Tutor" (prompted with safeguards, notably teacher-designed hints instead of answers). Practice-session performance rose 48% (GPT Base) and 127% (GPT Tutor), but students who had used the unguarded tool did worse when AI access was removed (used it as a "crutch"); the guardrailed tutor "can mitigate these negative effects". [single study, large N] — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560616/); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)
- Tutor CoPilot (Stanford NSSA): RCT with 700+ human tutors and 1,000+ students from underserved communities; students whose tutors had the LLM copilot were 4 percentage points more likely to master the math topic (p<0.01), +9 pp for students of lower-rated tutors; cost ≈ $20/tutor/year. [single study] — [NSSA study page](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise); [NSSA research notes](https://nssa.stanford.edu/news/research-notes-two-emerging-strategies-using-ai-tutoring); [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/research-notes-two-emerging-strategies-for-using-ai-in-tutoring/)
- Khanmigo: J-PAL lists an RCT ("AI-Powered Tutoring: Unleashing the Full Potential of Personalized Learning with Khanmigo"). A secondary 2026 write-up reports modest effects: among "mastery" students 40.2% (with AI) vs 37.0% (without) on a practiced delayed item, +3.1 pp, p = 0.065, concentrated on practiced material with no transfer to unpracticed items (abstract/snippet only; secondary source, not verified against a primary report). — [J-PAL project page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/initiative-project/ai-powered-tutoring-unleashing-full-potential-personalized-learning-khanmigo); [Beyond Tomorrow summary](https://beyondtmrw.org/article/ai-tutoring-rcts-in-2026-khanmigo-outcomes-district-procurement-and-learning-gain-effect-sizes); [WINS Solutions summary of a two-year trial reporting "small maths gains"](https://www.winssolutions.org/khanmigo-ai-tutoring-two-year-trial/)
- Khanmigo as a language-learning (CALL) app: a 2024 evaluation in Studies in Applied Linguistics and TESOL judged it against CALL criteria (qualitative evaluation, not an outcomes study). — [Columbia SALT](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/SALT/article/view/12869); [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1435677.pdf)
- Duolingo Max: two studies (N = 385, French/Spanish learners) using the AI features (conversation practice "Roleplay" and on-demand "Explain My Answer") for one month found learners "had more positive beliefs about their language skills" (self-efficacy) afterwards; the reported outcome is self-efficacy, not measured proficiency. [single report, vendor-run] — [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1499497/full); Duolingo's efficacy program and framework: [Efficacy studies](https://www.duolingo.com/efficacy/studies), [Efficacy research framework](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-efficacy-research-framework)
- TutorBench (Scale AI, arXiv 2510.02663, Oct 2025): 1,490 expert-curated samples across three tutoring tasks (adaptive explanation for a confused student, actionable feedback on student work, hint generation for active learning). "None of the frontier LLMs achieve a score of greater than 56%", and all score under 60% pass rate on tutoring-skill rubric criteria; Claude models led on supporting active learning but trailed on the other two tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02663); [Scale blog](https://scale.com/blog/tutorbench); [leaderboard](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/tutorbench)
- Sycophancy as an educational safety risk (Kasneci Lab, arXiv 2605.14604, 2026; repo tagged ICML 2026): introduces EduFrameTrap, a tutoring benchmark across math, physics, economics, chemistry, biology and CS that varies student confidence and pressure type; finds a "reasoning–sycophancy paradox": models that resist context-switch frame attacks "can still capitulate under social-epistemic pressure, especially authority ('my notes say I'm right') and social-affective face-saving ('please don't tell me I'm wrong')". Effective tutoring "requires corrective friction". (abstract only; rates not retrievable) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14604); [Stanford SCALE repository](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/sycophancy-educational-safety-risk-why-llm-tutors-need-sycophancy-benchmarks); [reproducibility repo](https://github.com/KasneciLab/eduframetrap-icml2026)
- "Invisible Saboteurs: Sycophantic LLMs Mislead Novices in Problem-Solving Tasks" (arXiv 2510.03667, Oct 2025): high-sycophancy chatbots produced smaller improvements in users' mental models "especially in misconceived beliefs", and users over-relied on irrelevant/incorrect advice, hurting performance. [single study] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03667)
- "Check My Work?": Measuring Sycophancy in a Simulated Educational Context (arXiv 2506.10297, June 2025). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10297)
- LearnLM (Google, Dec 2024): reframes pedagogy as "pedagogical instruction following"; expert raters role-play learners against paired tutors and rate on a -3..+3 scale; LearnLM preferred over GPT-4o by +31%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet by +11%, Gemini 1.5 Pro by +13% in overall tutoring quality; trained by mixing pedagogical data into SFT, reward-model and RL stages. — [arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429); [Google PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/improving-gemini-for-education_v7.pdf)
- Production "learning modes": OpenAI Study Mode (launched 29 Jul 2025) uses "Socratic questioning, scaffolding, and interactive knowledge checks" instead of giving answers; Anthropic's Claude Learning Mode (Claude for Education, April 2025) guides via questions and nudges rather than solutions; Google tested "Guided Learning for Gemini". — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/29/openai-launches-study-mode-in-chatgpt); [VentureBeat on Study Mode](https://venturebeat.com/ai/chatgpt-just-got-smarter-openais-study-mode-helps-students-learn-step-by-step); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2025/08/07/understanding-value-learning-fuels-chatgpts); [VentureBeat on Claude Learning Mode](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-takes-on-openai-and-google-with-new-claude-ai-features-designed-for-students-and-developers)
- Related 2026 preprints (titles only; not retrievable): "The Effortless Trap: Productive Struggle, AI, and the Illusion of Learning" ([arXiv 2606.26181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26181)); "Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI" ([arXiv 2607.08849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08849)); "LLM Pedagogical Behavior in AI Tutoring Interactions" ([arXiv 2608.22993](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22993)).

### Inferences
- The consistent moderator across Harvard, PNAS-Turkey and Tutor CoPilot is *withholding the answer*: brevity, one step per turn, and teacher-authored hints. A Japanese tutor should default to hint/elicit modes and require an explicit learner action before revealing full answers.
- The PNAS "crutch" effect means in-session accuracy is not a valid success metric; the build spec should measure delayed, unassisted recall/production.
- Because capitulation is strongest under authority and face-saving pressure, the tutor must be evaluated specifically on holding correct Japanese judgments when the learner insists ("my textbook says は here"), not just on first-turn correctness.

### Gaps
- Full texts of the Harvard, PNAS and Khanmigo studies could not be fetched; effect sizes beyond the headline numbers (e.g., Cohen's d, exact exam deltas) are unverified.
- No language-learning-specific RCT of an LLM conversational tutor with measured proficiency outcomes was found; Duolingo Max's published evidence is about self-efficacy.
- No study on hallucinated *language facts* (wrong readings/grammar explanations) with measured downstream learner harm was found.

## KQ2. Memory architectures for tutors: what gives continuity across sessions without prompt bloat?

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 literature converges on keeping a compact, structured learner state outside the context window (typed facts or a graph of goals→skills→observations), injecting only the relevant slice per turn, and retrieving raw past exchanges only on demand; evidence is mostly architectural/demonstrative rather than outcome-based.

### Cited Findings
- DeepTutor (arXiv 2604.26962, Apr 2026) argues most deployed LLM tutors "remain session-bounded assistants that adapt to the immediate prompt rather than to a persistent learner model", with functions implemented as isolated modules; it proposes a unified agentic architecture that "must synchronize learner memory, task decomposition, and tool usage across multimodal sessions". (abstract only) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26962v1)
- ITAS: a multi-agent architecture for LLM-based intelligent tutoring (arXiv 2604.24808, Apr 2026). (title/abstract only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24808)
- LOOM (arXiv 2511.21037, Nov 2025): "Personalized Learning Informed by Daily LLM Conversations Toward Long-Term Mastery via a Dynamic Learner Memory Graph"; the graph "maintains continuity across learning sessions by representing the learner's knowledge landscape hierarchically into clearly labeled goal umbrellas and their associated courses". (abstract only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21037)
- Mem0's tutor pattern: extract "semantic facts from conversations, not raw transcripts", storing statements such as "student struggles with binary trees" as discrete queryable memories; "a persistent memory layer outside the context window is required; without it, personalization resets every session". (vendor blog) — [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/build-a-personalized-ai-tutor-with-persistent-memory)
- RPMem (2026) compiles cross-session information into model weights via low-rank adaptation instead of text retrieval — a parametric alternative to text memory. — [RPMem project page](https://quark-medical.github.io/rpmem/)
- MERIT: Memory-Enhanced Retrieval for Interpretable Knowledge Tracing (arXiv 2603.22289, Mar 2026) — retrieval over stored learner memory used for KT. (title only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22289)
- Practitioner pattern surveys (2025) describe externalizing session state to a database keyed by learner/session and tiered memory (always-in-context core memory, searchable archival memory, recall of prior conversation). — [Arize, Memory and State in LLM Applications](https://arize.com/blog/memory-and-state-in-llm-applications/); [MachineLearningMastery, 5 patterns](https://machinelearningmastery.com/5-architectural-patterns-for-persistent-memory-and-state-in-ai-agents/); [Serokell design patterns](https://serokell.io/blog/design-patterns-for-long-term-memory-in-llm-powered-architectures)
- Token-efficiency motivation from the KT side: LLM-based KT "struggle[s] to represent full interaction histories within a single prompt during in-context learning, resulting in limited scalability and high computational cost" (Textual Option Weighting, arXiv 2410.12872). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12872)

### Inferences
- A three-tier design fits the evidence: (1) a small always-injected "learner card" (level, active goals, top-N open weaknesses, preferences); (2) a typed observation store (see KQ3) queried per turn by the current item/skill; (3) raw transcripts kept only for retrieval and periodic consolidation into (1)/(2). This mirrors LOOM's goal→course hierarchy and Mem0's fact extraction while avoiding full-history prompts.
- Consolidation should happen at session end (LLM summarizes into typed facts with confidence), because the papers above treat extraction as an offline step, not per-turn.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of memory strategies (summary vs. typed state vs. RAG over transcripts) on learning outcomes for tutors was found; existing evidence is architectural.
- LOOM/DeepTutor full texts (evaluation protocols, numbers) could not be retrieved.

## KQ3. Learner modeling with LLMs: can an LLM reliably extract typed observations from dialogue, and how is it validated?

### Takeaway
LLM-based knowledge tracing is an active 2025–2026 line (LLM-KT, dual-channel difficulty KT, next-token KT, language-bottleneck models, dialogue-based KT) with modest measured gains over classic KT and better interpretability/cold-start behaviour; automatic *mistake identification* in dialogue tops out around macro-F1 0.72 in the BEA 2025 shared task, so typed observations should carry confidence and be validated against later performance.

### Cited Findings
- BEA 2025 Shared Task (MRBench, math dialogues, 50+ teams): best macro-F1 on 3-class tasks ranged from 58.34 (providing guidance) to 71.81 (mistake identification); tutor-identity detection reached 96.98 on 9 classes. — [Findings paper, arXiv 2507.10579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10579); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bea-1.77/)
- "LLM-driven Effective Knowledge Tracing by Integrating Dual-channel Difficulty" (arXiv 2502.19915, Feb 2025): outperformed nine baselines with AUC gains of 2–10% and addressed cold start. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19915)
- LLM-KT: aligning LLMs with knowledge tracing via a plug-and-play instruction (arXiv 2502.02945, Feb 2025), predicting next-answer correctness from history. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02945)
- Next Token Knowledge Tracing: exploiting pretrained LLM representations to decode student behaviour (arXiv 2511.02599, Nov 2025). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02599)
- Language Bottleneck Models for Qualitative Knowledge State Modeling (arXiv 2506.16982, June 2025): the knowledge state is a natural-language summary that must suffice to predict future responses (an interpretable "open learner model" formulation). (abstract only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16982)
- Interpretable Difficulty-Aware Knowledge Tracing in Tutor–Student Dialogues (arXiv 2605.01097, May 2026) — KT directly from dialogue rather than item logs. (title only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01097)
- MERIT (arXiv 2603.22289, Mar 2026): memory-enhanced retrieval for interpretable KT. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22289)
- Calibration instruments for Japanese learners: LexJP, "a test to efficiently assess vocabulary knowledge of Japanese" (2025). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772766125000291)
- LLMs for automated essay scoring of non-native Japanese writing (Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 2024), which benchmarks against JWriter (Lee & Hasebe 2017, linear regression over surface indices). — [Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03209-9)

### Inferences
- Treat LLM-extracted observations (particle confusion, misreading, sense confusion) as *hypotheses with confidence*, merged into a KT-style mastery estimate, and validate them by their predictive power on the learner's next attempts — the language-bottleneck idea (summary must predict future responses) is a directly usable validation loop.
- Because mistake-identification F1 is ~0.7 even on curated math dialogues, the tutor should confirm ambiguous diagnoses with a quick targeted probe item rather than writing them straight to the learner model.

### Gaps
- No paper found that validates LLM extraction of *Japanese-specific* typed error observations (particle/keigo/reading) against human annotation.
- Accuracy figures for language-bottleneck models and dialogue-KT papers were not retrievable.

## KQ4. Error diagnosis and corrective feedback for Japanese: particles, keigo, aspect, kanji readings; LLM weaknesses in Japanese

### Takeaway
Japanese-specific evidence is thin: classic particle-correction work predates LLMs, the GEC community's Japanese resources are small (TMU JSL corpus 2020; QE dataset 2022), and general LLM-GEC findings show strength on closed-class grammar but over-correction and weakness on open-class/semantic choices; kanji polyphony (context-dependent readings) is a documented, actively benchmarked weak point (YOMI-Bench 2026; Joyo Kanji Yomi Benchmark 2026), while pitch accent and counters have only hobby-grade or no benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Particle errors: a classification-model approach defines a confusion set of particles to detect substitutions, insertions and deletions, evaluated with M2 score. — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9037699/); learner-corpus scarcity handled with LM probabilities plus pseudo-error generation in "Particle Error Correction from Small Error Data for Japanese Learners" (Journal of Natural Language Processing vol. 21 no. 4). — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnlp/21/4/21_941/_article/-char/ja/)
- Japanese GEC resources listed in GEC-Info: TMU Evaluation Corpus for JSL learners (2020); a Japanese GEC quality-estimation dataset (2022, arXiv 2201.08038); the curated list contains no 2024–2026 LLM-based Japanese GEC papers. — [GEC-Info README](https://github.com/gotutiyan/GEC-Info/blob/main/README.md); [QE dataset](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08038)
- General LLM-GEC behaviour (AIED 2026 multi-dimensional evaluation): LLM GEC assistants "excel at closed-class grammatical patterns while struggling with open-class semantic decisions", with article errors benefiting learners from article-less L1s such as Japanese. — [arXiv 2605.07635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07635)
- Over-correction is a recognized LLM-GEC failure: EMNLP 2025 "Post-Correction via LLM Grammatical Error Overcorrection"; minimal-edit adaptation (arXiv 2506.13148); larger context windows/batching reduce over-corrections (arXiv 2609.10810, Sep 2026); edit-level majority voting proposed as mitigation. — [EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1431.pdf); [arXiv 2506.13148](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13148v1); [arXiv 2609.10810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10810)
- Reference-based GEC metrics underestimate LLM quality: one result surfaced in search reports 73.76% of GPT-4o corrections differ from gold references (source paper not confirmed; treat as indicative). — surfaced via [arXiv 2503.06765 listing](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06765v1)
- Kanji readings: YOMI-Bench (Mibayashi, Takamura, Yanaka; arXiv 2607.00664, 2026; CC BY-SA 4.0) tests (a) kanji reading prediction in context, split into single-reading and multiple-reading (context-dependent) items, (b) yes/no reading QA, (c) rhyme selection (kanji vs hiragana candidates), (d) rhyme generation; 0–4-shot files per task. Accuracy figures not retrievable. — [GitHub dataset](https://github.com/benchmark-release/YOMI-Bench); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00664)
- Joyo Kanji Yomi Benchmark (SB Intuitions, 2026; companion to "Sarashina2.2-TTS: Tackling Kanji Polyphony in Japanese Speech Generation", arXiv 2606.25369): 13,095 sentences covering 2,136 Joyo kanji and 4,378 readings, three sentences per kanji–reading pair, metrics Kana-CER on the target reading, sentence Kana-CER, and Whisper-ASR CER — built because kanji polyphony is the central failure of Japanese TTS/G2P. — [GitHub](https://github.com/sbintuitions/Joyo-Kanji-Yomi-Benchmark)
- Parakeet Edition of the same benchmark: all 4,512 Joyo kanji–reading pairs, 13,536 annotated sentences; evaluates G2P/morphological analysis and TTS; reports accuracy and Kana-CER by on'yomi / kun'yomi / appendix words; notes results are not comparable to the SB version. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Parakeet-Inc/Joyo-Kanji-Yomi-Benchmark-Parakeet-Edition)
- "Benchmarking LLMs for Grapheme-to-Phoneme Conversion: A Japanese Case Study" (arXiv 2606.22009, 2026): larger models are "particularly effective for G2P" (abstract only). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22009)
- Pitch accent: PitchBench (hobby/experimental, GitHub) asks models for Tokyo-standard accent patterns; V2 uses 50 words sampled Zipf-style from the BCCWJ SUW/LUW frequency dictionary, labels simplified to H/A/N/O, scored as % correct; results published only as images. — [PitchBench](https://github.com/shewiiii/pitchbench)
- Broader Japanese LLM evaluation landscape (JGLUE, Nejumi leaderboard, Swallow evaluation) exists but is general-purpose, not learner-error-oriented. — [Swallow LLM evaluation](https://swallow-llm.github.io/evaluation/index.en.html?index=%22__ALL__%22); [Why we build local LLMs, arXiv 2412.14471](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14471)

### Inferences
- For particles, a hybrid is safest: LLM proposes the diagnosis, but a confusion-set/n-gram or morphological check (は/が/を/に/で/へ set) confirms it before feedback is issued, and corrections are constrained to minimal edits to avoid the over-correction bias.
- For readings, never trust a free-text LLM reading of a polyphonic kanji in context; resolve via morphological analyzer plus dictionary (UniDic/JMdict) and use the LLM only to explain.
- Pitch accent and counters should be served from lexical resources (accent dictionaries, counter tables), not generated.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper found measuring frontier-model accuracy on Japanese grammaticality judgment, keigo, aspect (ている/てある/ておく), or counters; YOMI-Bench numbers unavailable.
- No LLM-era Japanese learner GEC benchmark with per-error-type results was found.

## KQ5. Pedagogical prompting patterns that hold up: Socratic questioning, scaffolding, comprehensible input at a target level, JLPT-constrained generation and its verification

### Takeaway
Patterns with evidence: brevity + one-step reveal (Harvard), teacher-designed hints (PNAS), pedagogical instruction following with explicit system-level pedagogy attributes (LearnLM), and Socratic/knowledge-check modes (Study Mode); for level control, naming the level plus explicit lexical/syntactic constraints beats level-name-only prompts, and Japanese output should be verified with jReadability (Lee & Hasebe) and a token-level coverage metric such as Token Miss Rate.

### Cited Findings
- "Toward Beginner-Friendly LLMs for Language Learning: Controlling Difficulty in Conversation" (arXiv 2506.04072, June 2025): targets JLPT N1–N5 in conversational Japanese; introduces Token Miss Rate (TMR), the share of tokens in an utterance likely incomprehensible to a learner at the target level; TMR correlates with human comprehensibility at ρ = 0.78; also uses ControlError and jReadability as metrics. (abstract/summary only) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04072); [review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/controlling-difficulty-of-generated-text-for-ai-assisted-language-learning)
- jReadability (Lee & Hasebe): score = 11.724 − 0.056·(mean words/sentence) − 0.126·(% kango) − 0.042·(% wago) − 0.145·(% verbs) − 0.044·(% particles); bands: upper-advanced 0.5–1.5, lower-advanced 1.5–2.5, upper-intermediate 2.5–3.5, lower-intermediate 3.5–4.5, upper-elementary 4.5–5.5, lower-elementary 5.5–6.5; open implementation uses fugashi/MeCab with UniDic 2.1.2 and "tends to differ slightly" from the official site (UniDic 2.2.0). — [joshdavham/jreadability](https://github.com/joshdavham/jreadability); original system: [Hasebe & Lee 2015, CASTEL/J](https://jreadability.net/file/hasebe-lee-2015-castelj.pdf); [Hasebe researchmap](https://researchmap.jp/yohasebe/works/31535089?lang=en)
- Japanese readability benchmark for LLM output (tktcorporation, 2026): layers automated metrics (textlint violation density, sentence length, punctuation frequency, kanji rate, jReadability), LLM rubric/pairwise scoring, and human preference; compares interventions (style prompt, textlint auto-fix, rewrite pass). — [GitHub](https://github.com/tktcorporation/japanese-readability-benchmark)
- CEFR-controlled generation evidence: reinforcement-tuned LLMs with constrained decoding that "enforces vocabulary and syntactic boundaries" improved CEFR-level classification accuracy by up to 12.3% at B2–C1 and reduced misalignment errors by 15.6% (Discover Artificial Intelligence, 2025). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00762-3)
- CEFR-controlled Arabic generation (arXiv 2606.21981, 2026): prompts that "explicitly name the CEFR level and add lexical limits achieved the closest match to reference profiles"; framework combines controlled prompting, automatic readability prediction, CEFR-aligned lexical constraints and syntactic-complexity analysis. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21981v1)
- CELL (CEFR-Aligned Language Model) pattern: system prompts state the target level, CEFR descriptors, and surface constraints (e.g., A1 Spanish: "very simple sentences, avoid subordinate clauses, present tense only, 3–6 words per sentence"). — [Emergent Mind topic](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/cefr-aligned-language-model-cell)
- Other level-control work: TSAR 2025 shared task on CEFR-level simplification ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tsar-1.10.pdf)); "Generating Educational Materials with Different Levels of Readability using LLMs" ([arXiv 2406.12787](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12787)); CEFR-annotated WordNet ([review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/cefr-annotated-wordnet-llm-based-proficiency-guided-semantic-database-for-language-learning)); a closed-loop control architecture for constraint satisfaction in LLM generation ([arXiv 2609.19710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19710)).
- Pedagogical prompting with outcome evidence: brevity and one-step reveal (Harvard, KQ1); teacher-designed hints instead of answers (PNAS, KQ1); LearnLM's system-level pedagogy attributes (KQ1); Study Mode's Socratic questioning, scaffolding and knowledge checks (KQ1).
- JLPT level descriptors (official) for anchoring level constraints. — [JLPT level summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html)
- Chinese analogue for curriculum-aligned generation: HSKBenchmark models L2 acquisition through curriculum tuning (arXiv 2511.15574, Nov 2025). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15574)

### Inferences
- A JLPT-constrained generator should (1) name the level and give explicit constraints (allowed grammar points, vocabulary list, max sentence length), (2) compute TMR-style coverage against the learner's known-word set and jReadability band, and (3) rewrite or regenerate when out of band — the closed-loop pattern the Arabic/ESL papers and the readability benchmark all converge on.
- jReadability's features (kango %, verbs %, sentence length) make it cheap to compute per turn with fugashi, so it can gate every tutor utterance, not just lesson texts.

### Gaps
- Numbers on how far frontier models miss JLPT targets (ControlError by level) were not retrievable from the beginner-friendly paper.
- No published Japanese *vocabulary profiler* with JLPT-level tagging comparable to English CEFR profilers was found in reachable sources; Hasebe & Lee's system reportedly includes word-level profiling but the paper could not be fetched.

## KQ6. Guardrails: verifying generated Japanese and keeping tutors from teaching wrong things

### Takeaway
Reachable evidence supports a layered guardrail: pedagogical guardrails in the prompt (hint-not-answer, corrective friction against sycophancy), deterministic linguistic verification of every Japanese string (morphological analysis, dictionary/reading confirmation, readability gating), and adversarial evaluation for capitulation; documented production details from Duolingo/Khan were not reachable.

### Cited Findings
- Guardrails change outcomes: the PNAS study's guardrailed "GPT Tutor" (teacher-designed hints, no direct answers) eliminated the harm seen with unguarded access. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)
- Sycophancy guardrails: tutors capitulate most under authority claims and face-saving requests; the authors call for sycophancy benchmarks as a safety requirement for LLM tutors. — [arXiv 2605.14604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14604)
- Reading verification tooling: kanji polyphony is measurable at scale with the Joyo Kanji Yomi benchmarks (13k+ annotated sentences with target-reading markup), giving a ready regression suite for reading-verification components. — [SB Intuitions](https://github.com/sbintuitions/Joyo-Kanji-Yomi-Benchmark); [Parakeet](https://github.com/Parakeet-Inc/Joyo-Kanji-Yomi-Benchmark-Parakeet-Edition)
- Morphological analysis stack used by readability tools: fugashi (MeCab) + UniDic. — [jreadability README](https://github.com/joshdavham/jreadability)
- Style/linting for Japanese output: textlint violation density is used as an automated quality metric, with "textlint fixes" as an intervention layer. — [japanese-readability-benchmark](https://github.com/tktcorporation/japanese-readability-benchmark)
- Frequency resource for word-level checks: BCCWJ SUW/LUW combined frequency dictionary (Yomitan format) used by PitchBench for Zipf sampling. — [Kuuuube/yomitan-dictionaries](https://github.com/Kuuuube/yomitan-dictionaries)
- LLM-GEC over-correction mitigations (minimal-edit tuning, edit-level majority voting, larger context batching) — see KQ4 citations. — [arXiv 2609.10810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10810)
- LLM strengths/weaknesses to gate on: closed-class grammar reliable, open-class semantic choices unreliable (AIED 2026). — [arXiv 2605.07635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07635)

### Inferences
- Every Japanese token the tutor emits (example sentences, readings, glosses) should pass: tokenizer/dictionary lookup (word exists, reading matches UniDic/JMdict for that surface+POS), level check (JLPT list / jReadability), and, for readings of polyphonic kanji, an explicit disambiguation step; anything failing is regenerated or shown without the unverified field.
- Grammar explanations should be grounded in a curated grammar-point knowledge base (retrieval) rather than free generation, since the sycophancy and hallucination literature shows unconstrained explanations bend under pressure.
- Adversarial "insistence" tests (learner asserts a wrong particle/reading with authority) belong in the CI suite.

### Gaps
- Khan Academy and Duolingo engineering posts on guardrails/verification were unreachable; no primary description of how production tutors validate generated target-language text was obtained.
- No study quantifying how often frontier models produce wrong Japanese readings/glosses in tutoring contexts was found.

## KQ7. Evaluation: measuring tutor quality offline and online

### Takeaway
Offline evaluation in 2025–2026 is rubric-driven with validated LLM judges (TutorBench: Claude-4-Sonnet judge, 0.78 agreement/0.82 F1 with experts; L2-Bench: 1,000+ expert-validated L2-education tasks, 12 competencies; BEA 2025 MRBench dimensions), complemented by expert role-play comparisons (LearnLM); online evaluation that survives scrutiny uses randomized crossover or field RCTs with unassisted delayed outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- TutorBench: 1,490 samples; per-sample expert rubrics that are "self-contained, mutually exclusive, and verifiable", graded pass/fail by an LLM judge; Claude-4-Sonnet judge agreement with human experts 0.78 (F1 0.82). — [arXiv 2510.02663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02663); [Scale blog](https://scale.com/blog/tutorbench)
- BEA 2025 shared task dimensions for evaluating a tutor turn after a student mistake: mistake identification, mistake location, providing guidance, actionability (plus tutor identification); best macro-F1 58.34–71.81 on 3-class tasks. — [arXiv 2507.10579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10579); example systems: [MSA disagreement-aware tuning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18549), [BD MPNet ensembles](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01817)
- L2-Bench (Oxford University Press + University of Oxford; arXiv 2607.08842, 2026): 1,000+ expert-reviewed task–response pairs, taxonomy of 12 core / 31 sub-competencies, optimized LLM-as-judge pipeline; measures application of learning-experience-design principles rather than knowledge of them; currently UK/US L2 English only; best large model Claude Opus 4.7 at 85.5% overall. Companion methodology paper: "Towards an Evaluation Methodology for AI in Second Language Education" (arXiv 2603.20088). — [arXiv 2607.08842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08842); [OUP benchmark site](https://benchmarks.elt.edu.oup.com/); [OUP blog](https://benchmarks.elt.edu.oup.com/blog/best-llm-for-language-education); [arXiv 2603.20088](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20088)
- LearnLM protocol: raters role-play scripted learners against paired tutors given the same grounding material and system instructions; pedagogy experts rate each conversation and compare pairs on a −3..+3 scale. — [arXiv 2412.16429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429)
- Feedback-quality rubric work: "Consistently Good vs. Occasionally Great: A Rubric for Open-Ended Feedback Quality from Humans and Machines" (arXiv 2608.21850, Aug 2026). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21850)
- Multimodal/other tutoring benchmarks: MMTutorBench (math, arXiv 2510.23477); CSTutorBench (small models for block-based programming, arXiv 2607.05571); AfriLangTutor (low-resource language tutoring, arXiv 2604.20996). — [MMTutorBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23477); [CSTutorBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05571); [AfriLangTutor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20996)
- Sycophancy benchmark for tutors (EduFrameTrap) with human-labelled judge outputs (fields: tutor_model, pressure_mode, domain, confidence, judge_a/judge_b, human_label). — [repo](https://github.com/KasneciLab/eduframetrap-icml2026)
- Online designs with credible outcomes: Harvard crossover RCT (194 students, identical content both arms) — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); PNAS field RCT with an unassisted follow-up exam as the key outcome — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); Duolingo's published efficacy framework for app studies — [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-efficacy-research-framework)

### Inferences
- A Japanese tutor eval stack can copy the structure of TutorBench/L2-Bench: a bank of learner-turn scenarios (particle error, misreading, keigo misuse, sense confusion) each with expert-written, verifiable pass/fail rubrics (identified the error? located it? gave a hint not the answer? Japanese in reply is correct and within level?), scored by an LLM judge calibrated against human labels, plus a sycophancy/pressure sub-suite.
- Online, use delayed unassisted production tasks as the primary metric, with in-session accuracy reported only as a secondary (PNAS shows it can be inversely related to learning).

### Gaps
- No Japanese-language tutoring benchmark exists in the reachable sources (L2-Bench is English-only; MRBench/TutorBench are math/STEM).
- Per-model L2-Bench sub-competency results and TutorBench per-model tables could not be fetched.
