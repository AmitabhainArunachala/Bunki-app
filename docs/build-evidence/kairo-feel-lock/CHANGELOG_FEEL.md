# What you will feel — the feel-lock changelog

One entry per work package, in plain language. No jargon; the numbers live
in each WP's evidence folder.

**WP1 — Words no longer tear apart in the reader.**
Words like 含め and 認め used to split vertically — kanji on one line,
ending stacked underneath. Every word now sits whole on its line, and the
text no longer twitches when you tap a word (the glyph stays put to the
sub-pixel). English still appears under a word on the second tap.

**WP2 — The field composes itself, and the hint is readable everywhere.**
Overlapping words in the Drift settle their disputes: the louder word wins
the water, the quieter one recedes to a visible ghost — still there, still
tappable (a tap brings it forward). Pinch, double-tap-to-recentre, and
rotation all behave: surfacing mid-pinch never zooms the camera, double-tap
on open water glides you home (zoom and rotation included), and the field
can't be twisted past a half-turn. The hint pill is readable in every theme
(it was nearly invisible in 夜). The 岩絵具 and 緑青 palettes were deepened
so words carry the same presence there as in the light themes. Locking a
second word folds the first one's opened reveal.

**WP3 — The Drift and the dictionary are one app now.**
Tap a word in the field, open its card, and the card's study door opens the
corridor's own full entry — meanings, kanji doors, related-word chains —
right there. Walk the chain as deep as you like; when you come back, your
constellation is exactly where you left it. No word card dead-ends anymore.

**WP4 — English search finds the word you meant.**
Typing "world" now brings up 世界 first (it used to be buried under
single-kanji entries). "eat", "water", "school", "tomorrow" all land on the
word you'd expect. Kana and romaji search behave exactly as before — proven
byte-identical.

**WP5 — The app remembers where you were.**
Coming back to the shelf lands you exactly where you'd scrolled, not at the
top. The reader's hint now tells the truth for your furigana setting (with
readings always on, it says tap = English). Kanji part lists never say
"unnamed part" — they say what a part is honestly ("component — used in 76
kanji"). On a desktop screen the text sits in a comfortable centered column
instead of stretching across the window.

**WP6 — Two feel choices are yours to make (nothing pre-decided).**
The variants panel grew two rows. Ladder: keep the current three-tap ladder
(reading → English → entry), or try a four-tap one that starts quieter (word
alone → reading → English → entry). Satellite tap: keep the staged reveal,
or make a tapped satellite instantly become the new centre. All four
combinations work; the build takes no position — that ruling is yours.

**WP7 — The field is alive.**
The Drift used to be near-static. Now the whole surface leans together — a
slow current with a wandering eye, each word keeping its own small breathing
on top. Touch the water and everything eases to stillness under your finger;
lift, and it breathes again. If your device asks for reduced motion, the
field holds perfectly still (it actually didn't before — that's fixed).

**WP8 — Stroke order is a room, not a thumbnail.**
From any kanji page, the stroke diagram opens a full-screen page where the
strokes draw themselves like handwriting, in your theme's ink on your
theme's paper — replay, per-stroke counter, stroke numbers on demand. The
448 kanji without stroke data get an honest "not yet available" room with
the glyph large, never a broken page. Reduced motion gets a step-through
instead of animation. Back returns you to the kanji page exactly where you
were.

**WP9 — The library grew, and the deep water actually circulates.**
The shelf went from 26 to 40 articles (the 14 new ones are original graded
readers and essays — the wikinews/Aozora mirrors weren't reachable from the
build environment, so nothing was faked; every furigana in the new articles
was audited and 17 mis-readings repaired). And a real find: most of the
advanced vocabulary could never surface in the Drift — at N1, three quarters
of the words were permanently out of reach. Now panning slowly turns a
hidden wheel through the whole tier: everything surfaces eventually, and a
still field stays perfectly still.
