## 1. The goal in very plain language

Bunki should remember the Japanese the learner actually meets, help the learner
understand it, let the learner choose what is worth learning, turn that material
into rich practice, schedule only the material the learner chose, bring it back
in different contexts, and recommend the best real Japanese to encounter next.

It must feel like one app and one memory—not a dictionary, flashcard app,
reader, kanji app, and chatbot that happen to sit beside each other.

A learner must be able to:

1. start from zero, from N1+, or from any point between;
2. look up a word, reading, kanji, grammar point, sentence, or passage;
3. read articles in a calm Todai-like reader;
4. save YouTube videos, podcasts, screenshots, books, PDFs, conversations, and
   recent lookups for later;
5. ask an AI teacher questions and have bounded side journeys that return to the
   original material;
6. explicitly choose what enters scheduled study;
7. receive full-sentence, multi-sentence, dense-reading, dialogue, listening,
   production, contrast, cloze, reconstruction, and connected kanji practice;
8. meet important material again in new sources and new generated contexts;
9. see honest source, rights, generation, correction, and learning history; and
10. use it as a reliable daily iPhone learning environment.

The indivisible learning metabolism is:

> encounter → capture → understand → nominate → confirm → weave → retrieve →
> reintroduce → update → recommend

Every capability in this document is minimum product scope. It may be delivered
in safe increments, but it may not be reclassified as a distant optional
feature merely to make the build appear complete.

## 6. Product invariants

These are executable assertions, not slogans.

### 6.1 One state

- One stable identity follows an encounter through source, lookup, explanation,
  learner decision, generated practice, retrieval, later context, restart,
  chosen continuity mode, export, and restore.
- `@bunki/domain` is the sole authority for learner decisions, evidence,
  retrieval contracts, and scheduling semantics.
- There is one authoritative event history, one projection model, one identity
  system, and one scheduler.
- A feature may add an adapter or projection. It may not create a private
  learning database or hidden mastery score.

### 6.2 No accidental debt

- Twenty captures and zero promotions create exactly zero new retrieval
  contracts.
- Promoting exactly three of those captures creates contracts for exactly those
  three and no others.
- Browsing, reading, listening, looking up, chatting, importing, or receiving an
  AI suggestion never silently creates scheduled work.

### 6.3 AI is a candidate maker, not an authority

- AI may explain, propose, transform, recommend, transcribe, align, give
  feedback, or nominate.
- AI cannot directly mutate canonical dictionary facts, source truth, learner
  decisions, FSRS state, or a claim of mastery.
- Cancelled, abandoned, timed-out, superseded, or unmounted AI work attaches
  zero candidates.
- Every attached AI artifact records provider/model, prompt/template version,
  input lineage, creation time, and correction/invalidation state.

### 6.4 Evidence stays honest

- Passive exposure is not recall.
- Reading recognition is not listening recognition.
- Recognition is not production.
- Knowing one sense or register is not knowing every sense or register.
- Speech fluency alone is not a graded SRS event.
- Generated material is visibly distinguishable from authentic source
  material.
- A source-derived item returns to an exact honest source position or explicitly
  says that an exact position is unavailable.

### 6.5 Recovery is real

- “Saved” means durably recoverable, not merely rendered on screen.
- A failed write distinguishes reject-before-commit from commit-then-reject.
- Reconciliation is idempotent and creates no duplicate, reorder, false repair,
  or lost capture.
- Replay, export, chosen continuity mode, restore, and cold reopen reproduce the
  same projected state.
- Deletion remains deleted after replay and restore.

## 7. Canonical concepts

Implement the smallest versioned contracts that make the full loop possible.

### Source

An authentic or user-supplied origin: article, feed item, URL, video, podcast,
transcript, photo, PDF/book excerpt, audio file, conversation, lookup, manual
text, imported deck, or generated artifact. It records type, rights state,
privacy class, acquisition method, stable external identity when available,
and honest return capability.

### Source anchor

The best return location available: character span, paragraph, page and region,
transcript segment and time range, message turn, lookup query, media offset, or
pointer-only URL. Approximate anchors are labeled approximate.

### Encounter

A learner’s contact with Japanese in a source. Capture makes the encounter
durable. It does not schedule it.

### Learning thread

The stable cross-surface identity that connects an encounter with dictionary
senses, readings, kanji, grammar, questions, learner decisions, artifacts,
retrieval contracts, evidence, and later contexts.

### Candidate

A proposed explanation, target, correction, card, passage, dialogue, listening
experience, kanji task, or recommendation. Candidates have no scheduling
authority.

### Learner decision

A versioned, reversible event describing what the learner chose and, when
needed, exactly which sense, reading, grammar use, span, modality, or skill the
choice covers.

### Retrieval contract

A learner-confirmed, versioned definition of what will be retrieved: target,
prompt form, expected evidence, modality, rubric or answer, source lineage,
scheduler version, and active/suspended/deleted state.

### Retrieval evidence

A graded attempt tied to one retrieval contract. It records the task version,
response or privacy-safe receipt, grade source, timing, hints, interruption,
and relevant modality. Only valid retrieval evidence can update memory.

### Learner frontier

The explainable, multidimensional boundary between known, fragile, and unknown
material. It is represented by sense, register, reading, grammar function,
kanji skill, modality, and goal—not one JLPT scalar.

### Generated artifact

A versioned personalized learning experience whose relevant spans retain source
or motivating-thread lineage and generated/authentic labels.

## 8. Exact learner-action semantics

The UI wording may be refined, but these state transitions may not drift.

### Capture

Make an encounter durable in the inbox with its source and anchor. Create no
retrieval contract.

### Keep

Keep the encounter searchable, linked, and eligible for future contextual
suggestion. Create no retrieval contract.

### Learn

Open or accept an editable candidate, make the target and modality explicit,
and create a versioned retrieval contract only after confirmation.

### Study deeply

Request higher-order and/or multi-modal practice such as production, listening,
writing, nuance, contrasts, or kanji depth. This is not a system declaration
that the learner has mastered anything.

If the existing label **Master** is temporarily retained, the interface must
explain “study this deeply,” and stored semantics must use the same non-mastery
meaning.

### Ignore

Reversibly suppress proactive resurfacing or recommendation in the chosen scope.
Do not delete source history and do not manufacture negative learning evidence.

### Edit / reject

Edit or reject a candidate before promotion. Rejection does not rewrite the
authentic source.

### Suspend / bury

Suspend stops future scheduling until resumed. Bury defers an item within a
finite session without changing memory.

### Undo

Append a compensating event. Never erase history in place.

### Delete

Create a tombstone, remove private payloads according to policy, prevent
resurrection during replay/continuity/restore, and retain only the minimum
auditable receipt allowed by the privacy contract.

## 9. Learner model and SRS contract

### 9.1 Knowledge granularity

Track relevant dimensions separately:

- lexical written form;
- reading;
- individual sense;
- register and collocation;
- grammar recognition and production;
- kanji recognition, reading-in-word, component/phonetic structure, writing,
  and orthographic discrimination;
- reading recognition;
- listening recognition;
- cued recall;
- free production;
- pronunciation or speech task when explicitly graded; and
- handwriting when explicitly graded.

A thread may be strong in one dimension and fragile in another.

### 9.2 Evidence rules

- Deterministic objective tasks may grade automatically when the accepted answer
  space is versioned and inspectable.
- Atomic self-assessed reviews use clear answer reveal and grade meanings.
- Dense reading, listening, free production, speech, and handwriting use a
  versioned rubric.
- AI may propose feedback or a grade with uncertainty. Until an independently
  validated grading policy exists, the learner confirms or overrides any
  memory-changing AI grade.
- Hints, answer reveals, interruption, and partial completion are recorded.
- Contradictory evidence changes only the relevant dimensions.
- Learner correction always has an explicit route and is never silently undone
  by later AI inference.

### 9.3 Scheduling

- Pin the FSRS implementation and parameters.
- Scheduling consumes only valid retrieval evidence for active contracts.
- Clock movement, timezone boundaries, offline use, replay, undo, migration,
  and long histories are deterministic and tested.
- Sessions are finite and show a clear end.
- Workload controls include a daily ceiling, new/review balance, due-order
  explanation, pause, suspend, bury, undo, and leech handling.
- No recommendation engine or AI model writes directly to the queue.

### 9.4 Frontier

Recommendations and generation consume:

- confirmed retrieval evidence;
- recent source exposure, clearly labeled as exposure;
- learner goals and interests;
- accepted, dismissed, and corrected recommendations;
- desired support level;
- modality strengths and weaknesses; and
- current workload.

They must explain why something is near the learner’s useful `n+1` edge.

## 10. Learner profiles

All profiles use the same loop and state model.

### Absolute beginner

- Kana and phonology entry.
- Optional restrained romaji that fades out.
- Slow and clear audio.
- Foundational vocabulary and grammar.
- Highly comprehensible support without hiding authentic Japanese.
- A first successful sentence encounter, understanding, promotion, retrieval,
  and reintroduction.

### N5–N4

- Furigana and audio support.
- Common inflections and grammar.
- Short authentic sources.
- Guided sentence production.
- Gradual removal of support.

### N3–N2

- Longer and denser sources.
- Natural listening with optional support.
- Register, collocation, and contrast work.
- More production and cross-source weaving.

### N1+

- Direct advanced entry with no beginner gate.
- Japanese-language definitions where rights-clear data is available.
- Formal, literary, technical, and colloquial nuance.
- Rare compounds, dense prose, and natural-speed listening.

### Kanken-depth overlay

Kanken-style depth is orthogonal to conversational proficiency. It includes
rights-clear work on uncommon readings and forms, orthographic discrimination,
handwriting/production, components and phonetic series, compounds, lexical
relations, and yojijukugo. Do not use protected official test content or imply
official Kanken affiliation without permission.

## 11. Product capabilities

### 11.1 Production dictionary

The dictionary must work offline and include:

- reproducible, pinned production releases rather than demo subsets;
- written-form, reading, inflection, English, and pasted-text search;
- senses, parts of speech, restrictions, registers, attributed glosses, and
  source attribution;
- conjugation and inflection help;
- recursive word ↔ sense ↔ reading ↔ kanji ↔ component ↔ compound links;
- contextual examples where rights allow;
- deterministic content-pack builds, exact record reconciliation, migration,
  and update receipts; and
- production-device performance proof.

Recommended initial candidates are official JMdict/KANJIDIC2 releases and a
pinned KanjiVG release, but the exact versions and distribution obligations must
pass the content-license gate before production import. An old ~3,000-entry
branch is parser donor material, not completeness proof.

### 11.2 Grammar

Provide a rights-clear beginner-through-advanced inventory with:

- stable identities;
- form, meaning, constraints, register, and contrast;
- recognition and production tasks;
- contextual authentic or properly licensed examples;
- clear coverage accounting and expandable gaps; and
- canonical sourced facts kept distinct from labeled AI explanation.

Do not copy proprietary grammar explanations or examples.

### 11.3 Deep kanji

Provide production-scale:

- readings connected to real words;
- components, radicals, semantic roles, and phonetic series;
- stroke order and writing practice where rights allow;
- compounds, contrasts, frequency/goal mappings, rare forms, and orthographic
  discrimination; and
- a route back to authentic source sentences.

Kanji practice must remain fused with vocabulary, grammar, sentences, and
sources while still allowing separate handwriting or discrimination contracts.

### 11.4 Living SRS and sentence mining

The review system must support:

- explicit promotion from encounter, lookup, article, transcript, conversation,
  or import;
- full-sentence and multi-sentence contracts;
- independent modality contracts when useful;
- finite mixed sessions;
- transparent grading and scheduling;
- sentence mining without silent debt;
- edit, reject, undo, suspend, bury, and delete; and
- replay/export/restore equality.

### 11.5 Article reader

Build a calm Todai-like reader for manual, user-owned, open, licensed, and
properly fetched text:

- Japanese typography and optional furigana;
- tap lookup and recursive reference;
- contextual grammar and teacher help;
- personal-frontier marking that remains an explanation rather than a false
  global level;
- annotations and selected-span capture;
- exact reading position and source return;
- lawful offline availability;
- restart recovery; and
- lawful audio synchronization, speed control, seeking, replay, and capture from
  the exact text/audio position when available.

Any promoted word, kanji, grammar point, sentence, or passage remains connected
to the original article position.

### 11.6 Source inbox and the five-video walk

While walking, the learner can save at least five YouTube or podcast sources
with one hand and process them later.

For each source preserve:

- URL or stable pointer;
- title, publisher/channel, and date when available;
- the best honest progress anchor;
- official deep-link/player return;
- transcript availability and rights state;
- user notes and selected spans;
- interruption and retry state; and
- later extraction, lookup, promotion, weaving, and review history.

If an official player does not expose reliable time, record an approximate or
unknown position. Never fabricate it.

### 11.7 Transcript and media ingestion

Build a provider-neutral Source Router. Initial allowed routes are:

- manual SRT/VTT;
- user-owned or user-authorized transcript;
- publisher-supplied transcript;
- Podcasting 2.0 or similarly compliant feed transcript;
- licensed/open adapter;
- manual pasted excerpt;
- pointer-only source when content cannot lawfully be ingested; and
- local audio where the learner has the right to process it.

Every route has a machine-readable policy:

`FULL`, `FEED_SUPPLIED`, `USER_SELECTED_EXCERPT`, `POINTER_ONLY`, or `BLOCKED`.

Include payload ceilings, redirect and SSRF defenses, privacy class, kill
switch, acquisition receipt, source version, and failure behavior. A
third-party scraper or MCP is an adapter behind this boundary, never a shortcut
around it. Before an external adapter processes a real learner source, check in
an admission receipt covering terms, license, authorization/consent, data sent,
retention/deletion, availability and rate limits, failure behavior, transcript
provenance, time-anchor fidelity, official-source return, kill switch, and
operator approval when required.

### 11.8 Screenshots, OCR, books, and PDFs

Support:

- screenshot/photo OCR;
- page/region anchoring;
- user correction of extracted Japanese;
- extraction method and confidence;
- user-owned PDF/book excerpt intake;
- local/private-by-default original bytes;
- selected-span rights state; and
- return to the original image region or page.

OCR output is a candidate until corrected or confirmed.

### 11.9 Recursive AI teacher

Text and voice teacher modes use the same learning threads. The teacher must:

- see only the approved minimum context;
- answer questions about a word, kanji, grammar point, sentence, source, or
  learner difficulty;
- offer bounded two- or three-way side journeys;
- return clearly to the original question;
- distinguish sourced facts, inference, and generation;
- nominate useful turns or recent lookups;
- let the learner edit and confirm before scheduling;
- stream when useful, cancel cleanly, degrade honestly offline, and never block
  capture; and
- respect privacy, cost, retention, and secret boundaries.

Provider credentials never enter the app bundle. A minimal secret-holding
gateway may call approved providers but cannot write learner state.

### 11.10 Voice and audio

Use provider-neutral interfaces for speech recognition, speech synthesis,
pronunciation feedback, and lawful source audio.

Before a real provider is enabled, freeze:

- consent classes for text and audio;
- what leaves the device;
- retention and deletion;
- latency and timeout behavior;
- recurring budget cap;
- interruption and cancellation;
- offline degradation;
- provider/model/version receipts; and
- the exact evidence rule for any memory-changing grade.

Voice conversation must update the same thread as text conversation. A
transcript or AI pronunciation opinion does not silently become recall evidence.

### 11.11 Personalized weave

The weave pipeline is inspectable:

1. retrieve approved source and learner context;
2. identify known, fragile, and unknown material;
3. propose a small target shortlist near `n+1`;
4. collect learner decisions;
5. generate one or more requested forms;
6. validate difficulty, duplication, facts, provenance, and rights;
7. allow edit, regenerate, reject, or accept;
8. create retrieval contracts only for accepted targets; and
9. later reintroduce the material in genuinely different contexts.

Required real forms are:

- source-anchored full sentence;
- multi-sentence card, including a real 10–15-sentence form;
- dense multi-paragraph reading;
- dialogue;
- one- to two-minute listening experience;
- cloze;
- sentence reconstruction;
- contrast;
- active production;
- separate but connected kanji recognition, discrimination, or writing; and
- conversational reuse.

These cannot be the same template with different headings. Relevant spans
retain motivating threads and multi-source lineage.

### 11.12 Reintroduction and cross-pollination

Important material should return in at least three genuinely different
contexts/styles and, when useful, at least two modalities. The system must
distinguish:

- authentic encounter;
- generated encounter;
- passive exposure;
- prompted practice; and
- valid graded retrieval.

Corrections to a source fact, dictionary release, transcript, or learner
decision invalidate or regenerate affected candidates without rewriting
history.

### 11.13 Primary-source recommendations

Recommend lawful, high-quality Japanese sources using:

- learner frontier;
- interests and goals;
- modality;
- desired support;
- vocabulary/grammar density;
- register;
- prior source progress;
- accepted, dismissed, and corrected recommendations;
- freshness and quality; and
- current workload.

The learner sees a short plain explanation of why each source fits now.
Absolute beginner, intermediate, and N1+ clean profiles must receive materially
different recommendations and complete the same encounter-to-reintroduction
loop.

### 11.14 Anki warm start

Support a transparent import path:

1. TSV/CSV notes and tags;
2. collection/note/card/model mapping;
3. media references;
4. scheduling history as inspectable evidence, never unquestionable truth; and
5. later schema migrations without silent loss.

Every import has a receipt, source hash, counts, ambiguous mappings, corrections,
deduplication decisions, and rollback. Imported material joins normal Bunki
threads and contracts.

### 11.15 Map/Garden/Observatory

## 14. Frozen minimum performance and reliability budgets

Preserve any stricter frozen requirement. At minimum:

- local save acknowledgement p95 is at most 150 ms;
- warm local lookup p95 is at most 200 ms;
- share-to-durable acknowledgement median is at most 2 seconds and p95 at most
  4 seconds, or no worse than the current dictionary flow by more than 20%;
- zero captures are lost across 100 background, force-quit, and share trials;
- five ordinary captures feel no slower than the current dictionary flow in
  operator judgment;
- AI timeout or unavailability never delays durable capture;
- ordinary production dictionary lookup remains within budget on the oldest
  supported physical iPhone; and
- long-history replay has a measured bound and does not grow quadratically.

At native admission, add a checked-in benchmark protocol for the oldest
supported iPhone, including cold start, content-pack storage, 30-minute
reader/listener energy use, memory pressure, scrolling, and background idle.
Freeze the measured release budgets before whole-product hardening. A missed
budget is a defect or an explicit operator-reviewed tradeoff, not an omitted
measurement.

## 17. Continuous execution loop

### 17.1 Cold-start and collision admission

On every fresh launch, resume, worktree change, or context compaction:

1. reread applicable `AGENTS.md`, the authority documents in Section 3, and the
   current child contract;
2. fetch and resolve the live default branch and exact `main` SHA;
3. inspect repository status, worktrees, branches, stashes, open PRs, review
   threads, unpublished commits, and local untracked files;
4. preserve all unexplained user or agent work;
5. adopt an existing matching Bunki goal branch/PR rather than creating a
   duplicate;
6. detect path/ownership collisions before assigning a writer; and
7. use an isolated worktree for the active child.

Never reset, overwrite, clean, rebase away, or delete unexplained work. If it
cannot be preserved and isolated safely, stop with an exact collision report.

### 17.2 READY predicate

A child is `READY` only when:

- every declared dependency is human-merged and reverified on the resulting
  `main`;
- every required human gate has a recorded ADR/receipt;
- no branch, PR, worktree, or path owner collision exists;
- the allowed paths, success/failure tests, rollback, non-goals, and learner
  outcome form one reversible child contract; and
- the child can be verified without relying on an unmerged sibling.

### 17.3 Iteration

For every node in the dependency graph:

1. Refresh repository, live `main`, open PRs, checks, review threads, and exact
   dependency state.
2. Select the smallest ready child PRlet of a ready milestone on the critical
   path; decompose the milestone first if needed.
3. Write an exact child task contract: base SHA, allowed paths, lock clauses,
   acceptance journey, tests, failure paths, non-goals, and rollback.
4. Assign read-only research/falsification in parallel where useful.
5. Allow one Integrator to write one branch from exact merged `main`.
6. Commit the child contract and initial ledger state, push the branch, and open
   the one draft PR before substantial implementation. Thereafter commit and
   push a coherent resumable checkpoint at least every 90 minutes of active
   work. Unpushed local state is not a checkpoint.
7. Reuse donor code only after line-level review.
8. Run focused tests, full current tests, frozen integrity checks, migration,
   replay, export/restore, security, and relevant native proof.
9. Freeze a candidate head and dispatch independent exact-head code/security
   and UX/native falsifiers. They
   inspect before reading the builder’s claims.
10. Address all actionable findings; any source change creates a new head and
    requires the relevant exact-head ladder again.
11. When checks and reviews are green, present John one plain merge request with
    learner-visible outcome, exact head, proof, and remaining risk.
12. Wait for human merge. Read-only planning may continue; a second write branch
    may not.
13. After merge, refresh `main`, record closure in the live overlay, carry the
    merge receipt into the next checked-in ledger snapshot, and automatically
    start the next ready child.

Do not stop merely because one PRlet is complete. Do not continue writing on an
unmerged dependency.

## 19. Binding tests and extended end-to-end journeys

The Product Lock’s Tests 1–10 are incorporated by reference, verbatim in
authority, and are not replaced or weakened by the summaries below. Q01 runs
Product Lock Tests 1–9. Product Lock Test 10 remains the operator’s post-build
week.

The additional `EJ` journeys close gaps such as zero-state curriculum, voice,
OCR/PDF, Anki, production-data update, and state continuity. Each must pass in a
production-configured native build with authentic or rights-clear data.
Fixtures remain useful for deterministic tests but cannot be the only proof.
If an `EJ` summary is less strict than a Product Lock test, the Product Lock
wins.

### EJ01 — Zero to first living sentence

A clean absolute beginner learns the minimum kana/phonology support, encounters
a comprehensible sentence, understands it, explicitly promotes a target,
retrieves it, meets it in a new context, and sees an appropriate next source.

### EJ02 — Advanced direct entry

A clean N1+/Kanken-depth learner skips beginner gating, completes a rare
reading/orthography/nuance task in the same shared loop, and receives a fitting
advanced source.

### EJ03 — Three-profile frontier recommendations

Using clean beginner, intermediate, and N1+ profiles, receive an explained
primary-source recommendation, start it, record partial or complete
consumption, capture something, accept/dismiss/correct the estimate, and receive
a materially changed follow-up recommendation that reflects that evidence.
The beginner receives comprehensible support without advanced clutter; the
intermediate is not treated as an average; the N1+ learner has immediate dense,
natural, and nuanced options. A Kanken-depth goal changes orthographic/kanji
recommendations without pretending it is one higher JLPT scalar.

### EJ04 — Capture without debt

Capture 20 mixed encounters offline. Promote none: zero new contracts. Promote
exactly three: exactly those three acquire versioned contracts. Force quit,
reopen, export, and restore preserve the result.

### EJ05 — Recursive dictionary and kanji walk

From one real word, walk through sense, reading, kanji, component/phonetic
series, compound, contrast, grammar, example, source return, promotion,
retrieval, and later context in about ten minutes without losing the thread.

### EJ06 — Article loop

Open a fresh allowed article, preserve reading position, tap a lookup, ask a
contextual question, capture and promote material, retrieve it later, use it in
conversation, force quit, and return to the exact article span.

### EJ07 — Five-video walking loop

While walking, save five YouTube/podcast sources with one hand. Later obtain
only lawful transcripts, preserve unavailable/pointer-only truth, inspect
segments and honest time anchors, select unknown vocabulary/kanji/grammar,
weave confirmed material, practice it, and return to the official source.

The standard five-source acceptance set must include at least two distinct
sources with documented permitted transcripts and exact segment/time anchors,
and at least one unavailable or pointer-only source. Material from the two
permitted sources must cross-pollinate into an inspectable weave and return to
both correct official source positions. Blocked/pointer-only honesty is tested
separately and cannot stand in for real extraction and study proof.

### EJ08 — AI text conversation and recent lookups

Have a recursive conversation, follow a bounded side path, return to the topic,
nominate a useful turn and recent lookup, edit them, confirm only selected
targets, and verify that unconfirmed material creates no review debt.

### EJ09 — Voice conversation

Speak with the same teacher, inspect/correct the transcript, nominate material,
confirm it, and see the same thread in text, SRS, and source views. Cancellation
or provider failure attaches nothing and loses no capture.

### EJ10 — Screenshot/photo

Capture a screenshot or photo, correct OCR, select a region, understand and
promote material, weave it, retrieve it, and return to the original region
after restart.

### EJ11 — PDF/book

Select a lawful passage from a user-owned PDF/book, preserve page and region,
complete study and reintroduction, and return to the page after restore.

### EJ12 — Multi-source weave

Use at least an article, transcript, conversation, and lookup. Exercise every
required practice form. Verify span-level lineage, genuine contextual
variation, separate modality evidence, and learner-only promotion.

### EJ13 — Anki warm start

Import representative and adversarial decks, inspect ambiguous mappings,
correct them, review current material, migrate schema, export, clean-install
restore, and reconcile counts with zero silent loss.

### EJ14 — Production data update

Build a newer pinned dictionary/content release. Preserve stable identities and
learner history, account for every dropped or changed record, invalidate
affected generated facts, roll back safely, and reproduce the output hash.

### EJ15 — Offline continuity, conflict safety, and recovery

Capture and review offline on iPhone, force quit, reconnect or transfer, inspect
the authorized web/device view, delete an item, and restore on a new install
without resurrection or split state. In Mode A, create and deterministically
resolve a controlled concurrent edit. In Mode B, prove that the web snapshot is
read-only, stale state is labeled, concurrent writes are prevented, and an
explicit encrypted refresh reproduces iPhone authority.

### EJ16 — Seven-day native life

John uses the exact signed candidate on his physical iPhone for seven
consecutive days across lookup, reading/listening, capture, teacher, practice,
and interruption. He voluntarily returns on day eight.

## 20. Capability proof matrix

Proof is multidimensional. Do not compress it into a linear maturity label that
lets one kind of evidence hide another.

Every capability ledger row records:

| Dimension          | Allowed values                                                                         |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Implementation     | `ABSENT`, `CONTRACT_ONLY`, `FIXTURE_ONLY`, `WIRED`                                     |
| Canonical data     | `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `FIXTURE`, `PINNED_RELEASE`                                          |

`NOT_APPLICABLE` requires a written reason; it is not an escape hatch.

acceptance** only when it is `WIRED`, uses a `PINNED_RELEASE` wherever

`AUTHENTIC_RIGHTS_CLEAR_PROVED` input wherever the journey consumes real

- **Adversarial verifier:** fresh context, read-only, exact candidate SHA, tries
  to falsify completion.

With limited concurrency, combine compatible read-only roles. Never combine
Integrator and adversarial approval for the same PR.

### Enforceable ownership

The live ledger contains one assignment table with agent identity, role,
read/write mode, exact base SHA, allowed paths, child ID, and state.

- Exactly one assignment may be write-capable: the Integrator for the active
  child.
- Every other agent defaults to read-only.
- A read-only agent may spawn only read-only bounded subtasks; it may not create
  another writer or widen path ownership.
- One path has one owner during a child. Cross-path needs become explicit
  interface requests to the Conductor.
- Agent silence, timeout, crash, or missing report is not approval.
- Conflicting reviews are resolved by reproductions, tests, governing
  authority, and exact evidence—not majority vote or model prestige.

### Agent assignment

Every task states:

- exact base SHA;
- read/write permission and allowed paths;
- product clauses and journey advanced;
- dependencies;
- required success and failure tests;
- non-goals;
- rollback; and
- receipt format.

Every receipt states:

- exact SHA observed;
- files inspected or changed;
- decisions and assumptions;
- exact commands/results;
- physical device/provider/data used;
- capability proof dimensions earned;
- unresolved risks; and
- one plain sentence describing what is now better for the learner.

## 23. Evidence and resumability

The G00 bootstrap PR creates:

- `docs/goal/BUNKI_END_TO_END_GOAL_STATE.json` — machine-readable controller
  state;
- `docs/goal/BUNKI_END_TO_END_GOAL_LEDGER.md` — human-readable history and next
  action;
- `docs/goal/decisions/ADR-*.md` — versioned human decisions so later agents do
  not ask the same question again; and
- `docs/goal/BUNKI_OPERATOR_VISUAL_REFERENCES.json` — operator screenshots and
  visual constraints.

Initialize the visual-reference manifest as empty if no attachments are
available; never invent a screenshot requirement. When John supplies an image,
record its SHA-256, received date, local/Library identity, visible surface,
what it actually demonstrates, accepted preference or constraint, supersession
state, and the exact build/SHA if known. Do not infer off-screen behavior.
Relevant UX/native reviews compare against the manifest and name any deliberate
deviation.

The JSON state includes schema version, live main SHA, controller status,
active milestone/child, dependency state, active PR/base/head, last verified
head known at the time of the snapshot, capability changes, open human gate,
and next safe action.

Allowed controller states are:

`READY`, `ACTIVE`, `VERIFYING`, `AWAITING_HUMAN_MERGE`,
`PAUSED_HUMAN_GATE`, `BLOCKED`, `MERGED`, and `COMPLETE`.

`AWAITING_HUMAN_MERGE` and `PAUSED_HUMAN_GATE` are resumable pauses, never
completion.

Use two evidence layers:

1. **Checked-in snapshot.** Update the JSON and Markdown ledgers during active
   implementation, at controller start, at least every 90 minutes before
   candidate freeze, and as the final source change before exact-head
   verification.
2. **Post-freeze live overlay.** After candidate freeze, store CI, review,
   draft/ready, merge-request, and merge receipts in GitHub checks and the PR
   body/comments, each keyed to the exact immutable head SHA. These updates do
   not change repository bytes.

Never commit an evidence-only ledger update after exact-head verification merely
to record that verification; doing so would create a new unverified head.

The ledger records:

- live `main` SHA;
- active PR/base/head and draft state;
- current node and dependencies;
- capability proof-matrix changes;
- tests and device/provider/data receipts;
- review findings and resolutions;
- decisions still needed and first blocked node;
- next exact safe action; and
- how to resume without relying on chat memory.

The active draft-PR head carries the proposed checked-in ledger delta, and its
PR body mirrors the active child, exact head, gates, and resume instruction.
Freeze repository bytes before exact-head CI and independent review. Until
merge, live GitHub PR state plus the frozen branch copy are the authority for
that active delta; merged `main` remains authority for everything already
closed.

The Integrator owns draft-to-ready changes after both independent reviews and
exact-head CI pass; changing the GitHub draft/ready flag does not change the
head. The PR live overlay contains `merge_requested_for_head_sha`. A merge
request is emitted only when that value differs from the verified head.
Repeated controller runs do not send duplicate requests for the same SHA.

After human merge, the live overlay records the merge immediately. The next
canonical ledger snapshot—normally the first source change of the next child
PR—records the prior child as `MERGED` and names its merge SHA. If no next child
is opened because a human gate pauses the controller, live GitHub state remains
the closure authority until the next selected documentation/state PR. Do not
mutate the already verified or merged candidate solely to make its checked-in
status say `MERGED`.

Resume triggers are a new user turn, scheduled monitor, or fresh controller
run. On resume:

1. refresh live `main`, PR state, head SHA, checks, and review threads;
2. compare them with the machine ledger;
3. if merged, determine the merge method. For a merge commit, verify the exact
   reviewed head is the admitted parent and no extra change entered. For squash
   or rebase, verify patch/content equivalence between the exact reviewed
   main-to-head diff and the resulting main delta, including file set and
   governing hashes. Rerun the relevant focused and full gate on the merge SHA.
   Only then mark the child `MERGED` in the live overlay, carry it into the next
   checked-in snapshot, refresh dependencies, and select the next ready child;
4. if still open at the same head, remain paused without a duplicate request;
5. if the head changed, clear prior verification and reverify;
6. if closed unmerged, mark the child `BLOCKED` and propose re-admission; and
7. never assume an old PR was merged because chat context says so.

If execution stops or context is compacted, the next agent follows this resume
procedure rather than reconstructing state from memory.

### Failure classification and escalation

Classify every material failure as one of:

- **deterministic defect:** the code, schema, test, import, migration, or
  controller reproducibly violates its contract;
- **transient dependency:** an otherwise valid external service or transfer
  failed temporarily;
- **environment failure:** required toolchain, platform, device, permission, or
  resource is unavailable or inconsistent;
- **authority gate:** a human-only decision, credential, license, cost, merge,
  entitlement, or release is required; or
- **architectural contradiction:** two governing constraints cannot both be
  satisfied by the admitted design.

For an identical transient failure, retry at most twice with bounded backoff.
Do not use a long blocking sleep. Repetition after that limit triggers a fresh
read-only falsifier, a minimal reproduction, and an explicit
fix/split/revert/escalate decision.

For any deterministic or environment failure, three materially different
unsuccessful repair attempts—or recurrence across two changed candidate
heads—triggers a fresh falsifier, minimal reproduction, and explicit
fix/split/revert/escalate decision. Do not repeat the same repair loop
indefinitely. An architectural contradiction escalates immediately.

A block report names:

- exact main, branch, and candidate SHA;
- exact command or external action and error;
- attempts and outcomes;
- causes reproduced or ruled out;
- affected milestone, child, journey, and proof dimensions;
- smallest human action that would unblock it;
- safe work that can continue independently; and
- exact resume step.

Severity means:

- **P0:** credible security/privacy breach, secret exposure, data loss or
  corruption, unauthorized source processing, irrecoverable state divergence,
  or fabricated canonical/retrieval evidence.
- **P1:** a required learner journey is broken; deterministic replay,
  migration, export/restore, provenance, scheduling, native reliability, or
  accessibility materially fails; or the UI makes a serious false claim about
  safety, source, state, or learning.

P0/P1 findings cannot be voted away, relabeled, or waived into completion under
this controller. A gate cannot be cleared by weakening a test, swapping required
authentic proof for a fixture, marking applicable proof `NOT_APPLICABLE`, hiding
the failure, or shrinking locked scope.

Everything else within these boundaries is implementation judgment and should
not repeatedly stop the controller.

## 25. Mandatory stops

Stop writing and report plainly if:

- a human merge is required;
- the live base/head changed and invalidated proof;
- a needed license, source right, provider consent, credential, paid cost, or
  Apple entitlement lacks approval;
- requested work would process protected or private content outside the
  approved policy;
- the only path requires destructive history rewriting, data loss, secret
  exposure, or weakening a frozen invariant;
- two authoritative stores or schedulers cannot be reconciled safely;
- migration/export/restore cannot preserve existing learner state;
- P0/P1 security, privacy, data-loss, or source-truth defects remain;
- independent exact-SHA review is unavailable for a release claim; or
- an operator decision would materially change scope or shipped behavior.

A failed test, difficult implementation, incomplete fixture, or ordinary bug is
not by itself a reason to abandon the goal. Fix it within the active PR or
re-scope the PRlet without reducing the locked outcome.

only when:

6. a signed trial/TestFlight artifact was built from the exact verified `main`

## 28. Final plain-language test

The build is successful when a learner can encounter Japanese anywhere, save
it once, understand it deeply, choose what matters, practice it in rich and
connected forms, meet it again elsewhere, and become measurably stronger—
without losing the source, their data, their privacy, or control of what they
study.
