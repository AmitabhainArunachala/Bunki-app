## 0. The reframe

I split the brief (10-08 00:09) into its 25 phrases; §6 answers each one, in his order. One phrase says what all the others are for:

> "I want the user to fall in love with the depth and beauty of Japanese, with the recursive quality of the language and the culture."

The rest is what makes that love possible (depth, flow, speed, clarity, beauty) or what kills it (boredom, confusion, blandness, clutter, sameness). Here is how I read the whole.

**Two names, two motions.** The app has carried two names, and they are the two motions of the journey. 分岐 Bunki, a branch point, came from his wish for "bifurcating and trifurcating learning journeys for deep memorization and immersion" (07-27, T2). 回廊 KAIRO came on 08-07: "It is all one single APP and the APP name is 回廊 KAIRO" (T2), a temple's roofed corridor that circles the court and brings you back. Branch out, come back, at every size. A tap opens a word into its kanji and family and returns you to the sentence. A day goes out through the rooms and back to the door. A year branches into his fields and keeps returning to the same words, deeper each time. Japanese draws this itself: 回 began as a picture of water circling in a pool, and today it is written as a square inside a square. (Which name the app wears has been open since 08-07. That is his call.)

**The engine is the half-known word.** His founding sentence (07-27, T2): "It is always, I don't know this perfectly and I don't want to lose it." Bunki finds that word at the edge, keeps it, brings it back in new places until it is his, and lets him see that it is. That is my reading of "addivtive" (most likely addictive): the pull of the almost-known becoming known. Points are not the pull; on 08-05 he said "Gamified is not the right word" (T2).

**Beauty and learning are one object.** The sky at the door is beautiful, and it is his memory. The woodblock article is beautiful, and it sits at his level with a voice. The samurai is a joke, and it is the honest verdict. When the beautiful thing and the true thing are the same thing, study stops feeling like study. The tears the brief asks for come from that recognition.

**Layers, for everyone.** Message 5: "not just an intellignet adult, but layers of depth". The surface opens for anyone, his father at 74 included (08-19, T3), and each tap goes one layer down, as far as Kanken 1級.

## 1. The journey

### 1.1 Opening Bunki

He opens it on a platform with one bar of signal, and it is there in under a second (a target, unmeasured), because it lives on the phone. Words drift like ink on water around the torii and its single star, his pick (08-11, T3). This is the one screen he has defended: "the opening page is not the problem. that is cool and intuitive and perfeclty fine." (09-29). It stays.

What changes is what the sky is made of. On 07-27 he called Kanji Garden's knowledge wallpaper "the most visually cool thing in all these environments… But it is not even interactive at all." (T2). Bunki's sky is that wallpaper alive: every word from his record, the ones due today a shade brighter, missed answers drifting back "in the floating world first drfit page" (09-29), the word he saved last night floating near the gate. Under the star waits one line: the thread he left open, or "Today's walk · 15 min" (今日の稽古 · 15分 in 日本語 mode). One touch and he is walking. Every room is one touch from here and from anywhere: "THE DOJO, or study room, needs to be accessible from ANY LOCATION WHATSOEVER" (09-17).

### 1.2 The first three minutes

A stranger arrives from a shared link (someone's sky after a hundred days, a samurai clip, a woodblock page) and lands in the sky. No form and no placement test: for strangers his 09-23 pick was "infer" (board q8).

- **0:00** Words drift at every level. They touch 名人.
- **0:15** It opens: めいじん, master; 名 name, 人 person. "a light tap on one word will bring up at least ten" (08-05, T2): 有名, 名前, 達人, 職人 light up around it.
- **0:40** A three-sentence passage at their level, judged from what they touched and passed over: 名人 is the oldest title in shogi, first held in 1612 and studied today with AI engines. A human voice reads it while each phrase lights. That is his own method, "read Japanese books with audio transcript listening at the same time" (voice), handed to a stranger in the first minute.
- **1:30** They save 達人. It lifts out of the sentence and becomes their first star.
- **2:10** The passage returns as a card with 達人 hidden. Wrong: the paper samurai he loved on 09-23 cuts once, for two seconds, then the answer, "clear, precisse, and crispt and EASILY UNDERSTANABLE, though elegant when an answer is wrong" (09-23). Right: the star brightens.
- **3:00** Back at the door, with one star of their own.

His own first three minutes are different, because his sky should be full on day one. Agents counted 8,331 notes and 47,400 reviews in his Anki decks from 2009 to 2024, "an archaeology of methodology reboots" in which each reboot orphaned its history (GitHub issue #32, agent-measured). ### 1.5 The year, toward N1 and beyond

I'd shape the year as 守破離, the learning path of the tea and sword schools: keep the form, break it, leave it.

- **守, keep the form: to N1.** N1 and N2 words, kanji and grammar thrown "in the blender of the high level meta topics i want", as cards "that reinforce themselves … compounding each other explontially as they go" (both 10-07). Anki is the floor; FSRS keeps the time.
- **破, break it: his voice.** For the channel he wants to "speak like this in Japanese consistently with deep confidence and as much as possible deep precision" (voice). In a speaking room he talks for three minutes about what he read, the sensei listens ("you speak to it, it judges your level", 08-10, T2), and the words he reached for and lacked become his next cards.
- **離, leave it: his fields.** "a master's degree in a few different topics in Japanese and be able to hold my weight on those types of conversations" (voice); "like four master's levels" (10-07). The shelf moves to native long-form in them, and the books he listens to (半導体戦争, GRIT, 脳に任せる) arrive pre-taught: their hard words already met, in cards, before he presses play.

A picture of the goal, mine: a 法話 he first met in October plays in a calm voice, and he follows every line. Under the last line, in small type, is the day he first missed 無明, and each place it came back since.

### 1.6 How every room flows into the next

A Japanese house is modular. Rooms are measured in tatami and divided by sliding panels, and two rooms become one when a panel slides. I read "conencted and flowing, moduluar" this way: each room complete in itself, every wall a panel. Moving is one motion. A tapped word lifts out of its sentence and becomes the word sheet; the dive into it is a true magnification (08-05, T3); a shelf picture is the doorway into its article; a finished card rises into the sky.

Each room has its own material inside the one world he picks. That was his 09-23 pick on the board (q6): 間 ma, relief between purposes, with the pull of the field kept for the door and the return.

The parts can go: grey pills, the settings-menu dojo, the same cream at one weight on every screen.

He has asked for the ancient and the electric together since August: "ancient Japan meets anime meets outer space meets obsidian graph meets additive AI study app" (08-05, T2); "1600 Japan on a 2335 device" (08-20, T3). For "cyberpunk meets washi" I propose the world as a woodblock print. A print is several carved blocks, each one layer, laid onto one sheet and kept in register by the 見当, the printer's mark (in everyday speech the same word means an aim or a guess; 見当違い is off the mark). Three blocks:

- **The ground is ancient Japan:** washi, sumi, mineral pigment, woodblock light, and ink that moves like real ink: "make it more natural still. More like real ink. Still feels very digital" (08-13, T2).
- **The key line is cutting-edge Japan:** "fresh, crisp, smooth, elegant, like an apple Ios feel, very smooth, sharp, textured" (10-02). Hairline rules, edges with relief, type at sizes he can read (furigana and English were "BARELY readable at all!", 09-29), "haptic feedback for eery scroll" (09-17).
- **The electric block is cyberpunk.** By day it prints only in the detail: his own record drawn finely in the margins of each word (stroke count, the day he met it, how often it came back, its frequency, its pitch accent), the way an Akira or Ghost in the Shell background holds detail you find on the tenth viewing. By night it takes the whole sheet. 殻 攻殻・燐光, phosphor on terminal black, was the one electric world he kept on 08-13 (T2); an AI-drafted roster dropped it on 08-15 with no word of his. It comes back as every room's night, readable first (08-14: "too dark, no other theme options, too hard to read", T2). Never ornament: "more real, more truth in the mechanism, never more decoration" (08-13, T2).

One world, many materials keeps both of his laws: "no room may change scheme unprompted" (08-20, T3, after "opened a dictionary paper and the scheme changed completely.", T2) and "no more pages that look the same with no differnetiatno". He picks the world with the stones (frame 23); each room keeps its own paper, light and layout inside it.

Motion is how I read "the recrusive animation of masters", with message 9's "80s and 90s anime artists and directors" behind it: hand-drawn detail, and one gesture at every scale, like the 回. The stroke that writes a kanji also opens a room and closes a day. Slow at the door, still during an attempt, quick in the instrument, never "erratic and jarring" (08-10, T3).

(00:09); "singluar focus on the best app in the world" (00:34); "yes, and it has to make massive money and splash as a japanese learning app that rivals all the others!" (00:49). His rule for winning (09-28): "integrate in our own way and style at least what all the other apps already have and do grat bu tdo it in our own way before narnoiong in on one stupid idea".

**Position** (a bet until strangers stay). Each rival does one part well, and Bunki takes each part into one corridor with one memory. Duolingo's first lesson before sign-up, without the guilt. WaniKani's kanji ladder, carried on to Kanken 1級. Bunpro's grammar by level, met in passages and in tests that explain every wrong option. Then a ceiling no rival aims at (N1, master's-level fields, Kanken 1級) on a spine that also serves a beginner of 74.

**What people share:** a sky after a hundred days; the samurai's two-second cut; a kanji written in living ink; a woodblock page; and "I read this", a page they couldn't read a month before, dated. Each shares in one tap, and each link opens straight into a sky.

**How it earns** (to test). Free: the door, the sky, the dictionary, one voiced article a day, a daily allowance of cards. One paid plan, monthly or yearly, in the band serious tools charge (Bunpro $5 a month and Satori Reader $9, per their pricing pages on 2026-10-08; WaniKani, Migaku and LingQ about $9–15, per a 2026 review site): every voice, the whole shelf, unlimited passage decks, the sensei, full mocks, the Kanken track, your own texts, sync. The sensei and the voices cost money per use, so the price has to carry them. Who pays: adults from about N3 up, Anki and AJATT people, JLPT candidates with a date, Kanken climbers, people living in Japan.

**From PWA to the App Store.** Keep the PWA, so a shared link opens with no install. For iOS, wrap it (Capacitor or a WKWebView shell) with real native value, since Apple's guideline 4.2 turns away repackaged websites: offline packs, notifications, a widget of his sky, background audio, and the haptics he asked for, which an iPhone web page can barely drive. Rules on paying outside Apple's in-app purchase have loosened in the EU, the US and Japan since 2024 and differ by country; check at launch. Android: a Trusted Web Activity.

**Licences** (an audit before launch). JMdict, KANJIDIC2 and KanjiVG are CC BY-SA: credit them and keep adapted dictionary data share-alike; a paid app is fine, a closed dictionary isn't. The SKIP codes carry a documented non-commercial conflict: clear it or drop them. Wikinews (CC BY 2.5) and Global Voices (CC BY 3.0) allow commercial use with credit. Aozora Bunko is mostly public domain; check each work. Asahi, Yomiuri and Mainichi (his 09-09 ask) need a licence deal. Real JLPT papers are never shipped: they stay a private import on his device, and paid mocks are original, in the official format. AI pictures: log each image's generator and its terms, and use nothing whose terms bar commercial use; purely AI-made images may not be protected by copyright, so the brand marks (seal, torii, samurai) need a human hand. Voices: confirm the provider allows serving stored audio to paying users.

**What has to be true before it earns.** Every article checked before it ships. A voice on every article and every card. English mode in English on every room. A first screen that is instant and works offline. Day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention measured from the first public day, beside one learning number: words read in real articles that were unknown thirty days earlier. And first of all, the whole walk works end to end, for him, on his phone, for a month. On 10-03 he said he "never got a single clean experience" (T2).

The bar is a felt test, so I've written it as questions the build must answer yes to. The questions are mine.

- **Dōgen:** 「仏道をならふといふは、自己をならふなり。自己をならふといふは、自己をわするるなり。」(正法眼蔵, 現成公案). Is each card the practice itself, whole, and not a step toward something else? Does Bunki learn him so well that he forgets it is there?
- **Musashi:** the last two of the nine rules in the 地の巻 of 五輪書, わずかなる事にも気を付くる事 (mind the smallest things) and 役に立たぬ事をせざる事 (do nothing that is of no use). Is every detail exact? Is anything on this screen doing nothing?
- **Bashō:** 不易流行, the lasting inside the passing. Does each walk leave one moment that stays?
- **Hakuin:** does the stick land, and does it help?
- **Ōtomo, Oshii, Miyazaki, Takahata, Kon:** freeze any frame. Does it hold as a picture, its background worked as hard as its foreground?

If any answer is no, it isn't finished. And his question, "can we do that!?": yes, on three conditions that come before any skin. The content becomes the best part of the app. Every sentence has a voice. And the whole walk holds on his phone, from the door and back.
