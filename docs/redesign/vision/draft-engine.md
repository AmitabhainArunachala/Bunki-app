## 1. What he is asking for, as I read it

One sentence tells the rest of the brief what it is for: "I want the user to fall in love with the depth and beauty of Japanese, with the recursive quality of the language and the culture." Everything else in it serves that love or gets in its way.

Japanese is recursive by construction. Parts build kanji, kanji build words, the same kanji return inside other words, clauses nest in front of the noun they describe, and the culture folds its history into everyday vocabulary. Bunki's job is to make that structure touchable and to remember every step he takes through it: one memory under everything, one web through everything, and rooms that are different views of the same web, each with its own material. In his three-part phrase, the ancient is the structure itself, the cutting-edge tech is the machine that remembers and brings things back, and the cyberpunk is where they meet: data drawn by hand.

He named it on 08-07: "It is all one single APP and the APP name is 回廊 KAIRO" (agent record). 回廊 is a temple cloister; its homophone 回路 is a circuit (bank-founding §1.5). Both go round and come back.

Can we do that? Yes. The floor exists: FSRS scheduling, the woodblock magazine shelf, the word sky he likes, and 2,000 N2/N1 passage cards written on a draft branch (none on his phone as of 10-07, per ASKS). Most of what he asks for is what is still missing: the web made visible, a voice on everything, the sensei as a presence, rooms that feel like different places.

## 2. The learning engine

His goal (10-07 00:37): "it was so so so clear that hte goal was to create flashcards that would get me to that level"!!!!!!!!!". And how (10-07 10:18): "cards that reinforce themselves and in a varity of differnt ways and context and forms compounding each other explontially as they go".

### 2.1 One web: the recursion you can touch

On 10-07 his deck showed him 財政 twice (frames 13 and 15): in a lecture on the French Revolution and in a news line about the deficit. Neither card showed what the word carries. 財 is 貝 plus 才, and 貝 is a cowrie shell. Shells were money in ancient China, so the shell sits inside 買 buy, 貨 goods, 費 cost, 貯 save and 資 capital. The same lecture passage holds 貴族 and 負担, and 貴 and 負 carry the shell too. 政 comes back in that passage as 王政 and in the news line as 政府. Three grammar patterns ride along (〜しかありません, 〜こそが, 〜わけです). And 財 opens 文化財, which walks out of French royal debt into Japan's national treasures. The structure was all there, folded and unseen. The engine's first job is to unfold it.

- **One web under the app.** Words, kanji, parts, readings, grammar patterns, passages and culture notes are nodes. The links are the language's own relations: shares a kanji, a part or a reading; a near-synonym in another register; appears in; uses; comes from. Cards, reader, sky, tests and sensei are views of the one web.
- **One tap, then a dive.** One tap gives reading, meaning and 覚える. A second tap dives into the word: its kanji open into their parts, and the family lights up across his sky. His asks: "a light tap on one word will bring up at least ten" (08-05, agent record), and "ANY KANJI OR WORD IN THE ENTIRE APP should be able to be clicked in and give the same information as any other place, furigana, english word, click deeper and get the whole entry! no excuses." (09-29).
- **Culture is a link.** 玄関, every home's front door, began as a Zen word for the gate into the profound. 挨拶 was the push and counter-push between Zen master and student. 我慢 meant pride before it meant endurance. For a learner whose fields include Buddhist philosophy, this is where the love starts.
- **Long sentences unfold** into their nested clauses (長く開かれていなかった三部会) and fold back.
- **Every walk comes back.** On 09-23 he liked the living thread: "I DO LIKE THE DIRECTION!" Go as deep as you like, and the thread returns you to the sentence you left.

Where it stands: the data exists (dictionary, kanji anatomy, deck tokens, glosses, grammar tables), and a Words room is step 4 of `STAGE2_PLAN.md`. Today's card back hides the kanji view in a collapsed row (frame 15).
