*The holistic vision for the 10-08 redesign, written 2026-10-08 for designers, builders and judges, and for John. It reframes THE BRIEF (message 1 of `JOHN_10-08_VERBATIM.md`) using everything he has said since July: the three banks (`bank-look.md`, `bank-learning.md`, `bank-founding.md`), the four lens drafts (`draft-journey.md`, `draft-engine.md`, `draft-world.md`, `draft-market.md`), the research (`../research/SURFACE.md`, `../research/INSPO.md`), Sol's foundation (`../FOUNDATION.md`) and today's screens (`../reference/*.png`).*

## How to read this

- **His words are always in quotation marks** and copied exactly, typos kept. Every quote is listed with its source in Appendix A. Anything outside quotation marks is my reading or proposal, and the text says so where it matters ("my reading", "proposal").
- **Provenance.** Most quotes are his own typed words (T1 in the banks). Some are his words saved verbatim by an agent (T2, marked *agent record* in Appendix A): this includes **everything from 10-04, the voice note and the typed brief**, and several August records. A few are agent paraphrases (*agent paraphrase*, direction only, never his wording).
- **Weighting, by his own rule.** Message 1, THE BRIEF, is the frame. Messages 3–5 are "just hints, jsut direction" (message 7): they tune the brief and never replace it. Message 9 is THE BAR, a felt test the whole thing must pass. Message 10 (money) sits inside the brief's "viral hit" and "best japanese learning app on the planet". The lens drafts are inputs, not the frame: where one of them has a pet idea (kintsugi seams, the ink stone, 守破離, strata percentages), it appears here as an option, not a law.
- **Every section ends with "Consequences"**: things a designer can do, check or refuse on Monday.

## 1. The vision

### In one sentence

**Bunki is one beautiful, fast, honest place where Japanese shows its own recursive depth (word into kanji into parts into family into passage into culture, and back) so vividly that a learner falls in love with it, comes back every day, and climbs, as John is climbing, all the way to N1, his fields, his own voice on camera and Kanken 1級.**

### In one paragraph

THE BRIEF asks for an app that is "inspiring, conencted and flowing, moduluar, interactive, engaging, immersive, elegant and refined but also engageble and addivtive", and it says what all of that is for: "I want the user to fall in love with the depth and beauty of Japanese, with the recursive quality of the language and the culture." Everything else in the brief either makes that love possible (cleaner lines, sharper details, quick and punchy, clean and organized, fluid, cutting edge) or names what kills it (boring, bland, confusing navigation, English mode showing Japanese buttons, the strewn dojo, pages that all look the same). My reading: Bunki is a 回廊, a temple corridor that circles and returns (the name he gave it on 08-07), built on a 回路, a circuit. Its ground is ancient Japan (washi, sumi, woodblock light), its instrument is cutting-edge Japan (hairline precision, exact numbers, instant response), and its night is the cyberpunk city glowing through the paper ("cyberpunk meets washi", a hint he gave in message 5). Under it runs one memory of the learner and one web of the language, so that every word is a door, every walk comes back, reading feeds the cards and the cards feed reading. It must be calm and it must pull; it must be beautiful and it must work. If it is only pretty it fails; if it is only functional it fails. The bar is his: "it would make Dogen and Musashi and Basho and Hakuin to their knees crying at the beauty.  As well as all the great 80s and 90s anime artists and directors."

(That line is attributed to John in `CLOUD_MISSION.md` as "if just pretty it fails, if just functional it fails — it must be both." I could not find it in `JOHN_10-08_VERBATIM.md` or the three banks, so I use it as the mission's standard and do not quote it as verified wording of his.)

**Consequences**
- Every screen must serve the sentence above. A screen that neither deepens love of the language nor moves the learner forward is cut.
- The brief is judged clause by clause (the self-check at the end lists 40 clauses). No single clause, hint or draft idea is allowed to become the whole frame.
- Beauty and function are judged together on every screen: a reviewer marks each screen "beautiful? yes/no" and "clear and useful? yes/no", and only yes/yes passes.

- **Read-while-listen is the central study act**, not a feature: the reader is built around a voice.
- **Open questions go to him, not into the design**: the N1 date, the four fields, the public name (§11).

## 3. The feeling

THE BRIEF's feeling words come in pairs that seem to pull against each other, and he has said more than once that picking one side "collapes it". The pairs:

- **Elegant and refined, but also engaging and addictive.** "elegant and refined but also engageble and addivtive" (THE BRIEF). He typed "addivtive"; beside "engageble" it reads as *addictive*. In September he gave the method: "we keep the main app as it is, subtle, zen, mature, refined aesthitc, but we fill it with little things like this!" (09-23), and "these are the little things that i think will set us apart and we should integrate more." (09-23).
- **Calm and alive.** The hint of message 5: "layers of depth, clarity, calm balnced with the engaging soothing crisp and eep detialed recrusive animation of masters". In August: "mesmerizing — recursive hypnosis that pulls you in; extreme focus and concentration but also ease" (08-10, agent record), and the focus room as "a tool for focus and clarity and peace and concentration." (09-23).
- **Zen, but not soft.** "zen is not softness. Hakuin's stick as much as the still water" (08-11, agent paraphrase). When an answer is wrong: "it needs to be clear, precisse, and crispt and EASILY UNDERSTANABLE, though elegant when an answer is wrong." (09-23).
- **Fast and deep.** "quick and punchy" and "fluid, fast, quick, clear, sharp" (THE BRIEF), and "like we're in a spaceship that's zooming and we're, everything is super clear and we're learning Japanese incredibly fast with incredible density and retention and recursion and duplication" (10-04, agent record of his dictation).
- **Alive, popping, fun.** The engaging half is not a garnish; he has asked for it loudly and repeatedly, not only for calm. "THIS IS ALL VERY VERY VERY PLAIN AND BORING!!!! needs to have more pop, more defintion, even art. like readign a newspaper or magazine!!! it shoudl be much much much more alive." (09-29). "more often than not, it needs to be as alive and interactive as possible. even in the SRS sessiions." (09-23). "Bunki still seems very flat and stale on many many dimensions." (09-23). From the founding week: "The experience should be beautiful, fun, and immersive." (07-27, agent record). And on the samurai: "this is a vibe i totally want to iterate, refine and develop 1000x!!" (09-23).
- **Organized, with some wildness.** "It's multi-layered, it's recursive, it's sophisticated, it's organized, it's logical and it has a systematic way to it, but it's also a bit haywire in some ways as well. So that's okay as long as it's organized." (10-04).

Against all of these stands his most repeated verdict, 26 times since August (`bank-look.md` §2): flat, bland, everything blending. "it all blends together, no reader knwos what is what!!!" (09-29). "It still feels too boring, too confusing to navigate, too, bland." (THE BRIEF).

My reading of how the pairs resolve: **calm is the ground and depth is the relief; the surface is alive and the pull is the next door, not a points system.** Both halves get equal design budget: a concept that is serene but does not make you want one more card has failed the brief exactly as much as one that is busy. Layers must read as layers (contrast, edge, shadow, material), so the eye knows what each part is for. The engagement comes from the language opening under your finger, from the half-known word coming back, from small authored surprises, and from content you want to read. "Gamified is not the right word" (08-05, agent record). In the logs searched he never asked for streaks or points, and never against them (`bank-learning.md` Z3); the design should not invent either position for him, so the pull is built from things he has asked for.

**Consequences**
- **Every screen has visible depth**: at least three distinguishable layers (ground, surface, signal), separated by edge, relief or material, never by a flat grey fill. A screenshot in greyscale must still show which part is which.
- **"fill it with little things"** (his method, 09-23): every room carries a layer of small authored responses (at least three per room, listed in the concept's `SPEC.md`): a word that answers touch with weight, a seal that presses, a returning word that glints in a new sentence, a picture that wakes at night. Each is under a second and carries meaning; none is ambient decoration.
- **One signature moment per room**, bigger and rarer (the samurai's cut, a word lifting into the sky, the 財 dive opening to the cowrie shell). This is where tears are designed: each concept names, per room, the moment meant to move the learner, and a reviewer who has not seen it says whether it did.
- **The pull, built in, without guilt** (my reading of "engageble and addivtive"): every session ends on a visible next door (tomorrow's first word half-shown, the article that now holds five of today's words); the shelf is fresh every day, never "the same artiles all the time" (09-17); the sky visibly changes with what you did; a run of right answers is felt (the card's pace and sound tighten) without being counted as a score. The test: a tester who finishes a session reaches for one more without being asked.
- **Rest states are still; meaning moves.** Ambient motion is slow (cycles of six seconds or more); interaction motion is crisp (under 300 ms for chrome). Nothing "erratic and jarring" (08-10, agent paraphrase).
- **A wrong answer is unmistakable within 300 ms**, then elegant: the verdict, a held beat, the why in one line, the right form inked in.
- **No guilt mechanics.** No loss-threat copy, no energy meters, no nagging. Any count records what was done.

## 4. The world

He asked for three Japans at once: "I want to feel a mixture between ancient japan and cutting edge tech japan and cyberpunk beautiufl createve hyper detail oreinted japan." (THE BRIEF). He has asked for the same fusion since August: "ancient Japan meets anime meets outer space meets obsidian graph meets additive AI study app." (08-05, agent record); "1600 Japan on a 2335 device" (08-20, agent paraphrase). The hint of message 5 names the meeting point: "cyberpunk meets washi".

Two facts from the record shape the world:
1. **The electric half went missing.** On 08-13 he was shown three cyber worlds (an AKIRA/Ghost-in-the-Shell set he had asked for, per an agent's note) and kept one, 殻 攻殻・燐光, phosphor on terminal black. An AI-drafted roster dropped it two days later with no word of his (`bank-founding.md` §3.2). Today's screens (`../reference/`) are cream washi throughout, with no electricity anywhere.
2. **Real over decoration.** "more real, more truth in the mechanism, never more decoration." (08-13, agent record). He warmed to the ink only when it became a real fluid simulation. He chose woodblock pictures: "more refinement, subtley, japanese traditional nihon ga or woodblock style, more sublime." (10-02), then "yeah, the woodblock is kind of nice" (10-02).

My synthesis (built from the world lens, `draft-world.md`, and INSPO's "paper lantern city"): **paper is the ground, signal is the instrument, night is the city.**

- **Ancient Japan, the ground.** Washi with real fibre texture that survives a screenshot; sumi ink with body; 藍 indigo (his default world since 08-13) as structure; one 朱 vermilion that means *this, now*; lacquer only as a frame, never a whole page (he rejected an all-lacquer sheet as "too dark, no other theme options, too hard to read", 08-14, agent record); gold only where something was earned. Shippori Mincho B1 for Japanese content, Kaisei Tokumin for editorial display, Yuji Syuku as a rare human hand, the system sans for chrome.
- **Cutting-edge tech Japan, the instrument.** It is how the app behaves as much as how it looks: instant lookups, spring physics on the sheet and the card (not CSS easing curves), haptic ticks on capture and grade, a voice line synced to the word, sync across devices that just happens, and the native shell's widget. Visually: hairlines instead of boxes, print registration marks, transit-map clarity, tabular numerals, buttons with physical travel ("buttons still feel antiquated and not sharp, not crisp or clear.", 10-02), and the iOS crispness he asked for: "fresh, crisp, smooth, elegant, like an apple Ios feel, very smooth, sharp, textured, much more clear and crisp." (10-02).
- **Cyberpunk Japan, the city at night, and the hyper-detail layer.** Cyberpunk is a light source, not a colour scheme (proposal, made as concrete as the ancient half): a phosphor signal colour taken from the 殻 world he kept (green on terminal black) or a 藍-compatible cyan, used as emitted light with real falloff, never as a fill; a monospaced tabular face for data (stroke counts, intervals, dates, codes); vertical 縦書き room titles that at night read like a lit sign down a building; rain and wet reflection layers on the night prints; the web drawn as 回路 traces between related words, alive when a dive is in progress; and one signal light per screen by day, the whole atmosphere at night, when the 藍 room glows like Tokyo seen from a quiet window. The woodblock heroes get night states (the same print, lanterns lit, rain), which joins Hasui to Ōtomo in one picture. Hyper-detail lives in the language itself: stroke counts, components, pitch, first-met dates, drawn finely in the margins like the background of an Akira plate, and every one of those marks is real data from his record.

The 80s–90s anime masters and the four Japanese masters of message 9 shape motion and restraint (§10 turns each into a test). "recrusive animation of masters" (message 5) I read as hand-layered depth (multiplane parallax), held frames at the moment of meaning, and the match cut: the same glyph carries you from sentence to kanji to part to family and back, so you never feel you changed page.

What this world must never become (INSPO Part 5): sakura wallpaper, rising-sun rays, the Great Wave on every surface, katana cursors, "Konnichiwa!", fake brush fonts, neon-pink pastiche, glass soup, fake HUD numbers. "DO NOT BE A FUCKIGN WEEB ON THIS!!!!" (09-28) was said about learning method, but common sense applies to the look too: "use common sense on everything" (10-01).

**Consequences**
- **Every screen contains all three Japans** in fixed roles: paper ground, precise signal layer, and (at night or in the detail) the city light. No room is "the cyber room".
- **One signal light per screen**: exactly one element carries the signal colour or glow (the live card, the word being spoken, the next step).
- **Three-Japans test, per screen**: a reviewer points to (a) the paper element, (b) the instrument element (a hairline, a tabular number, a crisp control) and (c) the city element (the signal light by day; glow, rain or lit sign by night). Any missing one fails.
- **Hyper-detail is countable**: the Words plate and every card back show at least five real-data marks (stroke count, components, pitch, first-met date, frequency or JLPT/Kanken grade), set small in the margin, all legible at 390px.
- **Cutting edge is felt in the hand**: every primary control has press travel and a haptic on native; the word sheet and card move on springs; a lookup answers before the finger lifts (check 20).
- **Every annotation is real data.** A reviewer can tap any number, date or code and find it in the learner record or the dictionary. Decorative coordinates fail.
- **Night is a designed state, not an inversion**: every room has day and night; night text is lifted, never sunk; contrast is measured.
- **Japanese comes from structure (type, paper, ruling, seal), not stickers.** If more than about one element in five is ornament, it is costume and fails.
- **The woodblock pictures stay** and gain a full-bleed hero, a cartouche title, and a night print.
- **The cyber world he chose returns** as the night side or a world option, pending his word (§11).

### 5.1 Quick, punchy, clean, organized, never confusing

"I want the app to be quick and punchy, clean and organized. Not confusing in the least." (THE BRIEF). His September and October notes show what confusion is to him: vague labels ("Unreviewed practice.. ?? what does that mean?", 09-23), redundant buttons, help boxes in prime space ("this is... weird, awkward. not clear.", 10-02), text too small ("furigana and english is too small, BARELY readable at all!", 09-29), and placeholders. ### 5.2 Five rooms and a door, distinct yet one family

Sol's foundation gives five destinations: **Today 今日 · Read 読む · Learn 学ぶ · Words 辞書 · Me 私**, with the word sky as the front door (the tab bar is hidden there). He defended the door: "the opening page is not the problem. that is cool and intuitive and perfeclty fine." (09-29). He also asked: "THE DOJO, or study room, needs to be accessible from ANY LOCATION WHATSOEVER" (09-17). The tab bar answers that.

### 5.3 Connected, flowing, modular, interactive

"conencted and flowing, moduluar, interactive" (THE BRIEF). "moduluar" is a new word in his record (`bank-founding.md` §4). My reading, two senses that support each other:
- **For the learner:** the same blocks (a card run, an article, a listening pass, a focus drill, a spoken answer, a test) assemble into a five-minute walk on a platform or a forty-minute sitting at a desk.
- **For the build:** rooms are modules on one record (`rooms/{today,read,learn,words,me,cards}.css` in the Stage 2 plan), sharing one capture action (覚える), one word sheet and one tap grammar, so any room can be rebuilt without touching his history.

Connected means rooms open into rooms. A tapped word lifts out of its sentence into the word sheet; a shelf picture is the doorway into its article; a finished card's word rises into the sky; back returns to the exact line. "NOTHING DISAPPEARS." (08-08, agent paraphrase). Interactive means every word is a door: "ANY KANJI OR WORD IN THE ENTIRE APP should be able to be clicked in and give the same information as any other place, furigana, english word, click deeper and get the whole entry! no excuses." (09-29). Today the reader and the sky answer a tap differently (`bank-founding.md` §3.6); that ends.

### 5.4 The EN/JA law

"No silly contraditions like the English setting showing japanese buttons or confusing navitation." (THE BRIEF). Today's deck player shows もう一度, 思い出せた and 削除 under EN (`../reference/sheet-6-deck-back.png`), and Sol's gallery still shows a "dojo" label and Japanese chips in places (`sol-foundation-contacts-01.png`). The foundation already provides the mechanism: `tx(ja, en)` throws without English in EN; `withEn`, `biLabel`, `[data-ui-content='learning']`, and `tools/lint-ui-language.mjs`.

The law: **the interface language governs all chrome, absolutely.** EN: every button, label, heading, tab, placeholder, aria-label and toast is English. 日本語: all of it is Japanese. The Japanese being learned (words, sentences, readings, titles of Japanese sources) is never translated away in either mode. Allowed in EN chrome: the named seals and room glyphs as non-interactive marks beside an English label, and the 日本語 option itself. On cards he wants English "there but not in your face. You have to click and work a little bit to see the English." (10-04); that is a learning rule, separate from chrome.

**Consequences**
- **One primary action per screen.** One obvious next step; everything else is secondary or behind one door.
- **Labels say what a tap does** in plain words. No vagueness like "Unreviewed practice.. ?? what does that mean?" (09-23) or "does A USEFUL DISTINCITON MEAN!!!!" (09-23); no AI-sounding copy.
- **No help boxes in prime space.** Teach by the first interaction; help lives behind an (i).
- **No placeholder promises** ("coming soon", 準備中) ever ship. A feature is present or absent.
- **No redundancy**: no two controls on one screen go to the same place; each practice has one home.
- **Every room is identifiable from a thumbnail**, by material, layout and light, without reading a word.
- **Tap grammar is identical everywhere**: one tap = reading + meaning + 覚える; a second tap dives; back returns to the exact line. Card fronts and a strict test mode are the only places a tap does nothing, and they say so.
- **EN mode: zero Japanese chrome. 日本語 mode: zero English chrome** (except the EN toggle). Lint plus screenshot review in both languages, every room, day and night.
- **Content first.** On Read, the first story's picture is visible above the fold; filters fold behind one control instead of two rows of grey pills.
- **Small, subtle utility**: the bug button stays reachable everywhere but "needs to be far more subtle" (10-02).

## 6. The learning

### 6.1 The recursive web

"the recursive quality of the language and the culture" (THE BRIEF) has roots back to the founding week: the app as a "multi-dimensional, structured, recursive learning journey" (07-27, agent record). Japanese is recursive by construction: parts build kanji, kanji build words, the same kanji return in other words, clauses nest before nouns, and the culture folds its history into everyday vocabulary. The engine lens's example (`draft-engine.md` §2.1): 財政 opens into 財, which is 貝 (the cowrie shell, once money) plus 才; the shell sits inside 買 buy, 貨 goods, 費 cost, 資 capital; 財 also opens 文化財, Japan's cultural properties. Culture is a link too: 玄関 began as a Zen word for the gate into the profound.

So the web is: **word ↔ kanji ↔ parts ↔ siblings (words sharing a kanji or part, near-synonyms in another register) ↔ passages where they appear ↔ grammar patterns they ride with ↔ culture notes and etymology.** Every card, article, test and the sky are views of this one web, over one learner record. "a light tap on one word will bring up at least ten" (08-05, agent record).

### 6.2 Reading feeds cards, cards feed reading

His method is reading while listening, and "EVERY SINGLE ARTILE NEEDS A VOICE!!!" (09-29). His content verdict is harsh and fair: "the artles that i'm seeing whe4n i read them they are mostly shit. bad content, bad for a learner, too short." (10-03). He wants a magazine: "we want this to be like a magazine, or a newspaper, with nice graphics and animations and colors for each article" (10-02).

The loop:
1. **Read and listen.** A voice that passed his ear reads the article; the spoken line lights as it goes. ("the basic computer voice needs to NOT BE AN OPTOIN AT ALL!!!", 09-17.)
2. **Capture in one tap.** A half-known word is saved from the sentence where he met it.
3. **The capture becomes cards, automatically, with an undo in view.** "it should do it automatically.." (09-29).
4. **Cards send him back to reading.** The shelf ranks articles by how many of his due and new words they hold, so reading becomes review nobody grades.
5. **Misses travel.** "missed answers appear more in SRS cards, in sensei conversations, in the floating world first drfit page, in the bookshelf, but discretely and in differnt contexts etc. so it is learned organically and presnted in multiple locations by a very very coordinated intelligence" (09-29).

### 6.3 Compounding cards

"we want very high uality japanese and cards that reinforce themselves and in a varity of differnt ways and context and forms compounding each other explontially as they go" (10-07). "take the same WORD or kanji or grammar point but present it in a dozen DIFFERNT WAYS!" (09-28). "I want longer cards … their interesting, their engaging, their recursive, their multi-layered, they have levels of repetition. They show the same word or the same kanji in different contexts with a lot of different textures." (10-04, voice). And the variety law: "if the exact same pattern appears in 28 cards that:s not a good sign" (10-07).

- **The unit is the passage card**: three or four sentences, one target, graded on the target alone, "front and center and super, super clear" (10-04).

## 8. The laws

### The eight laws

1. **The brief is the frame.** Hints tune it, the bar judges it, and no single line (his latest hint, or a draft's pet idea) replaces it. "i need a holsitic vision of what i want" (message 7).
2. **Beauty and function are one object.** Every beautiful thing carries meaning; every function is beautiful. A pretty screen that confuses fails; a clear screen that is bland fails.
3. **One app, one memory, one web.** "It is all one single APP" (08-07, agent record). Every word is a door with the same tap everywhere; every walk comes back to the exact line; nothing is a silo.
4. **One language at a time.** The interface language governs all chrome; the Japanese being learned is never translated away.
5. **One world, distinct rooms.** The colour world he picks never changes under him; each room has its own material, light, layout and motion; no two rooms look alike.
6. **One job per screen, one obvious next step.** Nothing strewn, nothing redundant, nothing waiting on an AI to answer a lookup.
7. **Real and true.** Real Japanese, real voices, real test formats, real data in every annotation; progress never faked; a wrong answer lands, clear and elegant.
8. **Calm ground, living detail.** Depth and stillness by default; the pull comes from the next door, the returning word and small authored moments, never from guilt.

### Always

- Show depth: layers that read as layers, contrast that tells each part's job.
- Keep the word sky, the woodblocks, Mincho and the 藍 world as the carried vibration (Track A).
- Give every article and every card a good voice.
- Keep 44px hit targets, measured contrast, reduced-motion fallbacks.
- Animate only transform and opacity.
- Keep storage markers, `data/fsrs-pin.json`, card ids and `bunki-cloze:*` keys untouched.
- Judge at 390×844, day and night, EN and 日本語.

### Never

- Japanese buttons in English mode, or English chrome in 日本語 mode.
- Two rows of grey pills, a tool wall or a tip box before content.
- "Coming soon", "準備中", or any promise of something missing.
- A robotic voice.
- A room that changes his colour world, or a page that looks like another page.
- Guilt notifications, loss-threat copy, energy meters, fake progress.
- Confetti, points rain, mascots (my reading, not his rule: he never spoke for or against them; they are cheap reward against "subtle, zen, mature, refined aesthitc". The pull is built from the little things instead).
- Sakura wallpaper, rising-sun rays, fake brush fonts, nonsense kanji, neon pastiche, fake data.
- Paywalled due reviews.
- Content without a licence of record.

**Consequences**
- The eight laws are printed at the top of every concept's `SPEC.md` and every room PR, and each PR states which laws it touched and how it was checked.
