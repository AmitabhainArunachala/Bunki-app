## Key question 1: What does an AJATT MCD card look like, front and back, in each format Khatz published (2010 original / AJATT+ era, 2012 "12 Free MCD Examples", 2017 new format)?

### Takeaway

Khatz never published a "2010 original" card format on the public blog: MCDs were developed summer–October 2010 and kept inside AJATT Plus/SilverSpoon until Feb–Mar 2012, so the first public Khatz-authored card examples are the twelve in "12 Free MCD Examples" (Oct 2012): front = native passage (one sentence to a multi-paragraph news excerpt) with the target hidden behind `＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃`, plus an English translation on the front for bilingual cards; back = the clozetext (bolded), then the full un-clozed passage, then dictionary entry/readings/source link. The 2017 "new format" is a change to the _base_ of dictionary-definition cards: leave the furigana on the front and treat the reading _and_ the kanji as base clozetexts, which Surusu then combinatorially explodes (はんね/半値 → ~9 cards).

**Format rules stated by Khatz (12 Free MCD Examples, 2012-10-07)**

- "here are a dozen free, real-life (i.e. from or inspired by actual cards in my actual decks) examples of MCDs. There are three basic types. Bilingual, tranny I mean transitional, and monolingual." — [12 Free MCD Examples](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/)
- "Remember, the basic MCD principle is this: instead of having 1 card with 9 unknowns, you have 9 cards with 1 unknown each. But you keep the same massive context as when you had a single big, bad, intimidating, f-off card. 9 is a fake number." — same
- "The front contains the cloze (the blank represented by ＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃ or some other equally awesome marker). The back contains the clozetext (the correct answer to the blank; the filler to the donut hole, if you will) as well as additional materials like definitions, readings, sound files and any other relevant reference material." — same
- Bilingual: "Bilingual MCDs are good for when you lack the knowledge — or the context — to happily handle monolingual cards. Beginners, noobs and nervous nellies should focus just on bilingual cards. Just as with old skool sentence cards, _don't go writing your own translations_. If you're noob enough to need a translation, ya shouldn't be rolling your own." — same
- Transitional: "Basically, they're MCDs of dictionary definitions of simple words you already know. The front of the card is monolingual, the back of the card is bilingual (so we have an English definition of the definition we're clozing). Contrast with bilingual cards, which are bilingual on both sides, and monolingual cards, which are monolingual on both sides." — same
- Monolingual: "Monolingual cards are especially well-suited to long, context-rich passages of L2 text. Having said that, they will of course work on short passages as well: they don't know how long your passage is 😛 . If you don't get how these are working, don't worry: you're not supposed to get it…yet. Focus first on bilingual and transitional cards. Get a few thousand of those under your belt. Your time will come." — same

**2017 "new format" (Khatz)**

- "up until now, the general advice (from me) on how to make MCD cards was that you should remove any and all furigana (kanji readings) from the front of the card. So the front of your base card (before inserting the clozes) would look like this: `【半値】 定価の半分。半分の値段。 「半値以下に値下げする」` After several months of experimentation, I am pleased to inform you that this is not necessary — and that you actually want to leave the furigana there, right on the front of the card, so: `はんね 【半値】 定価の半分。半分の値段。 「半値以下に値下げする」` And include it as part of your base clozetexts, so: はんね 半値. Remember, these are base clozetexts, so combinatorial explosion applies, meaning that you actually end up with (in this case) nine clozetexts, like so: はん　ね　半値　半　値　は　んね　半値　ん. Surusu, the world's sexiest SRS, handles the creation of extra clozetexts for you; just give it the base clozetexts and set 'JPNZ (n00b) (Japanese newbie)' as your clozemode." — Khatz, _A New MCD Card Format for Japanese (Even Lazier and More Effective Than Before)_, c. 2017-08, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/a-new-mcd-card-format-for-japanese-even-lazier-and-more-effective-than-before/)
- Rationale: "it gives extra context, which is always good, and makes the card easier without making it too easy; it's especially valuable to leave the furigana on the front of the card when you're trying to learn proper names … Also, leaving furigana on the front turns your learning into a divide-and-conquer proposition … we separate learning (testing) the readings of a new word from testing kanji knowledge of that same word. Ironically, while this makes each individual card easier, it actually sharpens your knowledge of both kanji and readings, and, going forward, prevents 'native-like forgetfulness' where you can accurately produce a reading but not necessarily always a kanji from memory". "Note that this doesn't make the old, 'no-furigana-on-the-front' cards obsolete, it just adds a new dimension, another card style, to your toolbelt." — same
- Khatz's signed reply (2017-08-26) to "these are for ddcs right? and also two clozes?": "right — and in the broadest sense possible, because they would also be good if, say, you had a Wikipedia article as context and you were learning the name of a notable person. … wrong 😀 . More than that. Combinatorial explosion applies, so: はんね / はん　ね / は / ん / んね / 半値 / 半　値 which makes about nine in this case." — same post, comments. (Note the two lists differ: the body lists `は んね 半値 ん`; the reply lists `は ん んね 半　値`. "ddcs" is the commenter's abbreviation, presumably dictionary-definition cards; Khatz did not expand it.)

**Multimedia / MXD cards (Khatz, 2019–2020)**

- Process: "Pick a YouTube video on a topic that interests you intensely … Convert the YouTube video to audio … Split the audio file into 30-second segments … Pick a small (30- to 90-second) segment of the video to focus on. You don't need to turn the whole video into learning material: that's both boring and overkill. … get a language tutor or language exchange partner to transcribe your small segment … Pick some target text. Anything from one sentence to three paragraphs is fine. Make this the FRONT of an SRS card. … Upload an audio file that matches some or all of that target text to the BACK of an SRS card. … Find a word you don't know (well) in that text. Call this your target word. … Add a dictionary definition of the target word to the BACK of your card. Hide the target word (create a cloze). Repeat from the #wordfinding step. Stop when you've done this for three words or get bored or tired, whichever comes first." — Khatz, _How To Turn a YouTube Video into a Permanent Learning Tool [i.e. a Series of Awesome Multimedia SRS MCD/MXD Cards]_, undated (video dated 2019.4), [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-turn-a-youtube-video-into-a-permanent-learning-tool-i-e-a-series-of-awesome-multimedia-srs-mcd-mxd-cards/)
- "Auto-generated (machine) subs are bad, avoid them." and subtitles in Asian languages "are more interpretations than true transcripts" because of diglossia. — same
- "You set up multiple SRS decks, each with its own format, let them compete against each other and see which comes out the most successfull (hint: it's MCDs/MXDs (Massive-Context Cloze Deletion Cards))." — Khatz, _The Problem With Conspiracy Theories Reloaded …_, undated (same era), [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/the-problem-with-conspiracy-theories-reloaded-colon-the-next-generation-of-electric-boogaloo-plus-how-and-why-this-applies-to-language-acquisition/)
- Surusu's multimedia capability predates MCDs: "Multimedia capability/audio and image support: … the ability to attach images and audio clips of [currently] up to 100KB each in size, to your cards." — Khatz, _Surusu Update: Multimedia et al._, c. 2009-04, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/surusu-update-multimedia-et-al/); later "Surusu multimedia now supports WAV files as well, in addition to OGG and MP3." — Khatz, _Surusu Update: MCDs/Cloze Deletions: Eurogram and Manual Mode Merged + WAV Support_, c. 2013-09, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/surusu-update-mcdscloze-deletions-eurogram-and-manual-mode-merged/)

**Newspaper-headline MCD recipe (Khatz, c. 2014-07)**

- "Create an MCD card containing the headline and the first three paragraphs of the adjoining article … The headline and first three paragraphs belong on the front of the card, the paragraphs will provide extra context, clues and cues — otherwise, all you've got is a disembodied headline, and nobody wants that. Hide (cloze) the words you didn't know. Obviously, after putting their dictionary definitions (monolingual or bilingual, your choice) and pronunciation aids (if necessary) onto the back of the card. Don't worry about understanding the whole article or knowing every word in the article. MCDs allow partial ignorance (partial knowledge). The sum of your partial knowledges will add up to complete knowledge. … focussing only on headlines (and on only one a day)". — Khatz, _How to Learn to Read L2 Newspapers Even Though You Suck Right Now and It Seems Impossible_, c. 2014-07, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-learn-to-read-l2-newspapers-even-though-you-suck-right-now-and-it-seems-impossible/)

**Lazy Kanji cards (Khatz, 2010; a separate single-kanji format, not an MCD)**

### Inferences

- Across all Khatz-authored examples the invariant is: front = untouched native text (or dictionary entry) with exactly one unit replaced by a fixed-width marker, with no hint text on the front other than (for bilingual cards) a professionally translated English sentence; back = clozetext first, then the full passage, then reference material. The `++++` divider and `XXXXX` marker in the rigabamboo series are reader conventions, not Khatz's.
- The "transitional" type is the clearest evidence that Khatz's MCD fronts can contain the headword _multiple_ times: every occurrence of the target is clozed (cards 7, 8, 10), consistent with his "single point of comparison/failure (that point may be repeated within a single card, but it's the same point)".
- The 2017 post implies that by then Khatz's default MCD base was a monolingual dictionary entry (DDC) rather than a long passage; the "massive" passage examples are from 2012.

### Gaps

- No Khatz-authored card from 2010–2011 is on the public blog; the AJATT+ "Korean MCD Examples", "MCDs Illustrated Using English", "MCD 102: Real MCD Examples From The Very Decks of Khatzumoto" are paywalled and not in the mirror.
- The MXD post gives no literal example card (only the procedure) and no date is recoverable from the mirror.
- The three 2018 "[SRS/MCD Bait]" posts are password-protected; their card format (if any) is unknown.

## Key question 2: What are Khatz's rules for deletion size, context size, cards-per-text, hints, and grading?

### Takeaway

Khatz's own public rules are few and mostly qualitative: hide a very small unit (≤1 word, "often 0.5~0.75 words"; one kanji or one particle in his Japanese examples; Surusu's singleton mode makes 1-character clozes), keep as much context as the source gives (one sentence up to a headline + three paragraphs), make one card per unknown (his cloze-generation tools cap at 512 cards per click and he later advised "Only 3 Clozetexts At A Time"), put the answer plus full passage plus dictionary material on the back, and grade only the clozed part on Surusu's 0–5 scale, erring high. The "never two kanji at once" rule and the "glance, don't read" rule are rigabamboo's forum summaries, which Khatz never contradicted but also never stated in those words.

**Surusu cloze modes (the "three modes" question)**

- The 2011 post is titled "Cloze Deletion Modes in Surusu" (slug "whats-the-difference-between-the-three-modes-for-cloze-deletions-in-surusu") and actually lists seven: Manual, Eurogram, Fullsubset ("Random words"), Singleton ("Single character / 漢"), LP ("long permutations / ふくわなが（複和長）/ scramble"), MX ("ミックス / mixed"), and "Random snippets = 部ぶん集ごう = Japanese subset". — Khatz, 2011-09-04, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-the-three-modes-for-cloze-deletions-in-surusu/)

**Grading**

- Khatz's Surusu scale: "0 = blank; 1 = totally incorrect; 2 = partially correct; 3 = correct, with great effort; 4 = correct, with some effort; 5 = correct, effortless; WARP = Out-of-this-world ballin' … generally about one or two orders of magnitude more powerful than a '5'. Use with great care." "Ironically enough, most people grade themselves too harshly. So: Don't be too harsh 😀 Don't grade yourself harshly on a card because you've had trouble with it in the past. Grade cards on a per rep basis". — _How To Score Reps In Surusu_, c. 2011-07, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu/); 2013 edition relabels "0 = total blank 1 = wrong 2 = mostly wrong 3 = mostly right 4 = right 5 = slam dunk". — [2013 Edition](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu-2013-edition/)

## Key question 3: What arguments does Khatz give for MCDs over sentence cards, and which are testable claims versus motivational framing?

### Takeaway

Khatz's case is a bundle of ~15 bullet claims from "MCD 101" plus reader testimonials. The testable ones are: (a) each card has a single failure point, so pass/fail is unambiguous and beginners can use advanced text; (b) MCDs make every card "i+1" by construction; (c) MCDs train particles/grammar that sentence cards let you gloss over; (d) MCDs convert to output (writing kanji, choosing particles) faster; (e) MCDs reduce cross-language interference; (f) the testing effect makes you feel you are not learning while learning. The rest ("fun", "elevator", "engineered inevitability", "unique to AJATT") is framing. He explicitly declines theoretical justification: "I can't justify any of it theoretically".

## Key question 4: What did Khatz and readers report about problems (boredom, leeches, too-easy cards, not understanding the passage, time cost) and the fixes?

### Takeaway

Khatz's public fixes are blunt: fall back to bilingual MCDs if you don't understand the whole, delete anything boring or too hard, run old and new decks in parallel and let one die, cull virgin cards after mass generation, and (2017) keep furigana on the front so cards are easier but not too easy. Readers reported MCD burnout and review time cost (several returned to plain sentences), spoilers from repeated particles, "false passes" and word-fragmentation from single-character clozes in Chinese, and persistent confusion about what MCDs even are; the forum's standard fix was "delete it".

### Cited Findings

**Not understanding the passage (Khatz)**

- "Hexe sayeth: You can cloze the kanji readings and individual word meanings, but how do you come to understand the meaning of the sentence? Can't you learn the meaning and reading of every word and still not know what the sentence means? — Yes, that is entirely possible. That's why you fall back to bilingual MCDs. If you don't get it, don't use it. If you don't like it, don't use it. If it's too hard or boring, delete it. Having said that, this ability to 'punch above your weight' — to handle texts far beyond your current level, is also part of the power of MCDs. So if curiosity drives you to want to crack a dense text without waiting for your language level to increase to a point where it's a cakewalk, then MCDs allow you to make a piecemeal, termites-in-wood, jab-into-submission attack on it, comprehending it bit by bit, until (if you stick around) eventually, you understand the whole." — Khatz, _MCDs: But What If I Don't Understand the Meaning of the Whole?_, c. 2013-08, [source](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/mcds-but-what-if-i-dont-understand-the-meaning-of-the-whole/)

**Tensions to flag for the report**

- _Massive vs. one-kanji_: Khatz's "massive" refers to context length; rigabamboo's "never two kanji" refers to deletion size. They are compatible, but Khatz's own examples cloze 2-kanji words, and the Chinese learner above found single-character clozes harmful. Khatz's Surusu singleton mode institutionalised 1-character clozes for CJK.
- _English on MCDs_: Khatz's bilingual cards put English on the **front** (translation as context) and transitional cards put English on the **back** (gloss of the definition); monolingual cards have none. Readers varied (クリス put English on the back of bilingual cards; Jan put English glosses on the back of micro-MCDs).
- _Must the passage be understood?_: Khatz says no ("partial ignorance", "curiosity, not comprehension"), with "fall back to bilingual" as the escape; forum regulars (ブライアン) say you must understand the gist. Khatz's 2010 sentence-era rule ("Understand the meaning of the entire sentence") was dropped for MCDs.
- _Input-before-output vs. active recall_: see KQ3.
- _Dating_: the blog's public MCD series dates from Feb–Mar 2012, but Khatz's deck switch was 2010-10-18 and AJATT+ content began 2010-11; the mirror's INDEX dates for the "MCD Revolution" posts are the 2012 publication dates.

### Inferences

- The failure modes readers report cluster into (1) review-time cost of long fronts, (2) over-generation of near-duplicate cards by automatic cloze tools, and (3) sub-word clozes damaging word-level recognition in Chinese. Khatz's institutional answers are deletion tooling (cull virgins, subset modes, "3 clozetexts at a time") and the 2017 easier-card format; he never publicly addressed (3).
- The "fall back to bilingual" rule means a learner should always keep an English-translated source for any passage they cannot parse — Khatz's sentence packs are explicitly marketed as that source.

- No leech-handling rule specific to MCDs beyond generic "delete"; no daily rep or card-creation targets.
