## Key question 1: Why did most of the community move away from MCDs toward vocab/targeted-sentence cards, in their own words? What did they say MCDs were good for?

### Takeaway

The retreat from MCDs began within weeks of Khatz's public launch (Feb–Mar 2012) and was driven by first-hand complaints that cluster into five themes: (1) review and creation time ("MCDs just took too much time for me to review when I could have been reading", "it took me nearly 25 minutes to make this card"); (2) seeing the same passage over and over because one passage spawns many cards ("I would end up seeing the same content over and over and over again… so very bored"; 500 sentences → 1,000 MCDs); (3) false passes and memorising the card rather than the item (sibling clozes giving the answer away; "you'll simply learn the card"); (4) ambiguity of the blank — multiple correct fillers, and single-kanji clozes that break words apart ("Clozing compounds one Kanji at a time >>> weak. Clozing words as words >>> strong"); and (5) the requirement for a monolingual, intermediate-plus level before MCDs work at all. What survived as consensus is that MCDs/cloze are good for _particles and grammar_, for _3+-kanji compounds_, for _production/output_, and for keeping native-media context alive — i.e. a supplement, not the main deck. Later guide-writers (Tatsumoto 2022, Animecards) formalised the rejection: cloze "stop[s] working once the intervals grow big enough" and MCD "hasn't received much adoption over the years due to being too difficult and complicated".

### Cited Findings — guides and critics [G]

- **Jalup (Bailey, 28 Jul 2014)**, pros: "forced 'output'… may help increase comprehension"; "words with three or more kanji"; "particles". Cons: "so much content on the front… you wind up doing a lot of skimming. Particularly for a newbie, you need to be digesting full sentences"; "'Fill in the blank' cards can sometimes feel more like a test"; harder to learn to make. Recommendation: "I wouldn't suggest anybody shift exclusively to MCDs until they are at least at an intermediate level… people who are intermediates or higher should use MCDs to learn words that are 3+ kanji." - **Tatsumoto (Oct 2022, updated)**: "Essentially they are just cloze cards… there is always an infinite number of answers for any given 'fill in the blank' question. For this reason cloze cards stop working once the intervals grow big enough. You either memorize the card itself… Or you just guess… I can't imagine a good application for MCDs and cloze cards in general when learning languages." Alternative for writing: "production TSCs" (word shown in kana in context; write the kanji). — [Tatsumoto, What do you think of using MCDs to train production?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/what-do-you-think-of-using-mcds-to-train-production.html)

- **Animecards (QM; current page)**: cloze cards are among formats that are "either useless or helpful only in specific situations (audio cards, cloze cards, simple word cards without context)". — [Animecards, Anki: Card Types](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)

- **Khatzumoto himself** continued iterating MCDs until at least 2017 (the furigana-on-front "combinatorial explosion" format, "about nine" cards from はんね/半値) — [AJATT, A New MCD Card Format, Aug 2017 comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/a-new-mcd-card-format-for-japanese-even-lazier-and-more-effective-than-before) — and in 2018 was still posting "SRS MCD bait" passages. ### Inferences

- The community never ran a comparison; the move away from MCDs was a cost decision (creation time, review time, repetition boredom) plus a validity worry (false passes, guessable or ambiguous blanks). Several people who quit explicitly kept the _ideas_ (one target per card, more context, highlight-not-blank) — フーラン in 2012 describes precisely the TSC that Tatsumoto later canonised.
- The consistent "good for" list across 2012, 2014 and 2017 is narrow and stable: particles/conjugations, long kanji compounds, output practice, and keeping context attached to media sentences. That is also the list modern note types still support as _optional_ cards (JPMN cloze card, Lapis audio card with the word blanked).
- Partial clozes (hiding one syllable or one kanji, leaving a stub) were a recurring practitioner fix for the "blank is unguessable" problem (L 2012; howtwosavealif3 2012 and 2017), but Thomas Smith's 2013 report is a direct counter-testimony for single-character clozes in compounds.

## Key question 2: The descendants — Matt vs Japan / MIA, Refold, Tatsumoto, Jalup, Nukemarine, Animecards, Kumasensei, LingQ — what each kept from AJATT, which card types they prescribe, and their stance on cloze

### Takeaway

MIA (2018–2020) was AJATT's sentence-card era re-stated in plain prose: 1T sentence on the front, no furigana, no picture on the front, definition of the target only on the back, full translations discouraged after the basic phase, monolingual transition after "a couple thousand" bilingual cards, delete leeches, 10–30 new/day, Anki = one-third of study time. It dropped MCDs entirely and renamed i+1 to "1T". Refold (2020–2026) keeps 1T mining, deletion, and "Anki is a supplement" but shifts the default beginner card to a word-front card with the sentence as support (JP1K: "Step 1: Recall the word's reading… Step 2: Recall the word's meaning… Flip to back"), lowers new cards to 5–10/day, adopts FSRS, and for the first time cites SLA literature. Tatsumoto claims the AJATT name, keeps sentence-front cards (TSC) and "keep your cards", and rejects both cloze and Refold. Animecards/TMW/Lapis push the word-front card for speed. Jalup is a premade one-unknown-per-card J-J sentence chain with "the meaning of the unknown word ONLY at the back". Nukemarine endorsed cloze only for subs2srs/grammar. Kumasensei and LingQ/Kaufmann could only be reached second-hand.

### Cited Findings

**Matt vs Japan / MIA [G, first-hand MIA text, Dec 2020 capture]**

- Definition: "A sentence card is an SRS card with a target-language example sentence on the front, and whatever is needed to understand the sentence on the back… In our opinion, sentence cards are the most effective way to use an SRS for vocabulary acquisition." The argument against vocab cards: "most Japanese words don't have a true English equivalent… if you look up the Japanese word '掛ける'… the definition listed is comically long." — [MIA JP Quickstart, "Sentence Cards"](https://web.archive.org/web/20201220134528/https://massimmersionapproach.com/table-of-contents/stage-1/jp-quickstart-guide/)
- 1T: "it's important that the sentence on the front be a one-target (1T) sentence… NOTE: Previously, MIA referred to '1T sentences' as 'i+1 sentences'… we decided to drop the term". — [same]
- Back of card: "Once you're past the basic vocab phase, putting full English translations on the back of cards is not recommended… it's best to stick to just putting the English definition of the target word/structure." Monolingual card = "a monolingual definition of the target word/structure on the back". Text-based front: "full kanji with no furigana… do not have any audio on the front… It can be useful to have native audio for the sentence on the back… but it is not necessary." — [same]
- Audio-based sentence cards (audio only on front) are presented as equal alternatives: "many people report finding it much easier to learn to read a word they have an audio-based card for than it is to learn to hear a word they have a text-based card for." — [same]
- Reviewing: "Do not try to translate… don't try to recall the exact definition of the target word… If you felt like you understood the sentence well, you don't have to read the back of the card." Grading: "would I benefit more from seeing this card more often?"; stick to Good/Again (Low-Key Anki). — [same]
- Pacing: "Aim to spend one third of that time on Anki… start with something like 10 cards a day… we don't recommend ever exceeding 30 cards per day… immersion is the most important component… the SRS is simply a supplement." — [same]
- Length: "sentence cards are likely to be most effective when the sentence is between 3 and 7 words long… it's perfectly fine to make cards out of fragments of longer sentences. Just make sure what you have on the front of your card is a complete clause." — [same]
- Pictures: "We don't recommend putting a picture on the front of any of your sentence cards… In real life a picture isn't going to be there… it can be useful to have images on the back of cards… especially… for nouns." — [same]
- Leeches: "When the same card lapses 5 times, Anki will mark it a 'leech'… it's best to just get rid of them… it's more productive to learn 5 normal cards in its place." — [same]
- Context-dependence (asked directly): "While reviewing, often times I feel like I have simply memorized the sentence, and still don't know the target word. — That's totally fine… When you come across that word in your immersion, just try to recall the original sentence." — [same]
- Monolingual transition: "After making a couple thousand bilingual sentence cards, we recommend making the monolingual transition." Then pitch accent, then output at ~80% comprehension. — [same]
- Provenance: "Matt and Yoga used variations of this basic technique for the entirety of their Japanese learning career." — [same]

**Refold [G, first-hand current text, read Oct 2026]**

- What Anki is for: "Anki creates the initial dictionary entry. Your immersion fills it in." Anki "does not… Teach you how a word feels… Give you all the definitions and uses of a word — it usually only teaches one meaning at a time… Replace actual time with the language". — [Refold, Learning words with Anki](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/learning-words-with-anki)

- Mining: "You encounter a sentence where you understand everything except 1-2 words… save the sentence as a flashcard (usually with audio and/or an image)". **Animecards (QM) [G]**

- "The two main formats are sentence cards and word cards ('anime cards')… anime cards are superior." - Hint field: reading disambiguation (武士 ぶし/もののふ etc.), fixed expressions, kana/onomatopoeia words, and "If kanji don't provide enough of a hint, or you've repeatedly failed a card, use the hint field… as a last resort." — [same]

## Key question 3: Community consensus on cloze vs recognition cards, card front/back design, and "what goes on the back" (AJATT comments, Koohii, Jalup, Discord-era guides)

### Takeaway

Across 2012–2026 the stable consensus is: **recognition over production**, **one target per card**, **native sentence on the card (front or back)**, **no furigana on the front**, **definition of the target word only — not a full translation — on the back**, with audio, pitch accent and an image as optional back-side extras. The live disagreements are (a) sentence-front vs word-front, and (b) J-J vs J-E definitions (resolved as "J-E until a couple thousand cards, then J-J"). Cloze is accepted only as a niche tool.

### Cited Findings

**What practitioners actually put on the back (first-hand card dumps):**

- ライトニング (13 Mar 2012), monolingual "vanilla" card: Front 「屁を放出することを放屁と言う。」 Back: the sentence with readings in 「」 after each word, then J-J definitions for 屁, 放出 (two senses copied), 放屁. "If there are multiple definitions, I copy both." — [AJATT Part 4 comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-4-the-active-output)
- 魔法少女☆かなたん (14 Mar 2012): Front 「地球が太陽のまわりをまわる周期は約一年だ。」 Back: full kana reading with ・ separators, then "the definition of any words that I feel I need. In this case, it was only 周期… I usually look for the specific definition that fits and add only that. For words that have about 8 million different uses like かける or とる, this can be time-consuming to prepare, but is more useful when actually reviewing." Also: "I tried using definitions I didn't understand and it didn't work very well." — [same]
- ブライアン (14 Mar 2012): "You don't [write your own definition]. You copy the definition from a (online/software) dictionary… Writing your own is far too error prone and too slow"; "You need to understand the definition, or else it doesn't do you any good later. (But, personally, I don't bother getting the readings for every word in a definition I don't know.)" He describes recursive J-J lookups (景気 → 売買 → …) and then "take that entire tree of definitions and make a bunch of cards out of them." — [same]
- アミール (14 Mar 2012): "I don't really make bilingual cards, ONLY when there's a totally new word… (although I eventually delete the English on those card after it's understood)." — [same]
- Matt Bonder (1 Mar 2012), bilingual MCD: "Front: 火曜日にクラスがありま#####。 On Tuesday I had class. Back: した… the English translation takes the place of the context." — [MCD Revolution comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds)
- 魔法少女☆かなたん (5 Mar 2012) on sources: "example sentences from bilingual sources tend to be…something to be avoided… not native quality… To all of the Japanese… learners obtaining sentences from Tanaka Corpus, Eijirou, tatoeba.org, et cetera, you might want to seek better (read: native) sources." JustinB had just been corrected by a native speaker for a spanishdict.com sentence ("No tengo dinero bastante"). — [Part 1 comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-1)
- KREVA's MCD back (Koohii, 11 Mar 2012): "あ・う / 会う （１） 集まってひとつになる． (対)離れる" — reading, then a J-J gloss with antonym. — [Koohii 9148](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309145250/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-9148.html)
- SomeCallMeChris (12 Mar 2017): two cards per note — "a context sentence, keyword in kana highlighted" (tests meaning; "a fail if I don't get the meaning very precisely") and "the same example, keyword in kanji highlighted, typed answer required" (tests reading); "an extra kanji card… if there's a second kanji spelling… worth it… if you intend to read novels." Notes generated from Rikaisama auto-import. — [Koohii 14428](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309101737/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-14428.html)
- cophnia61 (2017) settled on "sentence with target word in hiragana on front" to force sound→meaning rather than kanji→meaning. — [same]
- oregum (2012): "Most of my sentences have various degrees of English in the answer field, ranging from definitions to full translations. However, when I review, I simply glance over the answer field. If anything I look at the readings." — [Koohii 9148 p.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309145255/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-9148-page-2.html)

**Guides on the back of the card (2012–2026):**

- MIA (2020): definition of the target only; full translations "not recommended" after basics; audio on the back optional; picture on the back only, "especially… for nouns"; kanjify kana words the first couple of times. — [MIA JP Quickstart](https://web.archive.org/web/20201220134528/https://massimmersionapproach.com/table-of-contents/stage-1/jp-quickstart-guide/)
- Tatsumoto: back = "readings and dictionary definitions. Optimally you also want to add audio for the entire sentence and/or the target word, pitch accent information"; translation of the sentence "usually not practiced"; "Don't put audio on the front". — [Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)
- Animecards: reading, J or E definitions, word audio, context sentence; ideally sentence audio, screenshot, pitch accent. — [Animecards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)
- TMW/Lapis: SelectionText → MainDefinition → Glossary fallback; frequency harmonic rank for sorting; pitch position; picture for nouns; grading on meaning then reading; pitch graded leniently. — [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)
- Refold (2026): cards "usually with audio and/or an image"; JP1K back has native word and sentence audio, image, example sentence, and (v2+) isolated pitch accent audio. — [Refold Sentence Mining](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/sentence-mining); [JP1K](https://refold.la/decks/buy/fundamental-vocabulary-to-learn-japanese)
- Kumasensei [2nd]: English gloss of the item only, "not of the whole sentence". — [kumasensei.net](https://www.kumasensei.net/)

**Evidence cited for the back-of-card prescriptions:** none, in any guide read. Refold's research section supports spacing and incidental learning, not definitions/audio/pitch/images. Animecards' "review data suggests… 2 to 4 times faster" shows no data. Tatsumoto's "20 seconds per sentence card", "up to 2×/4× faster" for TSCs are asserted without data. — [Refold](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/learning-words-with-anki); [Animecards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/); [Tatsumoto](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)

**Recognition vs production:**

- AJATT 2008: recognition only ("Do not translate from English to Japanese"); MCD 2012: cloze recall (production-ish). Tatsumoto: "The AJATT method recommends using only recognition cards"; production only as kana→kanji writing TSCs. MIA: "you don't have to write out any part of the sentence, and we would recommend against doing so." Koohii 2012 (IceCream): cloze "not going to help you produce it in context". — sources above.
- [2nd] A Koohii thread "Cloze delete and recognition" (post 201427) exists; not read. — [Koohii](https://forum.koohii.com/thread-11439-post-201427.html)

### Inferences

- "What goes on the back" converged by 2020 on a field set (reading, chosen sense, word+sentence audio, pitch, image, frequency) that is driven by what Yomichan/Yomitan can fill in one click rather than by any argument; the 2012 commenters were hand-copying one dictionary sense and discussing _which_ sense to copy, which is the only part modern guides still debate (TMW: "Only the relevant meaning used in the sentence is ENOUGH").

### Gaps

- Discord-era (TMW, Refold, Animecards servers) consensus could not be sampled directly; only the published guides those communities maintain.

## Key question 6: Which AJATT ideas survived intact and which were dropped

### Takeaway

Survived in every descendant: immersion as the main engine with SRS as supplement; input before output; native sentences from one's own immersion; one unknown per card (i+1 → 1T); no furigana on the front; monolingual transition; delete freely; Krashen framing. Dropped or reversed: MCDs/cloze (dropped by MIA, Refold, Tatsumoto, Animecards); "massive context" (reverted to 3–7-word sentences in MIA, then to a single word on the front); RTK-first (Matt's "Recognition RTK" shrank it to 1,000 kanji, Tatsumoto to JP1K; Shoui did it after reading a book); hand-copying sentences; "total deletion bad" (Shoui deletes yearly; Refold says "it's the card's fault"); "keep adding" (Refold caps at 5–10/day, MIA at 30); the paid-secret model (Tatsumoto: "Learning languages should always be free", while Refold sells decks and coaching). Tatsumoto additionally reverses AJATT-era tolerance for dropping the SRS.

### Cited Findings

- Survivals, stated by descendants: MIA "immersion is the most important component… the SRS is simply a supplement"; "Matt and Yoga used variations of this basic technique for the entirety of their Japanese learning career" — [MIA](https://web.archive.org/web/20201220134528/https://massimmersionapproach.com/table-of-contents/stage-1/jp-quickstart-guide/). Refold: "Study primes your brain to notice and learn words; immersion is where the actual acquisition happens"; "Words aren't rare Pokémon" — [Refold](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/learning-words-with-anki). Tatsumoto lists Krashen, SRS, sentences and TSC as "Key aspects of AJATT" — [Tatsumoto](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/whats-ajatt.html). TMW: AJATT "still popular to this day… because of the mindset building" — [TheMoeWay resources](https://learnjapanese.moe/resources/).
- Drops: MIA renames i+1 → 1T and never mentions MCD — [MIA]. Tatsumoto: MCD "hasn't received much adoption… too difficult and complicated" — [Tatsumoto resources](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/resources.html). Animecards: cloze "useless or helpful only in specific situations" — [Animecards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/). Shoui: "I delete all my Anki decks almost every year" — [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/). Refold's cost model vs Tatsumoto's "AJATT's core principle of learning a language without spending money" — [Tatsumoto on Refold](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/should-i-trust-mattvsjapan-and-the-refold-site.html).
- The 2012 commenters already contested RTK-first: Joe4TheRecord (1 Mar 2012): "Khatz recommends doing Heisig before anything else, but that means you have to wait 3-6 months… I ended up starting sentences before I finished and I don't think it hurt me." — [AJATT MCD Revolution comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds)
- The 2012 commenters also already contested the grammar-free stance: Matt (9 Mar 2012): "It's actually fine to 'study' grammar, but only from a passive 'I need to understand what this means' point of view… Think of grammar… as just normal words." — [Part 2 comments](http://www.alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-2-the-awesomeness); MIA later formalised a "Basic Grammar" stage. — [MIA]

### Inferences

- The through-line that never changed is "one unknown per card from a native sentence". Everything about _how_ the card tests it (whole sentence → blank → bolded target → word with sentence hidden) changed roughly every four years, each time in the direction of less reading per rep.

### Gaps

- No source quantifies how many learners follow each lineage; claims like "many in the Japanese community have shifted to MCDs" (Jalup 2014) and "hasn't received much adoption" (Tatsumoto 2022+) are both unsupported assertions by partisans.
