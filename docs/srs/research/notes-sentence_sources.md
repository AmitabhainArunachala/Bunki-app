# Openly available Japanese sentence sources for mining vocabulary example sentences (as of Oct 2026)

Context: the goal is example sentences for 323 target words, mostly N2–N1 / news / academic, plus some literary, slang and onomatopoeia. The build environment can only reach github.com raw files (raw.githubusercontent.com). I checked this directly: raw.githubusercontent.com returned 200. tatoeba.org, downloads.tatoeba.org, edrdg.org, aozora.gr.jp, nlp.stanford.edu, phontron.com, kecl.ntt.co.jp, dumps.wikimedia.org, uni-leipzig.de, data.statmt.org and huggingface.co were all blocked by the egress proxy. github.com/.../releases pages returned 403.

## Q1. Licence, reuse, translations, size, register, quality and access for each source

### Takeaway

Two sources are both safe for an app and reachable from the build environment. **Tatoeba** (CC BY 2.0 FR, Japanese–English pairs, mirrored on GitHub) and **Aozora Bunko** (public domain, mirrored on GitHub as `aozorahack/aozorabunko_text`) are the only ones that clear licensing and network access together. **SNOW T15/T23** (CC BY 4.0) and **KFTT** (CC BY-SA 3.0) are usable if their files can be found on GitHub. Two kinds of source should only be used as a manual reference and never mined into the app: research-only corpora (JParaCrawl, BCCWJ/Chunagon/NINJAL-LWP) and copyrighted sites (NHK Easy, Weblio, massif, ImmersionKit, yourei).

### Cited findings

- Tatoeba files are released under CC BY 2.0 FR, part of the sentences are also CC0 1.0, and there is a weekly CC0-only download. — [Tatoeba downloads](https://tatoeba.org/en/downloads); [Tatoeba wiki: using the corpus](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/using-the-tatoeba-corpus)
- Tatoeba bans sentences generated by AI or LLMs. The "OK" tag marks a sentence a native speaker considered correct, and @check/@change tags flag problems. — [Tatoeba guidelines](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/guidelines); [Tatoeba tags](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/tags)
- The Tanaka corpus was compiled by Prof. Yasuhito Tanaka (Hyogo University) and his students. About 150,000 pairs were imported into Tatoeba in 2007. It was originally public domain, and Tatoeba moved it to CC BY in late 2009. — [EDRDG Tanaka Corpus](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/Tanaka_Corpus.html); [Wikipedia: Tatoeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatoeba)
- Tanaka quality: "large numbers of errors, both in the Japanese and English … translations did not match at all". Sentences were typed by students to meet a work requirement. Some Japanese is unnatural "direct, hard translations of English". — [EDRDG Tanaka Corpus](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/Tanaka_Corpus.html); [Tatoeba: guidelines for editing Japanese sentences](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/tanakaedits)
- Tatoeba's Japanese indices (word→sentence, with a "checked" flag for good examples) come from the WWWJDIC integration. — [mwhirls/tatoeba-json README](https://github.com/mwhirls/tatoeba-json)
- GitHub mirror `TheSunphis/Tatoeba-Rework` has 232,778 unique Japanese sentences and 280,520 English links with furigana, built from official exports and labelled CC BY 2.0 FR (some CC0). I downloaded it from raw.githubusercontent.com (13.3 MB bz2). — [Tatoeba-Rework](https://github.com/TheSunphis/Tatoeba-Rework)
- **My own coverage test on that mirror** (substring counts): 懸念 13, 措置 12, 把握 12, 是正 2, 顕著 3, 脆弱 1, 遡及 0, 逼迫 3, ぐったり 3, ぎくしゃく 0, やばい 22, ウザい 1, 黄昏 4, 儚い 3, 憂鬱 26, 慮る 0. Coverage is good for common N2/N1 words and thin or zero for rarer N1, literary and onomatopoeic words. — [Tatoeba-Rework mirror](https://github.com/TheSunphis/Tatoeba-Rework)
- `odashi/small_parallel_enja` is 50k Tanaka pairs filtered to 4–16 words, tokenized (KyTea), with English lowercased. — [small_parallel_enja](https://github.com/odashi/small_parallel_enja)
- OpenJLPT: CC BY-SA 4.0, says commercial or closed-source use is allowed, Tatoeba examples for 93% of 7,811 words matched by dictionary form, rebuilt monthly. — [OpenJLPT](https://github.com/evanclan/OpenJLPT)
- jmdict-simplified publishes a version "with example sentences from Tanaka corpus maintained by tatoeba.org" (from JMdict_e_examp.xml), under CC BY-SA 4.0, as weekly GitHub releases. — [jmdict-simplified](https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified)
- Jisho's examples come mostly from Tatoeba/Tanaka plus Jreibun. Jreibun sentences are written by professional teachers with professional translations. All 18,877 Jreibun sentences were on Jisho as of 27 Sep 2026. — [Jisho about](https://jisho.org/about); [Jisho forum: Jreibun](https://jisho.org/forum/63fdba73d5dda7188e000002-jreibun-new-example-sentences)
- Aozora: copyright-expired works can be freely copied, redistributed and shared, paid or free, and adapted. Permission is given in advance and no individual consent is needed. — [青空文庫収録ファイルの取り扱い規準](https://www.aozora.gr.jp/guide/kijyunn.html)
- `aozorahack/aozorabunko_text` collects every Aozora-format text from `aozorabunko/aozorabunko` cards zips, rebuilt daily, with paths like `cards/000005/files/53194_ruby_44732/53194_ruby_44732.txt`. Files are Shift_JIS. — [aozorabunko_text README](https://github.com/aozorahack/aozorabunko_text). I fetched Kokoro from raw (374 KB) and saw ruby 《》 and ［＃］ annotations.
- globis aozorabunko-clean: 16,951 works, CC BY 4.0, cleaned for modern-Japanese ML. — [HF aozorabunko-clean](https://huggingface.co/datasets/globis-university/aozorabunko-clean)
- SNOW T15: 50,000 manually simplified sentences with English translation of originals, core vocabulary of 2,000 words. T23: 35,000 more (originals from Tanaka). Both CC BY 4.0. — [SNOW T15](https://www.jnlp.org/GengoHouse/snow/t15); [SNOW T23](https://www.jnlp.org/GengoHouse/snow/t23); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SNOW-NLP/snow_simplified_japanese_corpus)
- JESC: crawled movie and TV subtitles; Stanford, Google Brain and Rakuten; over 3.2M examples per the paper, 2.8M in other sources; Creative Commons licence (CC BY-SA 4.0 per a secondary summary). — [arXiv 1710.10639](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10639); [HF Hoshikuzu/JESC](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Hoshikuzu/JESC)
- JESC GitHub derivatives: `yusugomori/jesc_small` (100k, tokenized), `DayuanJiang/giant_ja-en_parallel_corpus` (2.8M, MeCab tokenized, "。" appended, spaces replaced with "、"). — [jesc_small](https://github.com/yusugomori/jesc_small); [giant_ja-en](https://github.com/DayuanJiang/giant_ja-en_parallel_corpus)
- KFTT: Wikipedia Kyoto articles (NICT), CC BY-SA 3.0. — [KFTT](https://www.phontron.com/kftt); [OPUS KFTT](https://opus.nlpl.eu/KFTT/corpus/version/KFTT)
- JParaCrawl: not for commercial use, research only, and derived data falls under the same restriction. — [JParaCrawl](https://www.kecl.ntt.co.jp/icl/lirg/jparacrawl/)
- BCCWJ fees: ¥25,000 non-commercial academic; commercial ¥400,000 (2 years, up to 10 users) to ¥8,000,000 unlimited. NINJAL-LWP for BCCWJ is limited to research and education, and commercial use such as dictionary-making needs prior consultation. — [BCCWJ fee](https://clrd.ninjal.ac.jp/bccwj/fee.html); [NLB terms](https://nlb.ninjal.ac.jp/search/)
- Japanese Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects moved to CC BY-SA 4.0 in 2023. — [Creative Commons blog](https://creativecommons.org/2023/06/29/wikipedia-moves-to-cc-4-0-licenses/)
- Wikinews: the standard licence was CC BY 2.5; en moved to CC BY 4.0 on 16 Dec 2024 and ja voted in favour. WMF made all Wikinews editions read-only on 4 May 2026, and dumps remain downloadable. — [Wikinews 2024 licence upgrade](https://en.wikinews.org/wiki/Wikinews:2024_Copyright_license_upgrade/other-Wikinews); [Wikipedia: Wikinews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikinews); [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Wikimedia-Foundation-is-shutting-down-news-project-Wikinews-11249432.html)
- NHK forbids reproduction beyond what copyright law allows and forbids modification. Third-party tools note that the NHK Easy list endpoint returns 401. — [nhk-easy on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nhk-easy/); [japanese-vma issue #404](https://github.com/av3000/japanese-vma/issues/404)
- Weblio's terms forbid reproduction or reprinting without prior written consent. — [Weblio terms](https://www.weblio.jp/info/terms.jsp)
- Massif indexes novels and dramas and relies on "fair use" for small excerpts. Its author calls Tatoeba and 用例.jp "questionable quality/authenticity and antiquated sources". — [rsimmons/massif README](https://github.com/rsimmons/massif)
- ImmersionKit: free API with no key; categories anime, drama, games, literature; about 500k anime sentences with audio and images; no licence for data reuse found. — [ImmersionKit docs](https://docs.immersionkit.com/public%20api/search/)
- CC-100: Japanese about 15 GB; no IP claimed; users bound by Common Crawl terms. OSCAR: only annotations are CC0, and text is under Common Crawl terms. — [CC-100](https://data.statmt.org/cc-100/); [OSCAR-2301](https://huggingface.co/datasets/oscar-corpus/OSCAR-2301)
- Leipzig jpn_newscrawl_2018: 19,603,181 sentences; downloads up to 1M sentences "under CC-BY". — [Leipzig jpn](https://corpora.uni-leipzig.de/en?corpusId=jpn)

### Inferences

- In a Tatoeba-based pipeline, prefer sentences with the "checked" index flag or the OK tag, keep the sentence IDs and contributor names for attribution, and filter out Tanaka-era translationese.
- Aozora text needs `iconv -f SHIFT_JIS`, removal of 《ruby》 / ｜ / ［＃…］, and a filter against old kana (ゐ, ゑ, 旧字) before sentences can be shown to learners.
- JESC's licence must be confirmed on the Stanford page before shipping. Mixing CC BY-SA sources (Wikipedia, KFTT, JESC, JMdict) into the deck forces share-alike on the sentence data.

### Gaps

- Primary pages for Tatoeba, EDRDG, JESC, KFTT, JParaCrawl and Leipzig were blocked, so licences were taken from search snippets and secondary pages. The JESC licence version and the Leipzig CC-BY version are **unverified**.
- KFTT size (about 440k) comes from memory and was not verified.
- No GitHub mirrors found for: Japanese Wikipedia sentence extracts, NHK Easy, SNOW T15/T23 raw files, KFTT, or Leipzig jpn. The JESC full-file path in DayuanJiang's repo was not found (train.ja returned 404 at the root).
- Jreibun's licence and bulk availability are unknown.
- I found nothing on yourei.jp's terms or sources.

## Q2. Which sources are best for (a) news/academic, (b) literary/rare, (c) colloquial/slang/onomatopoeia

### Takeaway

(a) News/academic: Tatoeba for common N2/N1 words, plus Japanese Wikipedia (CC BY-SA) and, if verified, Leipzig news. KFTT is too narrow and JParaCrawl/BCCWJ are not licensable.
(b) Literary/rare: Aozora Bunko (PD, on GitHub).
(c) Colloquial/slang/onomatopoeia: JESC (verify the licence), then Tatoeba. ImmersionKit and massif are reference-only.
Expect to write original sentences for rare N1 words and onomatopoeia. A sample test found zero Tatoeba hits for 遡及, 慮る and ぎくしゃく.

### Cited findings

- Tatoeba covers common news/academic words (懸念 13, 措置 12, 把握 12) but has few or no hits for rarer ones (是正 2, 脆弱 1, 遡及 0) — own test on the [Tatoeba-Rework mirror](https://github.com/TheSunphis/Tatoeba-Rework)
- Wikipedia is CC BY-SA 4.0 and has an encyclopedic register — [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2023/06/29/wikipedia-moves-to-cc-4-0-licenses/)
- The Leipzig Japanese news crawl has 19.6M sentences — [Leipzig](https://corpora.uni-leipzig.de/en?corpusId=jpn)
- Aozora is public domain literary text, fully on GitHub — [aozorabunko_text](https://github.com/aozorahack/aozorabunko_text)
- JESC covers "casual language, colloquialisms … narrative discourse" — [giant_ja-en README](https://github.com/DayuanJiang/giant_ja-en_parallel_corpus); [JESC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10639)
- SNOW is limited to a 2,000-word core vocabulary, so it is a poor fit for N1 — [SNOW T15](https://www.jnlp.org/GengoHouse/snow/t15)

### Inferences

- Within the GitHub-only constraint, a realistic pipeline is: (1) Tatoeba-Rework JSON for words with English pairs; (2) aozorahack/aozorabunko_text for literary and rare words (no English, so translations must be written); (3) original sentences for anything still missing, especially news-register N1 words and onomatopoeia.
- Wikipedia/news sentences would need a dump from outside the build environment (pre-fetched and committed), with CC BY-SA attribution.

### Gaps

- I did not measure coverage of the actual 323-word list, only a 16-word sample.
- I did not test onomatopoeia coverage in JESC because the data was not reachable.
