## Key Question 1 — For an advanced learner, what do the best-regarded templates show on reveal, in what order, and what do they hide?

### Takeaway

Every current mainstream template (Lapis = TheMoeWay default; its forks lapis-modified/Kiku; jp-mining-note) puts the **word + reading + pitch** at the top of the back, then **one definition box** (the user's chosen "main" dictionary, with the other dictionaries reachable by click or collapsed), with the **mined sentence, picture and audio as "non-essential" context** placed below or beside, and **everything else (misc notes, extra dictionaries, source info, kanji relations, frequency lists) behind a hover, click or collapsed `<details>`**. Tatsumoto's AJATT-successor prescription is the same in substance ("the back of the card contains everything you need to understand the target word… Any information on the back should help you understand the target word and not be in the way"). The authors are explicit that reviewers should **not read the whole back on a pass**; the extra material is there for lapses and for the first learn.

### Cited Findings

**Lapis (donkuri/lapis; Ruri, itokatsu, kuri; current TheMoeWay default)**

- Field list, verbatim from the Yomitan mapping table in the README (25 fields): `Expression {expression}`, `ExpressionFurigana {furigana-plain}`, `ExpressionReading {reading}`, `ExpressionAudio {audio}`, `SelectionText {popup-selection-text}`, `MainDefinition` ("Something like `{single-glossary-jmdict/jitendex}`"), `DefinitionPicture`, `Sentence {cloze-prefix}<b>{cloze-body}</b>{cloze-suffix}`, `SentenceFurigana` (empty), `SentenceAudio`, `Picture`, `Glossary {glossary}`, `Hint`, `IsWordAndSentenceCard`, `IsClickCard`, `IsSentenceCard`, `IsAudioCard`, `PitchPosition {pitch-accent-positions}`, `PitchCategories {pitch-accent-categories}`, `Frequency {frequencies}`, `FreqSort {frequency-harmonic-rank}`, `MiscInfo {document-title}`. — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis) (n.d., repo current as of 2026-10-04)
- Order of the back, read from the template source `src/back.html` (top to bottom): (1) a top bar with the **`FreqSort` number** and a **`freq-dropdown`** that reveals the full `{{Frequency}}` list; (2) **`Expression` rendered with ruby furigana** (`{{furigana:ExpressionFurigana}}`, falling back to plain `Expression`) beside a **pitch display** (`{{kana:ExpressionFurigana}}` / `ExpressionReading` coloured from `PitchPosition`) and audio buttons; (3) `Picture`; (4) the **`Sentence`** (`{{furigana:SentenceFurigana}}`, else `{{furigana:Sentence}}`); (5) `DefinitionPicture`; (6) a **single definition box** labelled "First Definition 1/?" with `edge-prev` / `edge-next` tappable edges calling `changeIndex(±1)` and three stacked sources in order `SelectionText` → `MainDefinition` (id `primary`, "Primary Definition") → `Glossary` (id `glossaries`, "Glossaries"); (7) an alternate sentence slot; (8) **`MiscInfo` inside a `<details><summary>Misc. info</summary>`** (collapsed); (9) `Tags`. — [src/back.html](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/src/back.html)
- Defaults for what is visible vs behind interaction, from `docs/user_settings.md`: `sentence-furigana` default `"hover"` (values hover/always/off); `sentence-position` default `"above"` the definition box on desktop and `"below"` on mobile; `audio-buttons` default `"header"` (next to the pitch); `open-misc-info` default `"off"` ("Expands the misc-info box by default"); `glossary-separator` default `"off"`; `jitendex-format` default `"full"` with options `minimal` ("removes **everything** except the meanings"), `no-tags`, `no-sentence`, `no-forms`, `no-xref`, `no-img`. The doc notes that a layout "similar to Lapis-modified" is achieved with `--main-picture-position: "alt"; --sentence-position: "below"; --audio-buttons: "alt"`. — [Lapis user_settings.md](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/docs/user_settings.md)
- README on definition navigation: "You can click on the edges of the definition blockquote to traverse your dictionaries, hover over the sentence to show furigana, and hover over the top right to see frequency information." — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis)
- README on why three definition fields: "`SelectionText` – Use this when you want to highlight a specific sentence or definition from the Yomitan popup… `MainDefinition` – This is where you input the main dictionary you prefer… If you're new to Japanese or mining on your own, it's a good idea to start with a bilingual dictionary like JMDict. Alternatively, you can use your preferred monolingual dictionaries (such as 三省堂, 大辞林, 大辞泉...)… `Glossary` – This is where you place all of your dictionary definitions. We recommend having more than a single dictionary." Also: "If you decide you do not want to use any bilingual dictionary, simply use your main monolingual dictionary there." — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis)
- On the hint field (front, not back): Lapis quotes Animecards' four uses — disambiguating readings (武士 ぶし/もののふ etc.), words in specific contexts, onomatopoeia/kana words, and "Making cards easier… Use this as a last resort". — [Lapis README § What does Hint do?](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis)
- `MiscInfo` is for "any extra information you'd like. It will then appear at the bottom of the back of the card" (in the collapsed details box per `back.html`). — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis)

**lapis-modified (friedrich-de; the layout Animecards now recommends)**

- Design rule, verbatim: "The idea is to split the card's information into two categories: Essential: Word, Reading, Meaning. Non-essential: Picture, Sentence, Audio. When reviewing and passing the card, we only pay attention to the essential information. When lapsing a card, we use the non-essential information to remind ourselves. This allows us to review cards very quickly while maintaining high review quality." Changes: "The image no longer appears next to the word and is left-shifted… The definition has been moved below the word and reading -> It's essential information and should be higher on the card. Moved the audio buttons below the sentence." It also removes `ExpressionFurigana`, `SentenceFurigana` ("Furigana insertion is prone to mistakes and not needed. Make cards with native audio instead") and the four `Is…Card` switches ("not needed for a good workflow and clutter the card template"). — [lapis-modified README](https://github.com/friedrich-de/lapis-modified) (n.d.)
- Animecards' own page says its screenshots are "an outdated format. I recommend using either Lapis-modified or Lapis". — [animecards.site/ankicards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/) (author "QM", n.d.; scraped 2026-10-04)

**Kiku (youyoumu; Lapis-compatible, 2025–26)**

- Field list = Lapis's 25 fields plus `RelatedExpression` and `SentenceTranslation` (added in v2.0.0); `SentenceFurigana` is mapped to `{sentence-furigana-plain}`. Requires Anki 25.09+. — [Kiku installation.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/installation.md); [migration.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/migration.md)
- Back-side extras are interactive, not static: "Kanji Web: Explore your other notes that use the same kanji, same reading, same expression, or related to the expression itself" (needs AnkiConnect or a notes cache from the Kiku Note Manager add-on); multiple pictures/sentences/sentence-audios are **grouped and paginated** ("Each unique `data-group-id` will create a new page… `SentenceFurigana`, `SentenceTranslation` and `MiscInfo` fields will also be grouped"), with a "Merge Context" button that merges a second note's context into the first. — [Kiku features.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/features.md); [field-grouping.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/field-grouping.md)

**jp-mining-note (JPMN; Aquafina-water-bottle, maintained by arbyste; 2022–)**

- Default from the Yomichan field setup: "The above fields will create, by default, a basic **vocab card** in **bilingual format**, with all other definitions in collapsable fields." To make the first definition monolingual, change `{{~#set "opt-first-definition-type" "bilingual"}}` to `"monolingual"`; then "Extra bilingual definitions should be under `Secondary Definition`, and extra monolingual definitions should be under `Extra Definitions`." — [JPMN setupyomichan.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/setupyomichan.md)
- Dictionary routing on the back: `PrimaryDefinition` = "A dictionary specified by the user. Bilingual by default"; `SecondaryDefinition` = "All bilingual dictionaries outside of the one in `PrimaryDefinition`"; `ExtraDefinitions` = "All monolingual dictionaries outside of the one in `PrimaryDefinition`"; `UtilityDictionaries` = "traditionally-formatted dictionaries that… [do] not provide the meaning of the word" (e.g. JMdict Surface Forms); pitch, frequency and kanji dictionaries are routed separately. Example categorisation output lists 旺文社国語辞典 第十一版, 明鏡国語辞典 第二版, ハイブリッド新辞林, 新明解国語辞典 第五版, デジタル大辞泉 as monolingual; NHK日本語発音アクセント新辞典 as utility; JMdict and 新和英 as bilingual. — [JPMN yomichantemplates.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/yomichantemplates.md)
- What is collapsed: keybinds `8` "Toggles `Secondary Definition` field", `9` "Toggles `Additional Notes`", `0` "Toggles `Extra Definitions`", `[` "Toggles `Extra Info`". "Collapsed fields are collapsed by default" (a runtime option can force some open, or open them only on new cards, e.g. the example config opens `Extra Info` on new cards on PC only). Empty collapsible fields are hidden entirely unless `greyed-out-collapsable-fields-when-empty` is set. — [JPMN ui.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/ui.md)
- `AdditionalNotes` purpose: "useful if you want to write down even more notes, but keep it in a collapsible field to reduce vertical space… Recording the source where the scene came from; Adding custom notes on the scene's context; **Recording the sentences surrounding the mined sentence**. In general, this field should be used for any info that is not crutial to understanding the tested content." `PrimaryDefinition` "contains the main content, and should be the main field to edit"; bolding inside it is highlighted. — [JPMN fieldref.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/fieldref.md)
- Kanji information is a hover, not static text: "Kanji hover shows you if you have seen the kanji in previous cards or not… By default, it searches for the kanji within the 'Word' field, within 'JP Mining Note' types." Greyed results are from unreviewed cards; pitch is shown on hover within the tooltip. Word indicators 同 (duplicate), 読 (same reading), 漢 (same kanji, different reading) appear top-left of the reading. External links (jpdb etc.) are "shown as icons in the `Extra Info` collapsable field by default". The leech marker on the info circle "is only shown on the back side of the card." — [JPMN ui.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/ui.md)
- Pitch accent is **not tested by default**: "By default, pitch accent is not tested. To test for pitch accent, fill the `PAShowInfo` field," with `PATestOnlyWord`, `PADoNotTest`, `PASeparateWordCard`, `PASeparateSentenceCard` for finer control. Hints: "`Hint` … will show as a collapsible field at the front of card"; `HintNotHidden` shows it uncollapsed. — [JPMN fieldref.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/fieldref.md)
- Rationale for hybrid cards (front design, but motivates the back): "Sentence cards take a very long time to review, and can create context-based memories… The primary reason why this exists is to prevent context-based memories." — [JPMN cardtypes.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/cardtypes.md)
- Lapis's stated reasons for replacing JPMN: reliance on custom handlebars, "The heavy amount of JavaScript on the card caused noticeable lag on mobile devices", incompatibility with JL, auto-generated source, and the original developer going "MIA in the summer of 2023… no further development is planned except for bug fixing." — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis)

**Animecards (QM; animecards.site)**

- Back contents, verbatim: "An anime card contains… The target word on the front. The reading of the target word on the back. Japanese or English definitions on the back. Word audio on the back. A context sentence on the back. Ideally, it also contains: Sentence audio. Visual context (a screenshot). Pitch accent information. Depending on the situation, it may include: A hint field on the front." Review: "look at the word (and hint, if present) and [decide] if you know the meaning and reading. If not, you fail the card and review all the information on the back." — [animecards.site/ankicards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)
- The sentence is explicitly demoted to lapse-only use: "Anime cards address this by removing the context from the front, using it only as a reminder if you fail the card." — [same](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)

**Tatsumoto (AJATT successor)**

- WCC/TSC back: "The back of the card contains everything you need to understand the target word: Readings and pitch accents; Example sentence; Definitions; Audio; and so on." Review of a WCC after the first learn: "you see the isolated word on the front, recall the meaning, and if you are able to recall the meaning well then you move on to the next card without listening to the audio or reading the sentence on the back." TSC back: "it is enough to write the definition (or translation) of the target word… If available, consider adding audio recordings, pictures, or screenshots to the back of the card. Additional information helps you remember the target word better." Sentence card back: "readings and dictionary definitions. Optimally you also want to add audio for the entire sentence and/or the target word, pitch accent information and a picture if the sentence comes from a video." — [Tatsumoto, Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)
- Furigana rule for the back: "Although you don't want to put furigana on the front, It is **okay** to have furigana **on the back** of your cards. Any information on the back should help you understand the target word and not be in the way. At this point, inability to read the back of the card will hinder the learning process." — [Tatsumoto, Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html) (2024-03-28)
- Pitch on the back but ungraded: "Put pitch accent information on the back of your cards, but don't take it into account when grading yourself… To learn pitch accents of individual words, make separate cards." — [Tatsumoto, How should I grade the cards once I add pitch accent?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-should-i-grade-cards-once-i-add-pitch-accent.html) (2022-12-20)
- Pictures: "Yes, for concrete nouns on your SWCs… It's not that important for TSCs. Usually we make TSCs for words like adjectives and verbs." — [Should I use pictures instead of English definitions?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/should-i-use-pictures-instead-of-english-definitions.html) (2022-11-23)
- Sentence translation on a bilingual TSC: listed as item 1–4 "Sentence. Target word. English definition of the target word. Audio and image, if necessary", and "**Never** look at the English translation, ever… Reading just the definition of the target word should be **enough** to understand the entire sentence. If that's not the case, the sentence is not 1T." Exceptions where English sentences are acceptable: premade decks (Core10k, Ankidrone Essentials) and grammar guides. — [Should I put English translations on Bilingual TSCs?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/should-i-put-english-translations-on-bilingual-tscs.html) (2022-11-30)

**TheMoeWay / Shoui**

- Shoui's own back (Lapis with `IsWordAndSentenceCard: y`): "I put both the sentence and the word on the front. But I rarely ever read the sentence, it was just there for visual context". - What Anki is testing (guide, beginner section): "What you are being tested for here is whether you know the meaning and how to read the word. Everything else on the card is only there for aid." — [TheMoeWay Guide](https://learnjapanese.moe/guide/) (source: `docs/guide.md`)
- Pitch grading as an advanced add-on: Shoui "Started to grade my Anki cards not only based on the meaning+reading, but whether I got at least one (1) pitch pattern correct." — [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)

### Inferences

- The modern consensus layout for an advanced reviewer is a **two-tier back**: tier 1 (always visible, read on every reveal) = word, reading, pitch mark, one definition; tier 2 (visible but skipped on a pass, or behind a hover/click/collapse) = sentence, picture, audio, other dictionaries, notes, source, frequency list, kanji relations. lapis-modified states this split in so many words; Lapis implements it with a paginated definition box and a collapsed Misc box; JPMN with four collapsed sections and hover tooltips; Tatsumoto with the instruction to "move on… without listening to the audio or reading the sentence on the back".
- The direction of travel 2022→2026 is _less_ static text on the back and _more_ interactivity (JPMN hover/collapse → Lapis edge-tap pagination → Kiku pagination, Kanji Web, merge-context). None of the current templates renders multiple dictionaries' definitions fully expanded by default.
- Pitch accent is universally _shown_ on the back and, by default, _not graded_ (JPMN `PAShowInfo` off; Tatsumoto separate cards; Kaishi commented out), with Shoui's advanced-stage "at least one pattern correct" rule as the only grading-on-pitch practice found.

## Key Question 2 — What is the community's practical rule for J-J definitions on cards (how long, from which dictionary, simplified or verbatim, English fallback where)?

### Takeaway

The rule across AJATT → Tatsumoto → TheMoeWay → Refold is: **copy the dictionary definition verbatim** (never rewrite it), choose the **one sense that matches the mined sentence** (Tatsumoto allows all senses with the relevant one bolded), keep **English as a fallback only** — first alongside the J-J definition during a transition of weeks to a few months, then dropped — and **never try to recall the definition verbatim**; grade on the gist/meaning plus reading. The dictionaries preferred _for learners_ are 明鏡, 旺文社国語辞典 and 新明解 (simpler language), with 大辞林/大辞泉 as comprehensive defaults; 三省堂's "concise" dictionary was Khatz's starter pick in 2007. Jalup is the one system that _writes simplified J-J definitions_ (restricted to previously-learned vocabulary) rather than quoting a dictionary — but that is second-hand.

### Cited Findings

**Verbatim vs simplified; which sense; length**

- Tatsumoto, single meaning: "When a word has only one meaning, you simply copy the full definition to Anki." Multiple meanings: "While it's okay to copy only the relevant definition (1 or 2), including all the definitions is preferable as it provides a more enriching understanding of the word… you can use bold font on the card to highlight the part of the definition that is relevant to the example sentence… you don't lose anything by having more information on the back of the card." (Example: 大辞林 entry for 垂涎 with senses ① よだれをたらすこと ② ある物をしきりに欲しがること.) — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html) (2024-03-28)
- Tatsumoto's _contrary_ warning for early adopters: "Simply copying all definitions verbatim into Anki will result in an unreasonably long and overwhelming card. Instead, you want to carefully select the most relevant definition and add it on your card. If you're not yet at the stage where you can determine which definition is relevant… then it is a little early to start using monolingual definitions." — [Should I include monolingual definitions… even if I don't fully understand them?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/monolingual-definitions-early.html) (2024-01-22)
- Tatsumoto, multiple meanings FAQ: "`出す` has 8 meanings in `旺文社` and 29 in `大辞林`… You can either paste every definition on the card or put just the one that applies… Either is fine as long as the back of the card has the meaning that is used in the sentence on the front… When you review the card, **test yourself only on one meaning**." — [How to approach words with multiple meanings?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-approach-words-with-multiple-meanings.html) (2023-01-16)
- Shoui: "For words with multiple meanings, refer to the example sentences in the 研究社 dictionary. Memorize the definition that is relevant to the sentence you found them in. Importing monolingual dictionaries also makes the task of figuring out _what_ meaning was used in the sentence much easier. Only the relevant meaning used in the sentence is ENOUGH for Anki cards. You do not need to rep every single meaning." — [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)
- Shoui/TMW mining tip: "Highlight text in the dictionary before adding a card. This makes your cards cleaner. If your dictionary showed a lot of words, but you only wanted a part of that on your Anki card, then highlight the text you want with your mouse, _then_ press the green button." (This fills Lapis's `SelectionText`, which is shown first in the definition box.) — [TheMoeWay, Anki & Mining Setup](https://learnjapanese.moe/mining/)
- JPMN has the same mechanism: `opt-selection-text-enabled` — "If a section of text is selected, then that dictionary will replace the first definition. Additionally, that section of text will be highlighted (bolded)." — [JPMN setupyomichan.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/setupyomichan.md)
- Grading rule (not verbatim recall): "A definition is just one of many possible ways to describe the meaning. Don't recall the definition in case of a monolingual card, or the translation in case of a bilingual card verbatim… Having a general idea of the meaning in your head is enough. If the word has more than one meaning, don't recall all of them." — [How to review and grade TSCs?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-review-and-grade-tscs.html) (2022-12-26); and "Trying to memorize monolingual definitions word-for-word is a monstrous task… You aim at intuitive understanding, not conscious learning." — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)
- TMW FAQ worked example of "gist": for `きょうかつ [0]【恐喝】 ―する 相手の弱みなどにつけ込んでおどしつけ、お金や品物を出させようとすること。` "the gist I remember would be `弱い相手 おどしつける お金など 出させる`"; for 争奪 "`自分のものにする 奪い合い`, that would be enough to get the word." Pass/fail rule: "if the definition on the back made you understand the sentence better, then grade it as a Fail." — [TheMoeWay FAQ](https://learnjapanese.moe/faq/) (source: `docs/faq.md`)
- Khatz (2007 comment) on readers' unknown words inside definitions: "1. Add the definition to the old (current) entry, and…" (comment truncated in the archive). Reader Rokii (2008): "give definitions their own entries! A lot of vocab gets repeated in dictionary entries. Also they're a nice length," splitting 【使い分け】場合に応じて使い方・使う物などを区別すること into two entries. — AJATT, [How To Make the Transition…](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries) comments (2007-07-14); [How To Really Make the Transition…](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/how-to-really-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries) comments (2008-07-19)
- Khatz against writing your own English gloss: "trying to figure out an English translation… Don't bother with this. IMHO its damaging and a waste of time." — [same 2007 post, reply to John](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries)
- Tatsumoto on dictionary accuracy and example sentences inside entries: "You may want to grab the example sentence when making Anki cards. If you see a slash inside the brackets, the sentence comes from a piece of literature… They tend to be quite difficult, and you probably don't need to bother trying to understand them." — [Understanding monolingual definitions](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/understanding-monolingual-definitions.html) (2022-01-05)

**English fallback: where, and for how long**

- Tatsumoto process: "add **both** monolingual and bilingual definitions to make the transition smoother… Spend a few weeks adding both types… The downside is that this technique may lead to being too lazy to read the Japanese definition during review sessions… Try not to skip the monolingual definition when you forget a card. Sometimes people add an extra field to their note types just for bilingual definitions. Later this field can be removed from the note type, erasing all bilingual definitions in one click. Personally, I don't like adding more fields to my note type. I prefer tossing all definitions into one field, usually breaking them up with newlines." Final stage: "Don't add English definitions to your cards. Having both English and Japanese definitions is acceptable only during the transition period." Keep JMdict "enabled (but hidden)" in Rikaitan for words the J-J dictionaries miss. — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html); [How to make monolingual TSCs for vocab and grammar?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-make-monolingual-tscs-for-vocab-and-grammar.html) (2023-01-28, updated 2026-08-08)
- Lookup-time fallback: "If you don't understand the definition, look up any unfamiliar words within it. If you still don't understand, copy a definition from a bilingual dictionary as a last resort." — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)
- Tatsumoto's own trajectory: "All monolingual definitions on my cards had furigana. After some time I started to add furigana only for certain words. Then I stopped adding English definitions. And then I stopped adding furigana." — [same](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)
- Shoui (retired Monolingual Guide): "(肆). If you don't understand the definition even if you looked up all the words, just look at/use the bilingual definition (last resort)… (伍). It is okay to check the bilingual definition to check that you're getting the right basic idea… (漆). For monolingual Anki cards, just try to remember the gist of the definition… (玖). Forget looking up very concrete nouns such as animals in the monolingual dictionary, you are better off just using Google Images instead." He rejects Khatz's cold-turkey advice: "I just disagree with throwing away the bilingual dictionary completely, because you still need it in the beginning of the monolingual transition." — [TheMoeWay Monolingual Guide](https://learnjapanese.moe/monolingual/) (last full version at commit `02db051` of shoui520/shoui520.github.io; **retired 2026-09-10** in commit `7e763a6` "Retire monolingual guide"; the live page now reads only "Removed for now as it's outdated")
- Shoui's later position (2025): "eventually, I started using bilingual and monolingual together, because I felt like I already immersed so much… Even if I have bilingual dicts in Yomitan, I am completely unphased by needing to read a monolingual definition." — [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)
- Refold (current library page): "The transition is gradual, not sudden: 1. Start by checking the monolingual definition _first_, then falling back to bilingual if needed. 2. Add monolingual definitions to your flashcards alongside bilingual ones. 3. Gradually rely more on monolingual definitions. 4. You've completed the transition when ~90% of your lookups are understood monolingually." Tips: "Don't use monolingual dictionaries for simple concrete nouns… Technical and academic terms are also fine to look up bilingually… If you don't want to make the transition, then don't!" It also now suggests AI: "asking an AI chatbot… 'Can you please explain "this word or phrase" to me in simple Italian?'" Timing: for distant languages "wait until Phase 2C or even later into Phase 3." The heading is hedged: "Why It (Might) Matter". — [Refold, Monolingual Transition](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/monolingual-transition) (n.d.; redirected from `/roadmap/stage-2/b/casual-monolingual-transition/`; scraped 2026-10-04)
- Khatz 2007 four-step ladder: "Step 2… Put monolingual and bilingual definitions side-by-side in SRS entries. Step 3. Keep using sentences that have a translation in the base language… but only use definitions from the monolingual dictionary. If you are really stuck… look that up in the bilingual dictionary — but… go find and use the relevant monolingual entry right afterwards. Step 4… Go strictly monolingual." — [AJATT, How To Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries) (2007-07-13); and in 2008: "FORGET WHAT I SAID… Look up definitions of words you already know." — [How To Really Make the Transition…](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/how-to-really-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries) (2008-07-16)

**When to switch (thresholds)**

- Tatsumoto: "After you've mined at least 2,000-4,000 unique Anki notes from native material. When you know at least 3,000 words… Around 3-4 months after starting AJATT." and FAQ: "Once you reach ~2000 TSCs that you mined yourself. Premade ones don't count. Or if you know at least ~3,000 words total. The longer you wait, the easier it becomes." Against going too early: "you might end up spending an excessive amount of time doing recursive lookups." — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html); [When should I make the monolingual transition?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/when-should-i-make-the-monolingual-transition.html) (2022-10-26); [Is it best to make the monolingual transition as soon as possible?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/is-it-best-to-make-the-monolingual-transition-as-soon-as-possible.html) (2024-04-03)
- Shoui: "(壱). Read at least 1 novel (it can be a light novel or visual novel) before going monolingual." and "I started using monolingual… dictionaries quite early, after I finished reading 1 book in Japanese." — [Monolingual Guide (retired)](https://learnjapanese.moe/monolingual/); [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)

**Which dictionaries and why**

- Tatsumoto's short list "commonly recommended in the AJATT community": 大辞林, 新明解, 大辞泉, 明鏡 (+ 広辞苑, 実用日本語表現辞典 on the guide page). "`大辞林` and `大辞泉` are quite similar, have good definitions and many entries. Prefer `デジタル大辞泉`… It has an extra 120,000 entries and contains images. `明鏡` and `新明解` use simpler language and are considered beginner-friendly. `新明解` has fewer entries and a convoluted definition structure with redirections marked by `△` and `（）`… `大辞林` and `新明解` include pitch accent information, so you may want to import them first." On 旺文社国語辞典: "Advertised as easy to understand for people new to monolingual dictionaries. However, some definitions use harder vocabulary than `大辞林`." His scepticism: "Some people say dictionary `A` has clearer definitions than dictionary `B`. In reality differences are minor… Almost all Japanese-Japanese dictionaries copy each other… `新明解` is probably the only monolingual dictionary with notably original definitions, but it's harder to follow because it often backtracks." Practical limit: "Pick 3 or 4 monolingual dictionaries you like… Japanese-to-Japanese definitions are longer than Japanese-to-English ones, and it's easy to clutter Rikaitan popups with dozens of entries." — [Rikaitan, EPWING and MDX dictionaries](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/yomichan-and-epwing-dictionaries.html) (2020-12-13); [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)
- Why several J-J dictionaries are still needed: "Japanese-Japanese dictionaries have gaps… `夢海鼠` is only in `日本国語大辞典`. `禿同` is only in `実用日本語表現辞典`." — [Rikaitan dictionaries](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/rikaitan-dictionaries.html) (2020-12-13)
- Shoui's ranked list "for first time monolingual users": 旺文社国語辞典 第十一版, 明鏡国語辞典, ハイブリッド新辞林, 実用日本語表現辞典, 新明解国語辞典 第五版, JMdict (English), 研究社 新和英大辞典 第５版, デジタル大辞泉. Reasons: 旺文社 "has the most easiest to understand definitions. It explains the difference between kanji usages, 逢う, 会う etc. and is also really nice for grammar definitions. It is my #1 recommendation for people new to monolingual dictionaries"; 明鏡 "#2… easier to understand than dictionaries like the Daijirin and Daijsen, and also talks about the difference between kanji usage… really nice for basic grammar definitions"; ハイブリッド新辞林 "#3… based on the second edition of the Daijirin, but simplified"; 新明解 第五版 "#4… explanations can get まわりくどい"; 大辞林 第三版 "a great all-rounder and personally my most used dictionary"; 精選版 日本国語大辞典 "Not recommended at all for beginners but you'll love this when you're advanced"; 広辞苑 "sorts its definitions from oldest to newest, which is not useful to learners of modern Japanese"; 実用日本語表現辞典 "absolutely rocks! You need this!" He also explains the many-dictionaries rule: "there will always be some words that are in some dictionaries and not in others… in many cases you may not understand one dictionary's definition, but understand another." — [TheMoeWay Monolingual Guide (retired)](https://learnjapanese.moe/monolingual/)
- Shoui's illustrative case for why bilingual glosses mislead: JMdict 悔しい = "vexing; annoying" vs ハイブリッド新辞林 "失敗や恥辱を経験して，あきらめたり忘れたりできないさま。"; and 悪い ≠ "bad" (skill-poor is 下手). — [same](https://learnjapanese.moe/monolingual/)
- Khatz's starter pick (2007): "the Sanseido Dictionary is really good because it's a 'concise' dictionary. The entries are short and to the point, rather than long and encyclopedic. Which makes it great for a learner." Momoko (guest post, 2007-07-25) moved from the "wordy and confusing" Sanseido to a children's dictionary (チャレンジ小学国語辞典, Benesse) because "It has furigana on EVERY kanji… The definitions are concise and clear, and there are helpful example sentences for each entry," and she mines the entries' example sentences. — [AJATT, Great Starter Dictionary](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/great-starter-dictionary) (n.d.); [Why I'm in Love with my Japanese-Japanese Dictionary](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/why-im-in-love-with-my-japanese-japanese-dictionary)
- Defining vocabulary: Tatsumoto notes Longman uses ~2,000, Macmillan 2,500, Oxford ~3,000 defining words, but "monolingual Japanese dictionaries are **not** made for learners… the list might be too large (more than 5,000 words) and vary significantly between dictionaries." — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)
- Matt vs Japan posted a "J-J Dictionary Walkthrough" video on 2019-03-14 ("I won't be offended if you don't watch the whole thing"). **Content not viewed**; no transcript was reachable. — [X post](https://x.com/mattvsjapan/status/1106065234769268736); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D-AfT8XW63w)

**Jalup (second-hand only; site unreachable/defunct)**

- Search-snippet claims (unverified wording): Jalup "sentence cards follow the +1 principle, meaning there is only ever one unknown piece on the card"; "4000 of the sentence cards are in Japanese > Japanese"; "the definition will only contain words and knowledge you already have, and every single card's definition will be understandable based on your current ability"; "all definitions in the Jalup Intermediate will appear in dictionary form - if the sentence card has a conjugated form like 走らせる, the definition will have the base form 走る"; and where "common language won't appear in a standard J-J dictionary… Adam can easily make a definition letting learners know about slang alternatives." ### Inferences

- "Verbatim entry, one bolded/selected sense, gist-graded" is the operational rule; Jalup's hand-simplified definitions are the exception, and they are _authored_, not dictionary quotes — which is exactly why they need native review and a controlled vocabulary.
- The learner-dictionary preference (明鏡/旺文社/新明解/ハイブリッド新辞林) rests on three stated properties: plainer defining language, explicit kanji-usage distinctions (会う/逢う, 撃つ/打つ), and good grammar/function-word entries; comprehensiveness (大辞林/大辞泉/日国) matters more later. Tatsumoto's "differences are minor" is a minority dissent.
- No source gives a character or line limit for the J-J definition on a card. The implicit limit is "one dictionary entry (or one sense) as printed"; JPMN/Lapis push surplus dictionaries behind a click rather than trimming them.

### Gaps

- Jalup's actual authoring rules for simplifying definitions (who checked them, what vocabulary cap, how English fallback was phased out across Beginner→Intermediate) could not be read; the site is gone and Wayback is blocked.
- Refold's Japanese-specific dictionary recommendations and its older "casual"/"structured" monolingual pages (stage-2/b and stage-2/c) were not readable; the stage-2/b URL now redirects to the generic library page quoted above.
- Matt vs Japan's dictionary preferences are not documented first-hand here.

## Key Question 3 — What do guides say about synonyms, kanji breakdowns, and multiple sentences on the back: recommended, tolerated, or warned against, and why?

### Takeaway

**Multiple sentences:** tolerated only as _hidden_ context (JPMN's collapsed `AdditionalNotes` "sentences surrounding the mined sentence"; Kiku's paginated groups) or as the _source_ of additional cards (Tatsumoto); no guide recommends several visible example sentences on one back, and Tatsumoto explicitly says hunting extra sentences per sense is "not efficient. Don't do it." **Synonyms/antonyms:** no community guide recommends adding them; they appear only as dictionary cross-references (⇔ 対義語, [類語]) that come along with a verbatim J-J entry, and TMW warns that distinguishing near-synonyms is a dictionary-reading task, not a card task. **Kanji breakdowns/mnemonics:** kept off the vocab back entirely in the AJATT/TMW line (kanji are learned "along with the words"); the tool authors replace them with _lookups_ (JPMN kanji hover, Kiku Kanji Web) that show which of _your own_ notes share the kanji/reading — i.e. "kanji family" by your deck, on hover, not static etymology.

### Cited Findings

**Multiple sentences / extra context**

- JPMN: `AdditionalNotes` is where you put "the sentences surrounding the mined sentence", and it is "a collapsible field to reduce vertical space… for any info that is not crutial to understanding the tested content." — [JPMN fieldref.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/fieldref.md)
- Kiku: without grouping "The pictures will be paginated, while everything else will simply be appended"; with `data-group-id` "Each unique `data-group-id` will create a new page" and groups are sorted descending (newest first). The Merge Context button exists to fold a _second_ mined occurrence into the first note instead of keeping two notes. — [Kiku field-grouping.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/field-grouping.md)
- Tatsumoto: "the question field may contain more than one sentence if it helps… However, prefer examples that aren't too long" (front, from earlier notes); for additional meanings "you'll have to look for additional example sentences that express each meaning. This is not efficient. Don't do it. When a word comes up in your immersion that you **already learned**, but it is used to express a different meaning, then it's acceptable to make another card." — [Sentence mining](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/sentence-mining.html); [How to approach words with multiple meanings?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-approach-words-with-multiple-meanings.html)
- Tatsumoto on the dictionary's own example sentences: "Monolingual dictionaries typically provide examples for the words they define… You may want to grab the example sentence when making Anki cards" (as the front of a _new_ card for a defining-vocabulary word, not as extra back material). — [Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html); [Understanding monolingual definitions](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/understanding-monolingual-definitions.html)
- Animecards: "If we took the idea of needing Anki to memorize expressions to its extreme, we'd have to create a card for every word usage and collocation. This is impractical and unprecedented." The sentence exists "only as a reminder if you fail the card." — [animecards.site/ankicards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)
- Shoui: "Complex cards with full context and pictures are super overrated." (from earlier notes, [Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/))
- Khatz (MCD era) is the outlier in _amount_ of context but not in its role: whole paragraph on front and back, "just glanced at until you recall the answer." — [What is it about these MCDs? Part 1](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-1)

**Synonyms / antonyms / near-synonym discrimination**

- TMW FAQ, on two words with the same English gloss: "They have the same English meaning. English is not the best language to express Japanese… You need to use a J-J dictionary such as the 大辞林 to find out the difference. If you're not good enough to read a J-J definition yet then don't worry about it because you can also find out the difference through immersion." — [TheMoeWay FAQ](https://learnjapanese.moe/faq/)
- Dictionary-side cross-references that ride along on a verbatim entry: `⇔` = 対義語・対語, `[類語]` = 類語, `⇒` = その項目を見よ, `→` = 参照せよ, `[用法]` = 用法の使い分け (table of デジタル大辞泉 symbols). — [Understanding monolingual definitions](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/understanding-monolingual-definitions.html)
- Lapis `jitendex-format` lets users strip Jitendex's `no-xref` "cross references to other entries (See also...)" and `no-forms`; default keeps them. — [Lapis user_settings.md](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/docs/user_settings.md)
- Momoko (AJATT guest, 2007) found "the multiple synonyms and the way the word is used in the example sentence(s)" in a children's J-J dictionary enough to guess meanings — synonyms consumed at lookup time, not added as a card field. — [Why I'm in Love with my Japanese-Japanese Dictionary](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/why-im-in-love-with-my-japanese-japanese-dictionary)
- I found **no** TMW/Tatsumoto/Animecards/Lapis/JPMN/Kiku text recommending a Synonyms or Antonyms field. None of the three note types has such a field (field lists above). Kiku's `RelatedExpression` is the closest, and it feeds Kanji Web rather than a displayed synonym list. — [Kiku migration.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/migration.md)

**Kanji component / etymology / mnemonic information**

- TheMoeWay: "Kanji only make sense when they are used in words… kanji are not to be studied and grinded like kana are… they are meant to be learned along with the words that use them." Component study is offered as an _optional separate deck_ for learners who confuse shapes, not as vocab-card content. — [TheMoeWay Guide](https://learnjapanese.moe/guide/)
- Tatsumoto's fix for a kanji you keep misreading is more _cards_, not more _back_: "I make several TSCs with different words sharing the kanji (悠々 / 悠長 / 悠久)." — [I forget kanji readings in sentences, should I use furigana?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/i-forget-kanji-readings-in-sentences-should-i-use-furigana.html) (from earlier notes). Rikaitan's kanji-dictionary popups are described as containing "a lot of redundant information, such as duplicated tags, stroke order diagrams, and empty table rows," with CSS to make them "more concise". — [Rikaitan dictionaries](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/rikaitan-dictionaries.html)
- JPMN replaces static kanji info with a deck-relative hover: "Kanji hover shows you if you have seen the kanji in previous cards or not. This is useful if you want to check whether you have seen the reading in a previous card, to differentiate between similar kanjis," plus 読/漢 indicators for same-reading / same-kanji-different-reading notes. It cites Cade's Kanji Hover (readings/meanings in English) and Hanzi Web as related tools. — [JPMN ui.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/ui.md)
- Kiku's Kanji Web uses "Kanji decomposition data derived from KanjiVG" and "metadata and heuristics… derived from publicly accessible information on WaniKani and JPDB," earlier "WaniKani Userscript… for the database" — so WaniKani-style component data is used to _find related notes_, not to print mnemonics. — [Kiku README](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku)
- Kanji Koohii stories and WaniKani mnemonics appear in the community as **kanji-deck** content (e.g. the "All in One Kanji Deck" credits "mnemonics from the Kanji Koohii community with every story credited to its author"), not as vocab-card back material. — search snippets of [AnkiWeb All in One Kanji Deck](https://ankiweb.net/shared/info/1862058740) and [Kanji Koohii](https://kanji.koohii.com/learnmore) (second-hand)
- Tatsumoto's "How to do monolingual RTK" exists as a separate kanji-card workflow (page present in mirror: `how-to-do-monolingual-rtk`), again separate from vocab backs. — [Tatsumoto](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-do-monolingual-rtk.html) (not quoted; title only)

**Translations of the sentence**

- Tatsumoto: "You can also include the translation of the entire sentence, although in the AJATT method, this is usually not practiced" and "**Never** look at the English translation, ever." — [Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html); [Should I put English translations on Bilingual TSCs?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/should-i-put-english-translations-on-bilingual-tscs.html)
- Lapis has **no** sentence-translation field; Kiku added `SentenceTranslation` in v2.0.0 (grouped per context page). — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis); [Kiku migration.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/migration.md)

### Inferences

- The community's density rule is additive-but-hidden: anything beyond word/reading/one definition is allowed only if it (a) does not require reading on a pass and (b) is reachable on a lapse. That is why the tool authors converge on collapse/hover/pagination instead of forbidding fields outright.
- "Kanji family" information has been re-implemented as a _query over your own deck_ (JPMN, Kiku). This is the one kind of kanji information advanced users actively want on the back, because it answers "have I seen this kanji/reading before" rather than teaching etymology.
- The absence of synonym/antonym fields is deliberate in spirit (TMW: distinctions come from J-J dictionaries and immersion) even though no source states a prohibition.

### Gaps

- No r/LearnJapanese thread text could be read (reddit blocked); claims about reddit consensus on synonyms/kanji breakdowns are therefore absent rather than negative.
- Outlier Linguistics / WaniKani mnemonics on _vocab_ backs: no community guide addressing this was found.

## Key Question 4 — Are there any community or published measurements of review time per card for dense vs minimal backs?

### Takeaway

No controlled measurement exists. The community's numbers are all _front-driven_ estimates: Tatsumoto's "on average, it takes 20 seconds to answer a sentence card" and TSCs "up to 2 times… up to 4 times faster"; Animecards' "Review data suggests anime cards can be reviewed 2 to 4 times faster than sentence cards" (data not shown); and self-reported Anki averages of roughly 4.7–9.9 s/card on word cards. Back-side density is addressed only qualitatively (lag from JPMN's JavaScript; "skip the sentence"; "it's not there to be read").

### Cited Findings

- Tatsumoto: "On average, it takes 20 seconds to answer a sentence card." With TSCs: "When you read the full sentence, you can rep TSCs up to 2 times faster compared to sentence cards. This is achieved by using Speed Focus Mode… When you rep TSCs by only reading the target word, you are able to do it up to 4 times faster… You're still going to hear the sentence's audio (if provided) or read the sentence once the back of the card is revealed, but it's totally optional." Community guideline for total SRS time: "The most popular community guideline is 1 hour a day… The amount of time is determined by what card type (template) you use, how many new cards you add each day, your retention ratio, and how fast you can read Japanese." — [Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html); [Anki to immersion ratio](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-much-time-should-i-spend-srsing.html) (2022-12-23)
- Animecards: "Review data suggests anime cards can be reviewed 2 to 4 times faster than sentence cards." (No dataset, N, or method given on the page.) — [animecards.site/ankicards](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)
- Lapis on back-side cost: JPMN's "heavy amount of JavaScript on the card caused noticeable lag on mobile devices." - Self-reported averages (second-hand, search snippet of a Bunpro community thread): "5.53 seconds per card", "about 4.67 seconds per card", "4-7 seconds per card average", "9.87 seconds per card". — [Bunpro Community, How do you use Anki for learning words?](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/how-do-you-use-anki-for-learning-words/99666) (not fetched)
- Khatz's framing of MCD backs implies no reading cost: the context "is not there to be read – just glanced at until you recall the answer." — [What is it about these MCDs? - Shoui's grading rule makes the back's length irrelevant to time on a pass: "if the definition on the back made you understand the sentence better, then grade it as a Fail. If not, grade it as a Pass." — [TheMoeWay FAQ](https://learnjapanese.moe/faq/)

### Inferences

- Because every guide tells reviewers to stop reading at the definition on a pass, the community implicitly treats back density as costing time only on lapses and first exposures. That is why dense-but-collapsed backs are accepted without anyone measuring them.
- The only quantified claims (20 s vs 5–10 s; 2–4×) are about _what is on the front_, so they cannot be used to argue for or against a dense back.

### Gaps

- No Anki-stats export, benchmark, or forum thread comparing seconds-per-card for the same words with minimal vs dense backs was found.
- The Animecards "review data" is unsourced.

### Wozniak's 20 rules, as the community cites them

- Text (from a GitHub mirror reproducing supermemo.com's English list; original 1999): "4. Stick to the minimum information principle - if you continue forgetting an item, try to make it as simple as possible. If it does not help, see the remaining rules (cloze deletion, graphics, mnemonic techniques, converting sets into enumerations, etc.)"; "9. Avoid sets - larger sets are virtually un-memorizable unless you convert them into enumerations!"; "10. Avoid enumerations"; "11. Combat interference - even the simplest items can be completely intractable if they are similar to other items. Use examples, context cues…"; "16. Context cues simplify wording"; "17. Redundancy does not contradict minimum information principle - some forms of redundancy are welcome. There is little harm in memorizing the same fact as viewed from different angles… Memorizing derivation steps in problem solving is a way towards boosting your intellectual powers!"; "18. Provide sources"; "20. Prioritize… you can review pieces of knowledge again, split it into parts, reformulate, reprioritize, or delete." — [ninja33/sm-20-rules-cn README](https://github.com/ninja33/sm-20-rules-cn) (mirror of supermemo.com/en/articles/20rules; the primary sites were blocked)
- Tatsumoto cites the minimum information principle for: grading only the target of a TSC ("it follows the Wozniak's minimum information principle better"), 1T sentences, testing one meaning per card, and keeping pitch off the grade, quoting Wozniak: "If you consider an item that is composed of two sub-items, you will need to make repetitions that are frequent enough to keep the more difficult item in memory. If you split the complex item into sub-items, each can be repeated at its own pace saving your time." He adds: "Making another card **does not** contradict the minimum information principle because the extra information is added in extra cards." — [Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html); [One target sentences](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/one-target-sentences.html); [How to approach words with multiple meanings?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-approach-words-with-multiple-meanings.html); [How should I grade… pitch accent?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-should-i-grade-cards-once-i-add-pitch-accent.html)
- The AJATT comment threads show the same reading applied to MCDs: a reader (2012-10-13) asked "doesn't this violate Piotr Wozniak's 'minimum information principle'"; reply by 名前: "It is simple and minimum information that is being tested though. Instead of needing to know a whole sentence you need only recall a single particle, word or kanji. The rest of the information is simply context and definitions if you should require them." Another reader points to rule 9 (avoid sets). — [12 Free MCD Examples comments](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/12-free-mcd-examples) (2012-10-13/17)
- Khatz himself distinguishes MCDs from SuperMemo-style clozes: "Every other example of cloze deletions I have seen, online or off, would make for a bad MCD." — [10,000 Sentences is Dead, Long Live MCDs](https://github.com/all-japanese-all-the-time/all-japanese-all-the-time/tree/master/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds)

### Frequency on the back

- Lapis shows the harmonic `FreqSort` number permanently in the top bar and the per-dictionary `Frequency` list on hover; the harmonic mean "approaches what the word's _true frequency_ looks like." — [Lapis README](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis); [src/back.html](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/src/back.html)
- Tatsumoto: frequency lists "are shown as tags below headwords… Use these to judge whether it's worth learning a word." Recommended: Netflix and Anime & Jdrama lists. — [Rikaitan, EPWING and MDX dictionaries](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/yomichan-and-epwing-dictionaries.html)

### Word audio vs sentence audio

- Tatsumoto: "If you have added audio for the sentence, adding audio for the target expression is not necessary."
