## KQ1. What exactly went on the FRONT and BACK of a sentence card, and how did that change?

### Takeaway

From day one (Oct 2006) the front was the bare native sentence in kanji with no furigana, and the back was a "clue, not an answer": kanji readings, a gloss/translation early on, and — increasingly — verbatim Japanese dictionary entries for the words. By 2007 Khatz's own cards had dropped the translation entirely (front = sentence, back = J-J dictionary entry for the one target word, often with "definitions of definitions"). In Jan 2008 he flipped his Chinese cards to reading-on-front/text-on-back (a dictation/production card) and said you "definitely can" do that for Japanese, then in 2008 moved his Chinese cards to audio+image front. For Japanese he kept the "classic… recognition-type" card "because it's so time-cheap" (2009). In Oct 2010 he replaced all ~15,000 Japanese sentence cards with MCDs (cloze on front, clozed text + dictionary entry + source on back), announced publicly Feb 2012; in 2017 he reversed his own MCD rule and put furigana back on the MCD front.

### Cited Findings

**The 2006 template (bilingual stage)**

- The "typical question-answer (Q-A) form flashcard": "Note that the answer is not always necessarily the full 'answer' that you give, it's more a clue—definitions of words, etc." Example front 「これは例文です。」, back: 「これ　は（わ）　れい・ぶん　です。」 / "This [as for] example-sentence is. (PL3)" / "*This is an example sentence." Second example front 「お前は何者だ？」, back: readings 「お・まえ　は（わ）　なに・もの　だ」 followed by verbatim J-J dictionary entries for おまえ【御前】, なにもの【何者】 and めした【目下】 (a word from inside the first definition). "Observe the following things: The direction is from Japanese only." — [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/), Oct 2006
- Translations belong on the back only as a check: "early on, you will be using Japanese and English together (later, Japanese only), and you will need probably get translations of the sentences, so put those translations in the 'answer' field of your SRS; use them as a check of your understanding. But again, you just need to remember the gist of the translation (not the exact wording)." — [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)
- Kanji readings go on the back, one reading per occurrence: "Learn the reading of each kanji as it used in the sentence. So, rather than learn all the readings of a given kanji, learn the one reading that is being used _in this case_… Add that kanji reading to the 'answer' section of the SRS." — Khatz comment, 15 Mar 2007, under [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)
- The "(PL2)/(PL3)" politeness tags in his examples are his own invention: "I made them up (set them myself). The origin of the PL system is Mangajin's Basic Japanese Through Comics. Even if you don't stick a PL on all your items, you'll naturally pick up what the register of a sentence is." — Khatz comment, 24 Jul 2007, under [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)
- Front must have no furigana: "always feel free to read documents with furigana… Just make sure your SRS 'question' section has no furigana in it." — [FAQs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/faqs-frequently-asked-questions/)
- Starter packs 1–3 (Nov 2006) show the beginner back: readings with dots between morae, a word-by-word gloss in brackets, a natural translation, and notes on dictionary forms of verbs, e.g. Q 「道路で遊ぶのは危ない」 A 「どう・ろ　で　あそ・ぶ　のは（のわ）あぶない (PL2) / Street [in] play [business as-for] dangerous / It is dangerous to play in the street」. Pack 2 "focuses on one word, and contains several sentences using that word, plus some sentences that are tangentially related" (危ない ×5, plus 首, 橋を渡る…). — [Sentence Starter Pack 1](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/sentence-starter-pack-1/); [Sentence Pack 2](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/sentence-pack-2/)
- Starter Pack 4 shows the transitional back: readings + J-J definitions + gloss, with recursion: 「にしき【錦】立派な着物｡　(This, by the way, is an example of a definition of a word in the definition. This is one of the cool things about using Japanese only, there's this great recursion whereby one word leads you to the next」 — [Sentence Starter Pack 4](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/sentence-starter-pack-4/)

**The 2007 monolingual card (his real deck)**

- "Just to give you an idea of what life is really like on the streets of my SRS, here are some examples of sentences that I've entered over the past few days." Front 「ＩＴ革命において、主要先進国のナショナルフラッグキャリアは熾烈な国際競争を繰り広げ、」; back only 「しれつ【熾烈】〔戦いなどの〕勢いがはげしく盛んなようす｡激烈｡猛烈｡」. Front 「『夕凪の街 桜の国』」; back 「ゆうなぎのまち さくらのくに」 + the 夕凪 entry. Front 「人口に膾炙している、ヤクザ物とは違う、…」; back = the 人口に膾炙する idiom entry. No translations on any Japanese card. (Chinese cards: pinyin + a Japanese translation + Chinese-Japanese entries.) — [Real Sample Sentences](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/real-sample-sentences/), Nov 2007
- Khatz's own enumeration of back-side contents: "The answer fields are a combination of one, some or all of the following: [a] verbatim dictionary defs of words in the sentences [b] readings of the kanji in the sentences [c] verbatim dictionary defs of [a] [d] rarely, some other information (e.g. notes on what the key (grammar) point of the sentence is…" with example front 「害虫が隠れていそうな場所すべてに殺虫剤を撒きまくるのと、…どちらが健康にいいだろうか？」 and back 「〔終止形動詞〕＋のと)」. — Khatz comment, 18 Nov 2007, under [Does Input REALLY work?](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/does-input-really-work/)
- A beginner can have an English translation on the back only if it is authoritative: "You can have [Japanese question field] and [Japanese answer field + (if you are a beginner) an authoritative English translation from the source text]. You will, of course, want to move away from English altogether as soon as poss." — [Does Input REALLY work?](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/does-input-really-work/), Nov 2007
- Minimal back is fine: asked "if I leave out the translation in the answer part, what (besides from readings) do I put in there?" — "Nothing. Only put in what you need. If readings are enough, then they are enough… often, the English will only pollute it, IMHO." — Khatz comment, 27 Aug 2007, under [10,000 Sentences: Answers To Questions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-where/)
- The back is reference, not test material: "The defs are only for reference, I only look at them if I don't know the meaning of a word… So the defs don't always get looked at by any means." — Khatz comment, 20 Jan 2008, under [Chinese Project Notes 8](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-8-ch-ch-changes-stuff-that-applies-to-japanese-too/)
- Furigana for the whole back is allowed during the transition: "until you feel comfortable doing otherwise, I would include furigana/bopomofo/pinyin for the full text of monolingual definitions (i.e. those in the 'answer' section of your SRS) — after all, it's not like you are trying to test your knowledge of the answer section, so give yourself all the help you need." — [How To Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/), Jul 2007
- Ambiguous words get separate cards: "should the other meanings be added as other cards so you are more, fully aware of the words uses?" — "Yes. 😀" — Khatz comment, 1 Jul 2007, under [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/). Same rule in the scoring post: "one sentence, one meaning. Fix a context. So 'A bun in the oven', cannot refer to both baking and pregnancy. Make one 'card'/QA pair that's about bakery, and a separate card about babies." — [How to Score Yourself on Repetitions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/), 2007
- Pictures may be added to sentence cards: "a lot of people may be concerned that adding pictures to SRS sentence items… would make things too 'easy'… Adding pictures for sentences, and stories for individual kanji items, I think, is completely non-detrimental." — [Just Because It's Not Painful, That Doesn't Mean You're Not Learning](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/just-because-its-not-painful-that-doesnt-mean-youre-not-learning/), Jun 2008

**The Jan 2008 "flip" (reading → text) and the 2008 audio/image cards (Chinese; offered for Japanese)**

- The "How" post carries the note "Update: I've made some major improvements to this, discussed here", linking to Chinese Project Notes 8. There: "The way I had been SRSing Mandarin… Chinese sentence front, reading and meanings on back. I've flipped that slightly": front = pinyin of the sentence; back = hanzi text + Japanese translation + dictionary entries. "My task is to produce the Chinese text (and of course know its meaning…, not give a translation per se but just know what it means), given only the phonetic reading… I'm finding that the readings basically memorize themselves if I'm able to produce the hanzi given only the reading." "Could you do this for Japanese sentences/phrases, too? My first answer was actually, 'no'. But Momoko said 'yes'. And after trying it, I would say, 'yes', too… I would heartily recommend you try." Example front 「くさなぎ　もとこ／しんちょう：ひゃくろくじゅうはち　せんち／こうあん　きゅう　か　の　じっしつてき　りーだー。」 back 「草薙素子／身長：168糎／公安９課の実質的リーダー。」 Exception: personal names stay kanji-front ("中山廣幸" → 「なかやま　ひろゆき」). "Am I going to go change all my Japanese sentences in my SRS? - For Japanese he kept the original recognition card: "This is of course all assuming you're doing the 'classic'/'original'/'vanilla'/'recognition'-type sentences, where you are to read aloud and understand a sentence written in actual Japanese. **The MCD card (2010 private, 2012 public)**

- "The front contains the cloze (the blank represented by ＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃ or some other equally awesome marker). The back contains the clozetext (the correct answer to the blank…) as well as additional materials like definitions, readings, sound files and any other relevant reference material." Three types: "Bilingual" (front = Japanese with cloze + English translation; back = clozetext + sentence + J-E entry), "Transitional" (cloze inside a J-J definition of a known word; back adds the J-E entry: "The front of the card is monolingual, the back of the card is bilingual"), "Monolingual" (long passages from Yahoo!知恵袋 and Yomiuri, back = clozetext + J-J entry + source link). "Beginners, noobs and nervous nellies should focus just on bilingual cards… don't go writing your own translations. If you're noob enough to need a translation, ya shouldn't be rolling your own." "Focus first on bilingual and transitional cards. Get a few thousand of those under your belt." — [12 Free MCD Examples](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/), Oct 2012
- 2017 reversal on furigana: "up until now, the general advice (from me) on how to make MCD cards was that you should remove any and all furigana (kanji readings) from the front of the card… After several months of experimentation, I am pleased to inform you that this is not necessary — and that you actually want to leave the furigana there, right on the front of the card" (はんね／【半値】／定価の半分…), with base clozetexts "はんね 半値" exploding to nine clozes. "we separate learning (testing) the readings of a new word from testing kanji knowledge of that same word." — [A New MCD Card Format for Japanese](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/a-new-mcd-card-format-for-japanese-even-lazier-and-more-effective-than-before/), ≤ Aug 2017
- c. 2020 "MXD" multimedia card: "Pick some target text. Anything from one sentence to three paragraphs is fine. Make this the FRONT of an SRS card… Upload an audio file that matches some or all of that target text to the BACK… Find a word you don't know (well) in that text… Add a dictionary definition of the target word to the BACK of your card. Hide the target word (create a cloze)… Stop when you've done this for three words or get bored or tired, whichever comes first." Human transcription preferred: "Auto-generated (machine) subs are bad, avoid them." — [How To Turn a YouTube Video into a Permanent Learning Tool](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-turn-a-youtube-video-into-a-permanent-learning-tool-i-e-a-series-of-awesome-multimedia-srs-mcd-mxd-cards/)

### Inferences

- The constant across 2006–2020 is "the back is a reference clue, never the thing tested"; what changed is the front (sentence → reading → audio/picture → cloze) and the language of the back (gloss+J-J → J-J only → J-J or J-E depending on MCD type).
- Khatz never described a Japanese card with target-word highlighting; the "target" was implicit (the one word whose dictionary entry sits on the back). The 2012 MCD and 2017 furigana-cloze formats are the first to make the single target explicit.

### Gaps

- No post states whether his Japanese sentence cards carried audio; audio is documented only for Chinese (2008) and songs/MXD (2010, 2020).

## KQ2. Rules for sentence selection, length, sources, and reader-made vs native sentences

### Takeaway

Selection rules: pick sentences that "stretch your knowledge slightly" (i+1 by another name), that you "REALLY, ACTIVELY want to learn immediately", from anything native; never write your own Japanese or your own translations; prefer rigorously edited dictionaries and avoid unverified example corpora (WWWJDIC/Tanaka, Eijirou, Yahoo's [慣用表現] sentences); confirm anything heard with written Japanese or use the dictionary's sentence instead. Length: start with 3–6-character phrases, keep most Japanese items 10–15 characters with a hard cap of 30 (June 2008), breaking long sentences at commas/particles — a correction of his own earlier long examples. Early vocabulary priority: demonstratives, interrogatives, indefinites, particles, native action verbs, conditionals.

### Cited Findings

**What to pick**

- "Pick the ones that stretch your knowledge slightly, not so much that you're lost, and not so little that you're simply tagging 'です' on the end. One vague guideline when you learn, say, a noun, is to learn it with the verbs that act on it. Picking the ones you'd like to say or write one day is an excellent start… Go for what seems the most valuable." — [10,000 Sentences: Answers To Questions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-where/), Oct 2006
- Khatz endorsed the i+1 framing explicitly in 2008: "You're right — the i+1 principle, and by extension 'fail-point count' lie at the core of all this." He agreed with reader "rich" that "If you're a beginner, then everything is '+1' to you. So naturally, your sentences will be short. As you advance… your sentences can get longer, with fewer '+1's." — Khatz comment, 23 Jun 2008, under [Popping Bubblewrap](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/)
- "I'm always working to expand my knowledge, but only ever by one small step — one small quantum — at a time. Never too much, never too big a jump." — [Sentence-Picking in Action](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/sentence-picking-in-action-making-lemons-into-lemonade/), Dec 2007
- Interest is the filter: "Only pick sentences that are interesting to you at that moment. Only pick sentences that contain something you REALLY, ACTIVELY want to learn immediately. - Beginner length (comment, Mar 2007): "be sure to use SHORT sentences to begin with, don't overwhelm yourself. Something like 「テレビを見る。」 or 「夕食の支度をする。」 or 「慌てて登校する。」 is a good length." — Khatz comment under [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)

### Inferences

- Khatz's selection rules are two filters applied in series: a trust filter (edited native source, written confirmation, no self-made Japanese) and a desire filter (want-to-learn-now, delete freely). The i+1 difficulty rule is explicit but secondary; by 2008 he says even i+1 sentences fail if they are long or boring.
- "Native" for Khatz includes dubbed/translated media and dictionary example sentences; the line he draws is not "authentic vs. constructed" but "produced/edited by native professionals vs. produced by learners (including himself)".

### Gaps

- He never gives a numeric i+1 rule (e.g. "exactly one unknown") for sentence cards; "one thing at a time" is only stated for MCDs (2012).

## KQ3. How to review: pass criteria, reading aloud, writing, grading, failing, deleting, leeches, timeboxing, never resetting

### Takeaway

A sentence counts as known only if you can read it aloud in full without furigana, know every word's function and the whole meaning (gist, not exact translation); copying it out by hand was a fourth, optional criterion later narrowed to "write out the part you missed, each time you miss it". Grading used the 0–5 SuperMemo/Mnemosyne/Surusu scale with retention = score ≥3; he later told people not to grade harshly, to grade per rep, and to "take the top / move to the middle" when undecided. Deletion is the master rule ("If in doubt, throw it out", DADRA precedence: Don't add > Delete > Review > Add), applied card-by-card while reviewing — but never as a clean-slate reset ("Point deletion good. Bulk deletion good. Total deletion bad."). Timebox reps into 1–2-minute games; take addition holidays rather than stopping reps; "1 ≫ 0".

### Cited Findings

**Pass criteria**

- "In order to have learned a sentence you need to be able to do 4 things: 1. Read it in full, aloud, with kanji, no furigana… 2. Know the meaning of every word in the sentence. I don't suggest you over-analyze the sentence, but you should know the function of each part… You don't need to provide an exact translation when you give your answer, in fact, don't bother translating at all… 3. Understand the meaning of the entire sentence 4. Write (copy) out the sentence by hand… you don't have to do this for every sentence, but do at for as many as you can. You should do this on graph paper (one square per character)… By our definition, if you cannot do any one of these 4 things, then you have not learned the sentence." — [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/), Oct 2006
- "Do not memorize the sentences. That's too complex and too failure-prone… you will memorize the sentence just by seeing and reading it repeatedly over time." — [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/); "Understand sentences, practice understanding and reading them out loud with your SRS. Do not memorize them in terms of learning to say them by heart; it is too slow and too failure prone." — [FAQs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/faqs-frequently-asked-questions/)
- Remembering because you recall entering the card counts: "if you remember something. then, yes, it's a recall." — Khatz comment, 11 Aug 2007, under [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)
- Writing-out rule revised (Sep 2009): "Writing out the sentence (or some part of it, e.g. the part you got wrong) each time you miss it is enough*. In fact, it's ideal… there will be few/none that you can write but not read… *N.B.: Just so we're clear, this post is about sentences. For kanji, you're going to want to write everythaang out." The questioner reported "about 1 minute per card writing everything out". — [About SRS/Sentence Writing Practice](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/about-srssentence-writing-practice/). He admitted in a comment (22 Jan 2008): "I did not always write out all my sentences. I just did as much as I felt like" — under [Chinese Project Notes 8](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-8-ch-ch-changes-stuff-that-applies-to-japanese-too/)

**Grading scales**

- 2007 scale (Mnemosyne/KhatzuMemo/SuperMemo 0–5): "0 – Blank. Complete non-recall. You completely forgot the reading of 1 or more characters. You completely forgot the meaning of one or more words / 1 – Completely wrong. One big error or multiple small errors… / 2 – Slightly wrong. You got the reading of one character slightly wrong. You had a hazy idea of the meaning, and it was slightly off, or too broad/narrow / 3 – More or less juust right. Weak, struggling, but right. No mistakes. Maybe you had the meaning or reading wrong to begin with, but then you corrected yourself / 4 – Exactly right. Very good. Maybe a little slow or slightly halting, but not too slow / 5 – Perfect. It was a breeze. Like breathing." — [How To Score Yourself On Repetitions 2](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-srs-repetitions-2/), May 2007 (rewrite of [How to Score Yourself on Repetitions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/), which added: "As you advance, you might want to narrow your grading down to the part of the sentence that you were aiming to learn (since you advance gradually, almost every new sentence will only be partially new to you).")
- Binary core with graded shades: "a card is either correct or incorrect, but then within that binaryness, the 0~~5 scale gives uz a finer-grained way of explaining the correctness. 0~~2 is all incorrect, 3~5 is correct." — [Why The Way We Read Sucks, Part 5](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-the-way-we-read-sucks-and-how-to-fix-it-part-5-examples-shown-and-questions-answered/), Apr 2010
- Surusu scale (2011): "0 = blank 1 = totally incorrect 2 = partially correct 3 = correct, with great effort 4 = correct, with some effort 5 = correct, effortless WARP = … one or two orders of magnitude more powerful than a '5'… Ironically enough, most people grade themselves too harshly. So: Don't be too harsh… Grade cards on a per rep basis: if you did well on the card this time around, then give yourself a high score." — [How To Score Reps In Surusu](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu/); 2013 edition relabels "0 = total blank 1 = wrong 2 = mostly wrong 3 = mostly right 4 = right 5 = slam dunk" — [How To Score Reps In Surusu, 2013 Edition](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu-2013-edition/)
- Tie-breaking: "If you're stuck between two scores, say a 4 and a 5, take the high ground: give yourself the higher score. If you're stuck between many (i.e. three or more) scores… move to the middle… In most cases of confusion or uncertainty, I would just go for the (overall) midpoint score, which happens to be a 3 in Surusu. Think of it as your default." — [SRS Scoring Heuristics](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-scoring-heuristics/), Apr 2012
- Retention target and failure volume: SRS "promise retention in the range of 90-95%… allowing that you will forget 5-10% of what you learn, rather than being obsessed with 100% retention, has the counter-intuitive effect of leading you to actually learn more." — [What is an SRS?](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-an-srs/), 2007. "since New Year's Day 2007, I have done about 58000 flashcard reps with a retention rate of about 91%, where retention = a rep score of 3 or above… I have failed to correctly read and/or comprehend a Japanese sentence item at least 5200 times… if we… were to start counting from 2004, it would be about 100,000 reps with a similar 90-95% retention rate." — [Aim to Fail](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/aim-to-fail/), Apr 2009. Surusu defines "Retention: Percentage of reps where you got a score of 3 or more" — [Surusu Stats Definitions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/surusu-stats-definitions/)

**Deleting, leeches, "if in doubt, throw it out"**

- "Sucky sentence items… You'll feel it. The dread… You've gone your entire life up to now not knowing that sentence; if it really matters, it'll come up again… An item that's sucky is a weed… Delete it. Edit it if you really feel like it. But if editing feels like a waste of time, and for me it often does, then deletion is definitely the way to go… Remember, sentences is not S&M. If it hurts, then it's bad… Doing sentences should be like…popping bubblewrap." — [Popping Bubblewrap](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/), Jun 2008
- "Expect to have to delete sentences… it implies that you make 100% perfect item entry decisions on the front end… Typos, bad judgment, bad writing and misunderstanding do creep in… If in doubt, throw it out. Wondering whether or not to delete this sentence? That's an automatic delete right there… your SRS collection is full of leeches and duds that are too obtuse or too obscure or too long or too irrelevant… throw away most of what you marked up — 95% or more… 'if borin', don't put it in'." — [Secrets to Smoother SRSing, Part 4](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/secrets-to-smoother-srsing-part-4-collect-em-to-throw-away/), Sep 2008
- Weeding applies even to beginners and even to "essential" cards: "Even if you're just a beginner but you sense there might be an error, throw it out… Don't worry about false positives — there's plenty more where those came from." — [Secrets, Part 7](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/secrets-to-smoother-srsing-part-7-the-place-of-pre-mined-srsing-and-other-ramblings/). "Call it 'lazy processing': you can always undelete, or re-add the cards later." — [SRS Precedence Rules](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-precedence-rules/)
- DADRA (Mar 2010): "Don't Add, Delete, Review, Add. 0. Don't Add… If in doubt, don't add anything to the SRS… When something really needs to be added to the SRS, it won't feel like a chore at all. 1. Delete… There is perhaps nothing more threatening to your long-term SRSing prospects than bad cards… Doesn't matter if you would, could or should learn it. Delete it… I personally prefer to delete one card a time. I say, resist the impulse to 'push the reset button'… I virtually always do my deleting while reviewing… if you're doing reviews but you get bored by one or more cards… just start deleting and don't even bother stop until you hit a card that makes you want to do reviews. 2. Review… Click. Show back. Set score. Next. 3. Add… if in doubt, adding cards is the least important thing you can do." — [SRS Precedence Rules](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-precedence-rules/)
- Numbers from a purge day (Jan 2010): "Repcount: 135 / Added: 2 cards / Deleted: 100 cards. Total: ~235 cards processed, ~42% deleted… If you've got a really sucky deck, you could end up literally halving your cardcount — I once did. In the extreme, you could end up with only 10% of your original deck… Sentences are interchangeable. Motivation to learn is not… You can keep being liberal about what enters your SRS deck, just be liberal about what leaves it, as well." - Expected dud rate: "Accept that at least 5% and as much us 25~50% of the little that does get entered into the SRS, sucks and should be deleted." — [Why The Way We Read Sucks, Part 2](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-how-we-read-sucks-and-how-to-fix-it-part-2/)

### Inferences

- Design implications Khatz draws from his review rules: a card must have one short unambiguous answer; the back should let you self-check in seconds; cards that take long or feel bad are bugs in card design or selection, to be deleted rather than endured. His "leech" handling is deletion (or Surusu's automatic statistical culling), never ease-adjustment or re-formatting, with one exception — "Edit it if you really feel like it" (2008).
- The grading scale evolved from rule-based (which error type → which number, 2007) to feeling-based (effort level, 2011) to anti-perfectionist defaults (round up, default 3, 2012).

### Gaps

- He never specifies how many lapses make a leech, nor a daily review cap; Surusu's culling thresholds are not published in the mirror.

## KQ4. The monolingual (J-J) dictionary transition and how it changed the back of the card

### Takeaway

Khatz went J-J "at about 500-1000 sentences" himself and told readers with "a decent level of skill" to start J-J cards "as soon as possible" (Oct 2006). The July 2007 four-step plan (mix lookups → side-by-side definitions → keep translated sentences but J-J definitions only → strictly monolingual) was retracted a year later as "far too complicated" in favour of one instruction: "Look up definitions of words you already know." On the card, the back changes from reading+translation to reading+verbatim J-J entries, including recursive "definitions of definitions", with furigana permitted on the back during the transition; an English translation is actively discouraged ("pollute"). In 2012 the transition was re-implemented as "transitional MCDs" (cloze in a J-J definition of a known word, English on the back) and in 2013 productised as the 30-day MonoTran course.

### Cited Findings

- "Initially you can go Japanese-to-English, but eventually, you should start going Japanese-to-Japanese only. For me, this was at about 500-1000 sentences… You will probably have to do a lot of looking up, such that your answer area may contain definitions of definitions. That's super! Because everything you are doing is exercising your Japanese skills." — [10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/), Oct 2006. "I have a decent level of skill, do you think I should jump into the J-to-J cards? Yes! Absolutely! - Four steps (Jul 2007): "Step 1. Accept that it will be a bit slower to begin with… Step 2. Just try it… Start mixing your lookups in the bilingual dictionary with lookups in the monolingual dictionary. Put monolingual and bilingual definitions side-by-side in SRS entries. Step 3. Keep using sentences that have a translation in the base language (i.e. sentences from the bilingual dictionary, etc.), but only use definitions from the monolingual dictionary. If you are really stuck… feel free to look that up in the bilingual dictionary — but look go find and use the relevant monolingual entry right afterwards. - How recursive definitions go on cards (comment, 14 Jul 2007): "1. Add the definition to the old (current) entry, and 2. Add the definition (with example) as part of a new entry… [5 or 10 dictionary definitions per sentence] This asymmetry is normal, and even desirable." So part of the deal becomes: if you're not ready to understand a word's definition in Japanese, then you're not ready to learn it…but you will be at some point." — Khatz comments under [How To Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/)

"bilingual dictionaries definitely provide a false sense of security, a false economy of speed and a false idea of what words really mean." — Khatz comment, 14 Jul 2007, same post as above
- His own Chinese switch: "Today, July 20, 2007, I crossed the Rubicon and went fully monolingual in Chinese… I was initially doing 100 per day… However, it took too long per sentence… I haven't quite built up the mental strength to go for 100 Chinese-Chinese sentences in one day." — [Chinese Project Notes 2: Went Monolingual](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-2-went-monolingual/)
- Retraction (Jul 2008): "I know I've written on this topic before. FORGET WHAT I SAID. That explanation sucked; it was far too complicated. No chance of misunderstanding there, right?" — [How To Really Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-really-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/)
- Dictionary choice for the transition: Sanseido "because it's a 'concise' dictionary. - Testimonial (reader "Awkward Map", reproduced by Khatz, Jun 2008): "At the beginning it was perhaps 2-3 sentences per day (with maybe 3-4 hours available) with the monodic… Now on a good day spending about 8 hours working on sentences I'm able to put in 25-30 sentences… The 'back' sides of my cards are still friggin' huge, however, what with the circular nature of definitions." — [Reader Story: Three Months of Sentences](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/reader-story-three-months-of-sentences/)

### Inferences

- The J-J transition is the single biggest determinant of what his back side looked like: once monolingual, the back is purely the dictionary entry (plus reading), and the "translation as a check" of 2006 disappears entirely from his own cards by late 2007.

### Gaps

- The MonoTran course content is paid and absent; whether it prescribes a specific card template is unknown.

## KQ5. Khatz's later verdict: "10,000 Sentences is Dead", and which posts he contradicted or retracted

### Takeaway

Khatz replaced his ~15,000-card Japanese sentence deck with MCDs on 18 Oct 2010 (announced Feb 2012), calling the sentence method obsolete-but-not-broken ("was your cable connection 'broken' when you switched to fiber optic?"), and in 2013 wrote "10,000 Sentences is sooooooo 2007". Every original 10,000-sentences post was retro-stamped "@Update: 10,000 Sentences is Dead." He explicitly retracted: his long example sentences (2008), the four-step monolingual plan (2008), the "shouldn't delete essential cards" stance (2010), no-furigana-on-MCD-fronts (2017), and — by his own footnote — his "never clean-slate delete" rule, which he broke for the MCD switch. Positions that stayed constant: input before output, no self-made Japanese/translations, no furigana on sentence fronts, J-J as soon as possible, delete freely, fun as the governing criterion.

### Cited Findings

- "In the summer of 2010, I innocently started experimenting with a new SRS technique… On October 18, 2010, my sentence deck breathed its final breath, and the '10,000 Sentences' method literally died… actually about 15,0000 [sic] sentence cards died that day — in name and in form." Verdict: "Does This Mean That Ye Olde Sentence Method is Broken? Well, was your cable connection 'broken' when you switched to fiber optic?… No, you just moved on." - No post quantifies MCD results versus sentence cards; claims of superiority are testimonial.
