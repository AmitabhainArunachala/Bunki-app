## KQ1. What did Khatz say a good SRS card is, and what makes a bad one?

### Takeaway

A good card, in Khatz's words, has **one and only one correct answer, short, clear and concise**; is in the target language only (no English question side, no self-made translations); is short (one clause, not a page; but not a lone word either); tests one thing; is **easy** ("let it be too easy"); and is something you actually want to see again. A bad card is ambiguous (more than one right answer), too long or too short, boring/"should-learn", translation-direction (English→Japanese), typed-in rather than copy-pasted, or one you feel ambivalent about. He never wrote a post literally titled "keep cards simple"/"plain text"/"one fact per card"; the equivalents are the "one plausible correct answer" rule (2008, 2010), the "popping bubblewrap" length rule (2008), the three "If your SRSing is boring" causes (undated, late), and the "SRS Victory Formula" (2011).

### Cited Findings

**The one-answer principle (rule, stated twice):**

- "One key feature of truly useful SRS question/answer pairs, I think, is to have a very limited number of reasonably possible correct answers — preferably only one plausible correct answer. That way it's a good, 'fair', useful test. Translation does not allow for this. Plus it ties one to another language too much, which is not a good thing unless you're laddering…and even then." — _Chinese Project Notes 8: Ch-Ch-Changes_ (≤2008-01-19, earliest comment) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-8-ch-ch-changes-stuff-that-applies-to-japanese-too/
- "IMHO, one principle of good SRS cards is that there be one and only one correct answer, and that this answer be short, clear and concise (BTW lazy kanji cards kind of violate this principle to some extent, which bugs the side of me that wants a simple, straightforward game with a clear, unambiguous objective…but we'll leave that for another post)." — _Weird-But-Valuable SRS Hacks: Using the SRS to Remember Names_ (2010-05-29) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/weird-but-valuable-srs-hacks-using-the-srs-to-remember-names/

**"If Your SRSing Is Boring" — the three card/session faults (rule; whole post):**

- "If your SRSing is boring, then you're doing it wrong. And there are three general classes of possible causes: Card Content: The subject matter of your cards sucks — you're not into it. Card Structure: Your cards are too long (or, in some cases, too short). Other than for learning elements of a writing system, one-word cards are bad. Overlong, one-page cards are also bad. Cards with more than one possible correct answer are also bad: ambiguity is good and important in life, but not in individual SRS cards. Finally, drugs are bad, mmm kay? Session Structure: Your SRS sessions are too long or spaced out too far apart." — _If Your SRSing Is Boring, Then You're Doing It Wrong_ (undated, no comments; late era) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/if-your-srsing-is-boring-then-youre-doing-it-wrong/

**Short items, KISS (rule) and the "fix a context" rule:**

- "SRS sentence items. Yes they should be sentences, but you must kiss… KISS: Keep It Short and Sweet. Sentences, yes; books, no. Break up long sentences if you must… You might also consider incuding the original, full-length sentence in the answer section, for reference." Hard limits: "an absolute hard upper limit of 10 characters on my Chinese sentence items, with most items being 6-8 characters long… for Japanese… a hard upper limit 30 characters (kanji-kana mix), with most items being 10-15 characters long… Earlier in your journey… 5-10 character range." — _Popping Bubblewrap: Tips for Better SRS Sentence Items_ (≤2008-06-22) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/
- Khatz's comment under the same post explains the real reason is fail-points, not length per se: "the i+1 principle, and by extension 'fail-point count' lie at the core of all this… I personally had some basically i+1 sentences that were still too long (and therefore boring, for me)." (Khatz comment, 2008-06-23) — same URL.
- "one sentence, one meaning. Fix a context. So 'A bun in the oven', cannot refer to both baking and pregnancy. Make one 'card'/QA pair that's about bakery, and a separate card about babies. Better yet, give extra context so there is less ambiguity." — _How to Score Yourself on Repetitions_ (≤2007-06-06) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/

**Direction: Japanese on the front, no English question side, no self-translation (rule):**

- Reader: "is there ever an excuse to enter the question part in English and the answer part in Japanese?" Khatz: "No. Not that I can think of." (Khatz comment, 2007-08-06) — _On Input_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/on-input/
- "Do not translate from English to Japanese. Why? Well, because there are so many possible translations for a given sentence, how are you going to say which is right and which is wrong?… Good Japanese starts with mindlessly imitating good Japanese." and "The direction is from Japanese only. Initially you can go Japanese-to-English, but eventually, you should start going Japanese-to-Japanese only. For me, this was at about 500-1000 sentences." — _10,000 Sentences: How_ (2008-06-03 per INDEX; comments from 2007-03) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/
- "Sentence/definition and sentence/translation are both OK, by the way. Although, of course you should move away from sentence/translation ASAP." (Khatz comment, 2007-08-28) — _How to Score Yourself on Repetitions_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/
- The one sanctioned "reversed" direction is **reading → text** (a dictation card), not English → Japanese: front = kana/pinyin, back = the actual text + definitions. "My task is to produce the Chinese text (and of course know its meaning… not give a translation per se but just know what it means), given only the phonetic reading… I'm finding that the readings basically memorize themselves if I'm able to produce the hanzi given only the reading." Japanese example: FRONT 「くさなぎ　もとこ／しんちょう：ひゃくろくじゅうはち　せんち／こうあん　きゅう　か　の　じっしつてき　りーだー。」 BACK 「草薙素子／身長：168糎／公安９課の実質的リーダー。」 Exception: personal names go kanji-front, kana-back because "The number of plausible kanji variations for names like Hiroyuki… runs into the dozens and hundreds." — _Chinese Project Notes 8_ (≤2008-01-19) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-8-ch-ch-changes-stuff-that-applies-to-japanese-too/
- 2008 image/audio variant: "associating a specific image — one or more actual cartoons or photographs — with each SRS item"; QUESTION = image + [Audio]; ANSWER = text ("my task is to write this out correctly from hearing the audio") + romanization "for confirmation of audio… I do not put this in the answer due to not wishing to depend on it as a visual cue that does not exist in actual Chinese". Note on length: "this text is on the long side; you generally want to keep things shortish, even as you get better". — _Chinese Project Notes 11: Constant Improvement, SRS Image Hack_ (≤2008-06-01) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-11-constant-improvement-srs-image-hack/ ; Khatz in comments: "I wouldn't say go retrofit all your SRS items… All my new items are photo-items. I do retrofit items I fail on."

**The back of the card = dictionary matter, readings; "the answer is a clue":**

- "Note that the answer is not always necessarily the full 'answer' that you give, it's more a clue—definitions of words, etc." Sample: FRONT 「お前は何者だ？」 BACK 「お・まえ　は（わ）　なに・もの　だ」 + J-J entries for おまえ【御前】, なにもの【何者】, めした【目下】. "You will probably have to do a lot of looking up, such that your answer area may contain definitions of definitions. That's super!" — _10,000 Sentences: How_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/
- Furigana on the answer side is fine: "I would include furigana/bopomofo/pinyin for the full text of monolingual definitions (i.e. those in the 'answer' section of your SRS) — after all, it's not like you are trying to test your knowledge of the answer section, so give yourself all the help you need." — _How To Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries_ (≤2007-07-13) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/

**Easy cards / "let it be too easy" (rule, repeated):**

- "Pick low-hanging fruit / Eat it / Grow stronger / Climb higher / Pick higher low-hanging fruit / Return to (2) / Let it be too easy. Let life be easy." — _The SRS Victory Formula_ (≤2011-11-03) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/the-srs-victory-formula-srs-formula-victoria-p/
- "Doing sentences should be like…popping bubblewrap. Requiring conscious effort, while being relatively easy and SUPER satisfying… Doing sentences should make you feel like doing other sentences. If it doesn't, then be aware that the fault probably lies neither with you nor with the language in question, but in individual items causing you dread." — _Popping Bubblewrap_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/
- "While (I have read) having to work harder for a memory strengthens it, having to work too hard just makes you not want to practice at all… I think the spacing of repetitions provides enough 'strain'… we needn't add more. Also, in my own personal experience, struggle is not necessary to make a memory stick." — _Lazy Kanji Cards_ (2010-03-20) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/lazy-kanji-cards-a-new-srs-card-format/
- "telling yourself to do something hard is the same thing as telling yourself to do nothing at all… The choice is not all or nothing: it's easy or nothing." — _Do Something Easy, Or Nothing At All. There Is No Hard._ (2011-06-12) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/do-something-easy-or-nothing-at-all-there-is-no-hard/

**Bad cards: "shoulders", ambivalence, past-fun cards (riff → rule):**

- "They pick things they 'should'; things that are hard and boring and look erudite and respectable. They 'should all over themselves'… They pick things that are fun now, but hang onto them even after they're not fun any more… Repping bad content is the chore, not the SRS itself… Fun is not in the what of SRS but in the how of SRS: with fun content in a fun way. SRS itself, the tool, is largely neutral." — _SRS and Self-Determination_ (≤2013-07-02) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-and-self-determination/
- "You're probably overloaded with 'should-learn' sentences — 'shoulders', like I was in Cantonese. Or maybe you have cool sentences, but they lack the punch they had when you entered them. Those are now 'shoulders', too." — _When You Just Don't Feel Like Doing Sentence Reps Any More…_ (2010-01-23) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/i-just-dont-feel-like-doing-sentence-reps-any-more-dude/
- Leech definition (KhatzuMemo era): "sometimes your performance on an item is so bad, that it's actually just wasting your time; this usually happens when you've added an item that is too long, too complex, or not well thought out enough (remember that an SRS is not a substitute for learning or memory techniques, it'll help you to not forget, but only if you've gotten — remembered — the item in the first place). Such items are called 'leeches' in SuperMemo terminology. In KhatzuMemo you now get a warning about them, and some suggestions as to what to do [delete or rethink]." — _KhatzuMemo Update — Item Warning, Swap QA et. al_ (≤2008-07-08) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/khatzumemo-update-item-warning-swap-item-et-al/

**Friction: copy-paste, no typing, no intermediate notebooks (rule):**

- "Sentence-picking is not a typing exercise. Reduce your typing load as far as possible. Software dictionaries allow you to copy and paste… As far as possible enter directly into your SRS… if you can't be bothered to put that sentence into the SRS, then it wasn't worth it in the first place." — _How To Banish Boredom from Sentence-Mining (Sentence-Picking)_ (≤2007-08-31) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-banish-boredom-from-sentence-mining-sentence-picking/
- Lazy Kanji rationale: "It's easy to make — it's pure copy-paste". — _Lazy Kanji Cards_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/lazy-kanji-cards-a-new-srs-card-format/

**Other card types he documented (what goes where):**

- Name cards: front = salient features ("Big nose. Incredibly loud laugh. Dad looks like Don Knotts."), back = "TANAKA Taro"; "the name… is only allowed on the back"; grouped into decks like "rolodex-frisbee". — _Weird-But-Valuable SRS Hacks_ (2010-05-29) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/weird-but-valuable-srs-hacks-using-the-srs-to-remember-names/
- Song cards: split the mp3 into "10~30-second clips with ~5 seconds of backward overlap"; front = "one line or less of the lyrics"; back = audio clip + lyrics of the whole clip; "The task is to read aloud or sing the line". Sample back includes a J-J/J-C dictionary gloss (jìshì【既是】…). "Throwing the entire song in there is (1) boring and (2) defeats the purpose of even having an SRS." — _Learning Songs Using the SRS: My Current Method_ (≤2010-03-24) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/learning-songs-using-the-srs-my-current-method/
- 2020 multimedia MCD/"MXD" recipe: convert a YouTube video to audio, "Split the audio file into 30-second segments", "Pick a small (30- to 90-second) segment of the video to focus on", get a human transcript (auto subs "are bad, avoid them"), "Pick some target text. Anything from one sentence to three paragraphs is fine. Make this the FRONT", audio on the BACK, "Add a dictionary definition of the target word to the BACK… Hide the target word (create a cloze)… Stop when you've done this for three words or get bored or tired, whichever comes first." — _How To Turn a YouTube Video into a Permanent Learning Tool_ (undated; cites a 2019 video; post ID after 2020-05) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-turn-a-youtube-video-into-a-permanent-learning-tool-i-e-a-series-of-awesome-multimedia-srs-mcd-mxd-cards/

### Inferences

- Khatz's "simple card" doctrine is really three rules stacked: (1) one unambiguous short answer; (2) target language only on the question side, dictionary material on the answer side; (3) emotional fit ("you want to see it"). Length limits and the later MCD cloze are both instruments for rule (1) — minimise fail-points per card.
- "Plain text" as an aesthetic was never argued for; in fact he added images/audio (2008) and audio clips (2010, 2020). What he rejected was _cost_ (typing, retrofitting, intermediate notebooks), not media.

### Gaps

- No post uses the phrases "one fact per card" or "minimum information principle"; the closest are the quotes above. Hints: the only explicit discussion of hints is the grading heuristic (see KQ2); Khatz never said whether to put hints on the front, except that Heisig-style story/keyword hints on kanji fronts are OK (KQ3).

## KQ2. How did he grade, fail, delete, and keep reviews short? What about English on cards and hints?

### Takeaway

Grading: 0–5 (SuperMemo/Mnemosyne/KhatzuMemo/Surusu), with 3 = "weak, struggling, but right"; retention counted as "score of 3 or above"; **grade per rep, not per card history; "most people grade themselves too harshly"**; when torn between two scores "take the top", between many "move to the middle" (default 3). Hints: if you needed the hint, use the same heuristic — don't agonise. Deletion: "If in doubt, throw it out"; delete _while_ reviewing; delete one card at a time; delete up to 90–99% but never the whole deck ("Point deletion good. Bulk deletion good. Total deletion bad."); "Delete, don't edit" (editing is allowed "if you really feel like it", but "if editing feels like a waste of time, and for me it often does, then deletion is definitely the way to go"); even kanji cards may be deleted. Leeches: delete or rethink; Surusu auto-trashes leeches ("decimation", ≤1% of a deck per pass) and can "cull" up to 10% of untouched cards. Session length: timeboxes of 2 min / 90–120 s / 15 min / 30 min; "SRS holidays" = stop adding, keep repping; ≈90 min/day of conscious study at most, in dozens of sprints (2011).

### Cited Findings

**Grading scales (rule):**

- First scale: "0: You had no idea whatsoever… 1: You had an idea, but were completely wrong… 2: You just missed it. You were slightly off… 3: You got just got by on this one. You knew what the sentence meant. Maybe you even initially gave the wrong reading but then changed your mind to the right one… 4: You knew exactly what the sentence meant. You read it out correctly… BUT… you had some doubt… 5: … cakewalk." Also: "As you advance, you might want to narrow your grading down to the part of the sentence that you were aiming to learn (since you advance gradually, almost every new sentence will only be partially new to you)." — _How to Score Yourself on Repetitions_ (≤2007-06-06) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/
- Re-worded scale: "0 – Blank. Complete non-recall… 1 – Completely wrong. One big error or multiple small errors… I think a tone error in Chinese should be graded as 'completely wrong' rather than 'slightly wrong'. Tone matters that much. 2 – Slightly wrong. You got the reading of one character slightly wrong… 3 – More or less juust right. Weak, struggling, but right. No mistakes. Maybe you had the meaning or reading wrong to begin with, but then you corrected yourself 4 – Exactly right. Very good. Maybe a little slow… 5 – Perfect. It was a breeze. Like breathing. In. Out. Next!" — _How To Score Yourself On Repetitions 2_ (≤2007-05-24) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-srs-repetitions-2/
- Surusu scale (2011): "0 = blank / 1 = totally incorrect / 2 = partially correct / 3 = correct, with great effort / 4 = correct, with some effort / 5 = correct, effortless / WARP = Out-of-this-world ballin'… WARP is generally about one or two orders of magnitude more powerful than a '5'. Use with great care." Then: "Ironically enough, most people grade themselves too harshly. So: Don't be too harsh 😀 Don't grade yourself harshly on a card because you've had trouble with it in the past. Grade cards on a per rep basis: if you did well on the card this time around, then give yourself a high score. Surusu knows the card's history and won't boot it into oblivion just because you did well on it once or twice despite a troubled history." — _How To Score Reps In Surusu_ (≤2011-07-27) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu/ ; 2013 re-labelling: "0 = total blank / 1 = wrong / 2 = mostly wrong / 3 = mostly right / 4 = right / 5 = slam dunk / WARP" with the same "don't be too harsh" paragraph. — _How To Score Reps In Surusu, 2013 Edition_ (≤2013-08-04) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu-2013-edition/
- A slow recall that "required some figuring out" = "3" (Khatz comment, 2007-08-11). "As long as you recall it from your own memory, that counts." — _10,000 Sentences: How_ — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/
- Retention definition: "retention = a rep score of 3 or above" — _Aim to Fail_ (≤2009-04-02) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/aim-to-fail/ ; Surusu stats: "Recall: Percentage of reps where you got a score of 3 or more… Retention: Percentage of reps where you got a score of 3 or more (with the added assumption that virgin cards count as perfect retention…)" — _Surusu Stats Definitions_ (≤2011-08-04) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/surusu-stats-definitions/

**On-the-fence grading and hints (rule):**

- Two reader questions quoted (the second from Mariah: "what do you rank it if you have to use the hint?… if you can't remember it at all and then read the hint and get it completely correct, would you still rate it as a zero?… I feel like I might be grading myself too harshly"). Khatz's answer: "Time wasted on decisions is the worst kind… If you're stuck between two scores, say a 4 and a 5, take the high ground: give yourself the higher score. If you're stuck between many (i.e. three or more) scores… move to the middle… Two? Take the top. Many? Move to middle… Score and move on… PPS: In most cases of confusion or uncertainty, I would just go for the (overall) midpoint score, which happens to be a 3 in Surusu. Think of it as your default." — _SRS Scoring Heuristics_ (2012-04-15) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-scoring-heuristics/
- Kanji hints are not cheating: "Isn't it cheating to give the mnemonic story in the question? Not really, because you still have to reproduce the entire kanji from memory… I recommend you do it." — _How To Learn and Review Kanji Using an SRS_ (≤2008-03-16; one of the earliest posts) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-learn-kanji-using-an-srs/ ; and "I remind them that Heisig said it was OK to give yourself the keywords and story as a hint" — _Well, Do Kanji Your Way Then…_ (2010-08-07) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/do-kanji-your-way-then/

**Failing is the point (riff with numbers):**

- "since New Year's Day 2007, I have done about 58000 flashcard reps with a retention rate of about 91%… I have failed to correctly read and/or comprehend a Japanese sentence item at least 5200 times over the course of two years and change… if we… were to start counting from 2004, it would be about 100,000 reps with a similar 90-95% retention rate — that means something on the order of ten thousand failures." — _Aim to Fail_ (≤2009-04-02) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/aim-to-fail/
- "SRSing turns shallow failure into success through repetition and moulding intervals to suit your needs. Timeboxing, meanwhile, is a way to systematically (and, again, repeatedly) turn deep or total failure into shallow failure." Definitions: "Shallow failure: showing up and missing shots / Deep failure: (showing up but) not taking shots / Total failure: not showing up at all". — _The Three Types of Failure_ (undated; c. 2018 by post ID) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/the-different-types-of-failure-how-to-tell-them-apart-and-how-to-use-them/
- "So you didn't do your reps today. Yes, yes, bad you, slap on the wrist. There's a helicopter standing by… If you can't do 100%, your next option is not 0%." — _Failing Gracefully_ (≤2013-01-26) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/failing-gracefully/
- "It's okay to forget some… [the SRS] gave me permission to not be 100% while guaranteeing me an effortless 90~95%. SRS is not about perfection… Both are about extremely high-functioning imperfection." — _Start Dirty_ (≤2013-07-16) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/dont-start-clean-start-dirty-why-a-clean-slate-is-bad-for-you-and-what-to-do-about-it/

**Write-out-on-miss rule for sentences; always for kanji:**

- Reader: "at about 1 minute per card writing everything out, I tend to burn out pretty fast." Khatz: "Writing out the sentence (or some part of it, e.g. the part you got wrong) each time you miss it is enough*. In fact, it's ideal… *N.B.: Just so we're clear, this post is about sentences. For kanji, you're going to want to write everythaang out." — _About SRS/Sentence Writing Practice_ (2009-09-26) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/about-srssentence-writing-practice/

**Deletion (rules):**

- Precedence rules DADRA: "Don't Add, Delete, Review, Add. 0. Don't Add… If in doubt, don't add anything to the SRS… 1. Delete… There is perhaps nothing more threatening to your long-term SRSing prospects than bad cards… What about 'essential' language elements like individual kanji…? My original answer to that would have been a 'yes, suck it up'. However, over time, my opinion has changed… it is better to, yes, delete even cards containing essential, fundamental language elements. Call it 'lazy processing': you can always undelete, or re-add the cards later… I personally prefer to delete one card a time. I say, resist the impulse to 'push the reset button'… I virtually always do my deleting while reviewing… if you're doing reviews but you get bored by one or more cards… just start deleting and don't even bother stop until you hit a card that makes you want to do reviews. 2. Review… Click. Show back. Set score. Next. 3. Add… if in doubt, adding cards is the least important thing you can do." — _SRS Precedence Rules_ (2010-03-06) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-precedence-rules/
- "Delete (or Edit): Sucky sentence items… Delete it. Edit it if you really feel like it. But if editing feels like a waste of time, and for me it often does, then deletion is definitely the way to go. Think of deletion as pruning or weeding… Length is not the only reason to delete a sentence item. Sentence items you just don't quite 'get', or that you're afraid might be wrong or awkward, also make good candidates for deletion." — _Popping Bubblewrap_ (≤2008-06-22) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/
- Live deletion stats: "Repcount: 135 / Added: 2 cards / Deleted: 100 cards. / Total: ~235 cards processed, ~42% deleted… If you've got a really sucky deck, you could end up literally halving your cardcount — I once did. In the extreme, you could end up with only 10% of your original deck… Sentences are interchangeable. Motivation to learn is not… You can keep being liberal about what enters your SRS deck, just be liberal about what leaves it, as well. Garbage in, garbage out." ### Inferences

- Khatz's grading is forgiving by design: a stumbling-but-correct answer is a pass (3), and "take the top" biases the whole scale upward. He never advocates binary pass/fail, but his "default 3" plus "don't be harsh" produce a nearly binary practice in effect.
- "Never reset" has two faces: never reset a _deck_ (clean slate) and never go back and redo RTK from scratch. He does not discuss resetting individual card intervals.
- "Delete, don't edit" is his personal default, stated with an explicit allowance for editing; the stronger rule is "delete _during_ reps".

### Gaps

- No explicit statement about Anki's Again/Hard/Good/Easy or about how to treat "Easy" — his scale was 0–5 throughout. No post gives seconds-per-card targets; the only per-card timings are reader-reported (J.R.: "about 1 minute per card writing everything out"; Maya: 100 reps in under 30 minutes; a reader in _The Difference Between AJATT and School_ reports 250 reps in 45–60 minutes). No leech-threshold number is given anywhere.

## KQ3. How did Lazy Kanji cards look and why; how did his kanji advice relate to word cards?

### Takeaway

2006–2009: classic Heisig cards, keyword + story (+ pictures) on the front, kanji on the back, keyword→kanji only, readings deferred to sentences, "continue reviewing kanji after finishing RTK". 2010-03: **Lazy Kanji** — front = the bare kanji; back = keyword(s) + pasted J-J (or MDBG) dictionary entry; task = write the kanji once by hand while looking at it (later: look, then write from memory) and give a ballpark meaning; reading optional; grading rewards meaning, reading is "extra credit". Rationale: pure copy-paste, no stories, no keyword collisions, writing + recognition in one, and "the SRS handles exposure timing" so struggle is unnecessary. Kendo mod (2010-12, guest): adds a fill-in-the-blank mnemonic sentence on the front. Kanji-first stayed the rule ("KANJI FIRST"), with 2014's "early cut-off" (switch to MCDs at ~1000) and the 2020 "deliberate overtraining" position. Kanji cards relate to word cards as a _prerequisite_: "A compound is meaningless until you know its parts"; readings are learned on sentence/MCD cards, not kanji cards.

### Inferences

- The kanji card and the word/sentence card were designed to never overlap: the kanji card trains _form+core meaning_, the sentence/MCD card trains _reading+usage_. Lazy Kanji blurred this slightly by letting readings on the back "for extra credit", and the MCD era (2010+) moved kanji _writing_ onto the cloze card as well (see rigabamboo's 合縁奇縁 example).
- The 2013 "keyword collision is okay" explicitly reverses Heisig's single-keyword constraint, which Khatz had praised in 2010 as "a really cool and revolutionary idea".

### Gaps

- Khatz never published a numbered "Lazy Kanji" grading rubric beyond the Surusu post; no data on retention for Lazy Kanji ("no long-term data" was never updated). No post specifies what the kanji front looked like in the MCD era beyond "I just flipped existing cards backwards".

## KQ4. How did the monolingual dictionary shape the back of the card, and which J-J dictionaries did he recommend?

### Takeaway

From the start the answer side was a pasted dictionary entry; "go monolingual" meant swapping J-E entries for J-J ones (first side by side, then J-J only), including "definitions of definitions" recursively, with furigana allowed on the answer. Switch point: "about 500-1000 sentences" (2007), or simply **look up words you already know** (2008). Recommended J-J sources, in order over time: Sanseido Web Dictionary (concise, beginner-friendly), Infoseek/Goo (大辞林-based), Yahoo!辞書 (more examples; 大辞泉), Weblio, ALC onomatopoeia dictionary (but _not_ Eijirou), Kotobank (2013, preferred over Goo), a children's 国語辞典 on paper (チャレンジ小学国語辞典, Momoko), electronic denshijisho (Canon Wordtank V90 etc.). Dictionary example sentences were a primary sentence source ("use the dictionary itself to break your dependence on dictionaries"), and the recursive look-up chain became a card chain.

## KQ5. What, in his framing, is the card for and what is immersion for? (input before output, fun, tadoku, shadowing)

### Takeaway

Immersion is "the foundation and center of the process"; the SRS is "a glue and bridge… a sealing agent" that keeps what the environment delivers from leaking out, and a "backup" against decay — explicitly _not_ "the main course". Reps are the small active slice (1–3 hours/day at most; "SRS sprints"); passive time "should far outstrip active time". Input precedes output "ALWAYS"; output "comes when it comes" after a critical mass; shadowing is the one output-shaped practice he describes (imitating one actor as a "surrogate parent"). Fun is the master constraint ("If it is not fun, then it is not of AJATT"). Extensive/random reading (tadoku/randoku, ReadMOD) is a late (2013) addition framed as "barely even a method".

### Cited Findings

**What the SRS is for vs. what immersion is for:**

- "As things stand right now, the immersion environment is still the foundation and center of the process. SRS acts like a glue and bridge. The SRS ensures that information from the environment is not lost, again acting as a sealing agent of sorts and a bridge into a more free-wheeling, on-the-fly enjoyment and use of the language… what's real is the environment… If you run away from that, trying to escape to the comforting (?), sometimes familiarly school-like arms of your SRS, then you are, in a sense, running away from reality… in my experience with and observation of purely SRS-centric, low-immersion language learners, I have yet to see good results… high-concentration, high-quantity exposure to engaging (=fun) native materials is a far better overall predictor of fluency than SRSing." — _Secrets to Smoother SRSing, Part 7_ (≤2009-05-14) — https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/secrets-to-smoother-srsing-part-7-the-place-of-pre-mined-srsing-and-other-ramblings/
- "I use the SRS; I have it do work for me that I would otherwise have to do [dynamically sorting 15,000 paper flashcards into dated boxes?…]; it is my secretary; it schedules my reviews so I don't have to. I wouldn't walk into any language unarmed with an SRS. But for too many people SRSing has become the main course… The only technique I used was maximizing enjoyable Japanese exposure time such that it asymptotically approached 24 hours/day." — same post.

### Inferences

- The division of labour is consistent 2007→2020: environment supplies frequency/volume and selects what matters (Pareto); the card stores the few things you chose and prevents decay; output is an emergent property of input + reps. Cards are never a _source_ of language, only a _retainer_.
- Fun and "easy" are the selection criteria for both media (massive turnover) and cards (deletion); the two practices are the same practice applied at different granularities.

## KQ6. Timeline of how his advice changed (2006→2020) and what he explicitly retracted

### Takeaway

The architecture (immersion + kanji-first + SRS + monolingual) never changed; the _card_ and the _attitude to the SRS_ did: sentences (2006) → shorter sentences, delete freely (2008) → "SRS is a servant" / fun first / SRS holidays (2008–09) → Lazy Kanji + timeboxing + "don't add; delete; review; add" (2010) → MCDs replace sentences (deck killed 2010-10-18, announced 2012) → "no clean slates", bulk-but-not-total deletion, 90-min cap on study (2011–13) → RTK early cut-off (2014) → micro-timeboxed reading, denshijisho return (2017) → multimedia MCD/MXD from YouTube, "overtraining" kanji (2020).
