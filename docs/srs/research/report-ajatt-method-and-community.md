# Khatz's one-blank rule outlived his MCDs

Khatzumoto never prescribed a single card format; he prescribed one invariant and changed everything around it. The invariant, stated in 2008 and repeated through 2020, is that a card must have "one and only one correct answer, and that this answer be short, clear and concise," with the back serving as "a clue" and never as the thing tested ([Using the SRS to Remember Names, 2010-05-29](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/weird-but-valuable-srs-hacks-using-the-srs-to-remember-names/); [10,000 Sentences: How, Oct 2006](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)). Around that invariant the front went from a bare native sentence (2006) to a 30-character clause (2008) to a native passage with one `＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃` blank (MCDs, built privately from summer 2010, published March 2012) to, in 2017, a dictionary entry with its furigana left visible and the reading and the kanji clozed separately. The back went from reading plus gist translation (2006) to a verbatim Japanese-Japanese entry (2007) to clozetext plus full passage plus entry (2012). He retracted, in his own words, his long example sentences, his four-step monolingual plan, his "never delete essential cards" rule, his no-furigana-on-MCD-fronts rule and the 10,000-sentence target itself. The community kept the invariant and discarded the massive context: within weeks of the 2012 launch readers reported MCD "burnout," 25-minute card creation, one passage spawning 10–30 cards, "false passes" between sibling clozes and single-kanji clozes that broke words apart, and by 2020–2026 MIA, Refold, Tatsumoto, Animecards and every current note type (Lapis, JPMN, Kiku) had converged on one native sentence or one word per card, one target graded, with a two-tier back where only the word, reading and one definition are read on a pass and everything else sits behind a tap, hover or collapse. No one in this lineage ever ran a controlled MCD-versus-sentence comparison; Khatz himself said "I can't justify any of it theoretically." For Bunki's N2–N1 learner mining their own words, the evidence supports a targeted meaning-retrieval card as the default, MCD as a deliberately gated preset for form, particle and collocation gaps with whole-word blanks and capped siblings, a reveal that shows target, reading, pitch and one J-J sense verbatim with English and everything else one tap away, and Again/Good grading on the target only with a delete-while-reviewing habit and a leech ladder of swap, hint, delete.

## Corrections to earlier research

The earlier project report and its notes (`docs/srs/research/report-sentence-mining-deck-design.md`, `notes-community_methods.md` §1) attributed the "What is it about these MCDs?" series to Khatzumoto. The new notes read the bylines: Parts 1–5 and the BONUS post (March 2012) are by guest author **rigabamboo**, who opens Part 1 with "Rigabamboo here with another installment of the best of the AJATT+ forum… Am I qualified to write this post? Most assuredly not. I'm a latecomer to the MCD craze" ([What is it about these MCDs? Part 1, 2012-03-04](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-1/)). Three consequences follow. The rule "Never quiz yourself on two kanji at once… I've got nine cards with one kanji to remember" is rigabamboo quoting forum member 安藤, not Khatz ([Part 3: The Format, 2012-03-11](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-3/)). The 吸収 worked example (a three-sentence paragraph with `############収` on the front) is reader アッシュ's card, and the gloss "It's not there to be read – just glanced at until you recall the answer" is rigabamboo's ([Part 1](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-1/)). The `++++` divider and `XXXXX` marker in those posts are reader conventions; Khatz's own marker is `＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃`. Khatz's own public MCD posts are exactly four: "10,000 Sentences is Dead. Let the MCD Revolution Begin!" (2012), "12 Free MCD Examples" (2012-10-07), "MCDs: But What If I Don't Understand the Meaning of the Whole?" (c. 2013-08) and "A New MCD Card Format for Japanese" (c. 2017-08), plus the undated multimedia "MXD" recipe (c. 2019–2020). The review register (`review-2026-10-04/RESEARCH.md`) had already flagged the Part 3 byline; this report extends the correction to Part 1 and to the `++++` convention.

Three further corrections. First, the earlier report wrote "AJATT MCD (2010+): Native passage with one blank… The blanked kanji/chunk… Many cards per passage" as if one-kanji blanks were Khatz's rule; his own thirteen published cards cloze units from one kanji (緒, 感, 覚, 劣) to a two-kanji word (卑劣) and multi-character chunks (研ぎ澄ま, がいます, すれば), and his Surusu "LP" mode generated 3–7-character clozes "for Japanese syntax" ([12 Free MCD Examples](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/); [Cloze Deletion Modes in Surusu, 2011-09-04](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-the-three-modes-for-cloze-deletions-in-surusu/)). Second, the earlier report's claim that "every tradition surveyed converges" on recognition TSCs understates the live split between sentence-front (Tatsumoto) and word-front (Animecards, TheMoeWay, Refold's JP1K) cards, which is documented below. Third, exact AJATT dates are unrecoverable from the mirror: the pages carry no publication date and INDEX dates come from pingbacks, so dates below are "≤ earliest comment" bounds or "c." estimates from post IDs, as the notes record.

## Khatz's sentence card put a bare sentence in front and a dictionary clue behind

The 2006 template was explicit about direction and about what the back is for. "The direction is from Japanese only," and "Note that the answer is not always necessarily the full 'answer' that you give, it's more a clue—definitions of words, etc." The published example is front 「お前は何者だ？」, back 「お・まえ　は（わ）　なに・もの　だ」 followed by verbatim J-J entries for おまえ【御前】, なにもの【何者】 and めした【目下】, a word from inside the first definition ([10,000 Sentences: How, Oct 2006](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)). Translations were a check, not content: "put those translations in the 'answer' field of your SRS; use them as a check of your understanding. But again, you just need to remember the gist of the translation (not the exact wording)" (same post). Furigana never belonged on the front: "Just make sure your SRS 'question' section has no furigana in it" ([FAQs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/faqs-frequently-asked-questions/)). But the back could carry as much help as needed: "I would include furigana/bopomofo/pinyin for the full text of monolingual definitions (i.e. those in the 'answer' section of your SRS) — after all, it's not like you are trying to test your knowledge of the answer section, so give yourself all the help you need" ([How To Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries, ≤2007-07-13](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/)).

By late 2007 his real deck had dropped English entirely. "Just to give you an idea of what life is really like on the streets of my SRS": front 「ＩＴ革命において、主要先進国のナショナルフラッグキャリアは熾烈な国際競争を繰り広げ、」, back only 「しれつ【熾烈】〔戦いなどの〕勢いがはげしく盛んなようす｡激烈｡猛烈｡」 ([Real Sample Sentences, ≤Nov 2007](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/real-sample-sentences/)). He enumerated the back's permitted contents: "[a] verbatim dictionary defs of words in the sentences [b] readings of the kanji in the sentences [c] verbatim dictionary defs of [a] [d] rarely, some other information (e.g. notes on what the key (grammar) point of the sentence is" (Khatz comment, 18 Nov 2007, [Does Input REALLY work?](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/does-input-really-work/)). Asked what to put on the back if not a translation: "Nothing. Only put in what you need. If readings are enough, then they are enough… often, the English will only pollute it, IMHO" (Khatz comment, 27 Aug 2007, [10,000 Sentences: Answers To Questions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-where/)). And the back was reference, not reading: "The defs are only for reference, I only look at them if I don't know the meaning of a word… So the defs don't always get looked at by any means" (Khatz comment, 20 Jan 2008, [Chinese Project Notes 8](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/chinese-project-notes-8-ch-ch-changes-stuff-that-applies-to-japanese-too/)). The monolingual switch came early for him, "at about 500-1000 sentences," and recursion was a feature: "your answer area may contain definitions of definitions. That's super!" ([10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)). The elaborate four-step transition plan of July 2007 was retracted a year later: "I know I've written on this topic before. FORGET WHAT I SAID. That explanation sucked; it was far too complicated. Just do this… Look up definitions of words you already know" ([How To Really Make the Transition to Monolingual Dictionaries, ≤2008-07-16](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-really-make-the-transition-to-monolingual-dictionaries/)). He recommended sites rather than titles: Sanseido Web "because it's a 'concise' dictionary. The entries are short and to the point" ([Great Starter Dictionary](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/great-starter-dictionary/)), Infoseek/Goo (大辞林-based), Yahoo (大辞泉), later "Lately, I'm preferring Kotobank to Goo — better dictionary selection and less visual clutter" ([Kotobank Dictionary Bookmarklet, ≤2013-10-27](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/kotobank-dictionary-bookmarklet/)), and he warned off Eijirou because "these examples have not been edited and have no usage cautions" ([More Online Japanese Dictionaries, ≤2007-07-01](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/more-online-japanese-dictionaries/)).

Selection and length rules tightened over time. Pick sentences "that stretch your knowledge slightly, not so much that you're lost, and not so little that you're simply tagging 'です' on the end," and "when sentences are your primary learning medium, you need to be able to trust what you read 100%" ([10,000 Sentences: Answers To Questions, Oct 2006](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-where/)). Never write your own: "Do not translate from English to Japanese… Good Japanese starts with mindlessly imitating good Japanese. Don't go inventing your own Japanese; no one will understand you" ([10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)). In June 2008 he confessed to misleading readers with long examples: "First of all, an admission of guilt. I have misled you. In some of my SRS item examples, I have shown some reeeeeeeearry long sentences… Anyway, I was wrong." The replacement rule was "KISS: Keep It Short and Sweet… a hard upper limit 30 characters (kanji-kana mix), with most items being 10-15 characters long… You might also consider including the original, full-length sentence in the answer section, for reference" ([Popping Bubblewrap, ≤2008-06-22](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/popping-bubblewrap-tips-for-better-srs-sentence-items/)). In the comments he gave the real reason: "the i+1 principle, and by extension 'fail-point count' lie at the core of all this… I personally had some basically i+1 sentences that were still too long (and therefore boring, for me)" (Khatz comment, 2008-06-23, same post). Polysemy got separate cards: "one sentence, one meaning. Fix a context. So 'A bun in the oven', cannot refer to both baking and pregnancy. Make one 'card'/QA pair that's about bakery, and a separate card about babies" ([How to Score Yourself on Repetitions, ≤2007-06-06](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/)).

The pass criterion, grading and deletion rules are where his practice was most concrete. A sentence counted as learned only if you could "1. Read it in full, aloud, with kanji, no furigana… 2. Know the meaning of every word in the sentence… 3. Understand the meaning of the entire sentence 4. Write (copy) out the sentence by hand" ([10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)); writing was later narrowed to "Writing out the sentence (or some part of it, e.g. the part you got wrong) each time you miss it is enough. In fact, it's ideal" ([About SRS/Sentence Writing Practice, 2009-09-26](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/about-srssentence-writing-practice/)). Already in 2007 he anticipated the targeted card: "As you advance, you might want to narrow your grading down to the part of the sentence that you were aiming to learn (since you advance gradually, almost every new sentence will only be partially new to you)" ([How to Score Yourself on Repetitions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/)). The scale was 0–5 with "3 – More or less juust right. Weak, struggling, but right. No mistakes" ([How To Score Yourself On Repetitions 2, ≤2007-05-24](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-srs-repetitions-2/)), and retention was "a rep score of 3 or above," which over "about 58000 flashcard reps" ran "about 91%" ([Aim to Fail, ≤2009-04-02](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/aim-to-fail/)). On Surusu the rule became anti-perfectionist: "Ironically enough, most people grade themselves too harshly. So: Don't be too harsh… Grade cards on a per rep basis: if you did well on the card this time around, then give yourself a high score" ([How To Score Reps In Surusu, ≤2011-07-27](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu/)), and for indecision "If you're stuck between two scores, say a 4 and a 5, take the high ground: give yourself the higher score. If you're stuck between many (i.e. three or more) scores… move to the middle… the (overall) midpoint score, which happens to be a 3 in Surusu. Think of it as your default" ([SRS Scoring Heuristics, 2012-04-15](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-scoring-heuristics/)).

Deletion was the master rule. "If in doubt, throw it out. Wondering whether or not to delete this sentence? That's an automatic delete right there… throw away most of what you marked up — 95% or more" ([Secrets to Smoother SRSing, Part 4, ≤2008-09](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/secrets-to-smoother-srsing-part-4-collect-em-to-throw-away/)). Precedence was "Don't Add, Delete, Review, Add… I personally prefer to delete one card a time. I say, resist the impulse to 'push the reset button'… I virtually always do my deleting while reviewing," and this post reversed an earlier stance on essential cards: "My original answer to that would have been a 'yes, suck it up'. However, over time, my opinion has changed… it is better to, yes, delete even cards containing essential, fundamental language elements. Call it 'lazy processing': you can always undelete, or re-add the cards later" ([SRS Precedence Rules, 2010-03-06](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-precedence-rules/)). One purge day ran "Repcount: 135 / Added: 2 cards / Deleted: 100 cards… ~42% deleted" ([When You Just Don't Feel Like Doing Sentence Reps Any More, 2010-01-23](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/i-just-dont-feel-like-doing-sentence-reps-any-more-dude/)). The one limit: "Point deletion good. Bulk deletion good. Total deletion bad… Delete 50%. Delete 90%. Dare I say it, delete 99%. Go to town. But hold on to the chosen few that work" ([SRS: How NOT to Delete Cards, ≤2013-07-11](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-how-not-to-delete-cards/)), and ambivalence is itself a deletion signal: "Cards you're ambivalent about are worse than cards that you hate… So recast ambivalence as hate. If in doubt, throw it out" ([SRS: If In Doubt, Throw It Out, ≤2013-06-16](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-if-in-doubt-throw-it-out-ambivalence-is-the-greatest-enemy/)). He noted deletion was reversible anyway: "Anki and Surusu both have reversible deletion options (IIRC Anki calls it 'suspend'; Surusu has a 'traschcan'), so it's not like deletion is even destructive" ([SRS and Self-Determination, ≤2013-07-02](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-and-self-determination/)). The frame around all of it: "the immersion environment is still the foundation and center of the process. SRS acts like a glue and bridge" ([Secrets, Part 7, ≤2009-05-14](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/secrets-to-smoother-srsing-part-7-the-place-of-pre-mined-srsing-and-other-ramblings/)).

## MCDs kept the context, shrank the test to one blank, and were never quantified

Khatz's own account of the switch: "In the summer of 2010, I innocently started experimenting with a new SRS technique… On October 18, 2010, my sentence deck breathed its final breath, and the '10,000 Sentences' method literally died… actually about 15,0000 [sic] sentence cards died that day." The technique stayed paywalled until 2012: "Only people in AJATT Plus (and, later, SilverSpoon) were allowed to know the whole truth" ([10,000 Sentences is Dead. Let the MCD Revolution Begin!, ≤2012-02-29](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds/)). The seed tweets show the deletion-size instinct predates the name: "This goes for cloze deletions, too. Blot out max 1 word. I often blot out less than that (0.5~0.75 words)" ([AJATT Twitter Tweets, 2010-06-26](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/ajatt-twitter-tweets-for-week-of-2010-06-26/)). His core argument is a single sentence: "MCDs are low-stress and easy to do reps on, because there is only a single point of comparison/failure (that point may be repeated within a single card, but it's the same point)," which is why they "work for all levels… forgiving of the incomplete knowledge of early-stage players… because you only have to get the hidden text right," and why "Everything becomes i+1, because we're only ever handling one thing at a time." He also made length the diagnosis of what had failed before: "MCDs free you from the shackles, the crushingly unnatural brevity of short, near-context-less sentences… MCDs (ironically) also free you from the mental and physical burden of overly long sentence cards" (same post). He did not tell readers to delete their sentence decks: "Make a new, separate, MCD-only deck; Fill it with stuff I love… there's no reason that both methods can't co-exist. It's not an either/or thing" (same post).

The only Khatz-authored card format on the public blog is the October 2012 set. "The front contains the cloze (the blank represented by ＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃ or some other equally awesome marker). The back contains the clozetext (the correct answer to the blank; the filler to the donut hole, if you will) as well as additional materials like definitions, readings, sound files and any other relevant reference material." The principle: "instead of having 1 card with 9 unknowns, you have 9 cards with 1 unknown each. But you keep the same massive context as when you had a single big, bad, intimidating, f-off card. 9 is a fake number" ([12 Free MCD Examples, 2012-10-07](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/)). Three types. Bilingual, with English on the front as context: front `感覚を＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃すことじゃ。そうすればわかる。` / `Use your feelings, Obi-Wan, and find him you will.`; back **研ぎ澄ま** / the full sentence / the translation / `とぎすます【研ぎ澄ます】 II 〔精神を鋭くする〕 研ぎ澄まされた芸術的感覚 a keen artistic sense`. "Beginners, noobs and nervous nellies should focus just on bilingual cards. Just as with old skool sentence cards, don't go writing your own translations." Transitional, a cloze inside a J-J definition of a known word with the headword blanked everywhere it occurs: front `【＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃る】１ 食物を噛んで、呑み込む。「生で＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃る」「ひと口＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃てみる」`; back **食べ** / the full 大辞泉-style entry / `たべる【食べる】 I 〔食う〕eat`. "The front of the card is monolingual, the back of the card is bilingual." Monolingual, "especially well-suited to long, context-rich passages of L2 text": front = a Yomiuri headline plus three paragraphs with `【「真犯人は＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃」…ＰＣ遠隔操作「犯罪予告」】`; back **卑劣** / `ひ‐れつ【卑劣／×鄙劣】 ［名・形動］品性や言動がいやしいこと。人格的に低級であること。また、そのさま。「─な行為」` / the full passage / the source link. A sibling card blanks only the second kanji: `【「真犯人は卑＃＃＃＃＃＃＃＃」…】` → **劣**. "Focus first on bilingual and transitional cards. Get a few thousand of those under your belt. Your time will come" (same post).

The operational rules he published are few. Context size: "Create an MCD card containing the headline and the first three paragraphs of the adjoining article… Hide (cloze) the words you didn't know. Obviously, after putting their dictionary definitions (monolingual or bilingual, your choice) and pronunciation aids (if necessary) onto the back of the card. Don't worry about understanding the whole article or knowing every word in the article. MCDs allow partial ignorance (partial knowledge)" ([How to Learn to Read L2 Newspapers…, c. 2014-07](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-learn-to-read-l2-newspapers-even-though-you-suck-right-now-and-it-seems-impossible/)). Cards per text: the AJATT+ tip title "MCD Tip: Only 3 Clozetexts At A Time" ran the same week as "With great power to create cards comes great responsibility to delete them. #SRS #MCDs #culling" ([AJATT Twitter Tweets, 2013-03-09](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/ajatt-twitter-tweets-for-week-of-2013-03-09/)), and the MXD recipe says "Stop when you've done this for three words or get bored or tired, whichever comes first" ([How To Turn a YouTube Video into a Permanent Learning Tool, c. 2019–20](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-turn-a-youtube-video-into-a-permanent-learning-tool-i-e-a-series-of-awesome-multimedia-srs-mcd-mxd-cards/)). His tooling institutionalised both the explosion and the cull: Surusu's singleton mode "Automatically creates clozetexts and cloze deletions that are exactly 1 character in length… Great for Chinese MCDs and Japanese syntax MCDs," its LP mode makes "3~7-character-long permutations… Great for Japanese syntax and usage MCDs" ([Cloze Deletion Modes in Surusu, 2011-09-04](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-the-three-modes-for-cloze-deletions-in-surusu/)), and "Cull virgin cards… is a decimator function, reducing a maximum of 10% of the deck's overall cardcount while only targeting virgins. Culling is useful for a deck that has become unmanageably overloaded with new cards… by… multiple cloze deletion card creation" ([Surusu Stats Definitions, ≤2011-08-04](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/surusu-stats-definitions/)). Comprehension of the passage was explicitly not required: "Yes, that is entirely possible. That's why you fall back to bilingual MCDs. If you don't get it, don't use it. If you don't like it, don't use it. If it's too hard or boring, delete it" ([MCDs: But What If I Don't Understand the Meaning of the Whole?, c. 2013-08](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/mcds-but-what-if-i-dont-understand-the-meaning-of-the-whole/)). Grading on MCDs follows from the single failure point; the only explicit statement is pre-MCD: "If the quote is a cloze deletion, then I grade myself my ability to fill in the (one-word) blank, just like any other informational card" ([Why The Way We Read Sucks, Part 5, ≤2010-04](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-the-way-we-read-sucks-and-how-to-fix-it-part-5-examples-shown-and-questions-answered/)).

The 2017 post is his last format change and an explicit reversal. "up until now, the general advice (from me) on how to make MCD cards was that you should remove any and all furigana (kanji readings) from the front of the card… After several months of experimentation, I am pleased to inform you that this is not necessary — and that you actually want to leave the furigana there, right on the front of the card, so: `はんね 【半値】 定価の半分。半分の値段。 「半値以下に値下げする」` And include it as part of your base clozetexts, so: はんね 半値." The rationale is divide-and-conquer: "we separate learning (testing) the readings of a new word from testing kanji knowledge of that same word. Ironically, while this makes each individual card easier, it actually sharpens your knowledge of both kanji and readings, and, going forward, prevents 'native-like forgetfulness' where you can accurately produce a reading but not necessarily always a kanji from memory" ([A New MCD Card Format for Japanese, ≤2017-08-26](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/a-new-mcd-card-format-for-japanese-even-lazier-and-more-effective-than-before/)). Note what this implies: by 2017 his default base was a dictionary entry, not a news passage, and one headword produced "about nine" cards. Kanji were handled separately throughout: Lazy Kanji cards (front 膂, back keyword plus pasted J-J entry, "To write out the kanji once, by hand, in full, while seeing it… giving the ballpark meaning is the main thing… giving a reading is just extra credit") were a parallel deck, started with the caveat "I've been doing it for about two weeks. I have no long-term data" ([Lazy Kanji Cards, 2010-03-20](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/lazy-kanji-cards-a-new-srs-card-format/)), and by 2014 he allowed cutting RTK short: "Cut off early, maybe at, say, 1000 kanji, start MCDs, picking up other kanji along the way" ([What If RTK Isn't Working For Me?, ≤2014-01-16](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-if-heisig-isnt-working-for-me-early-cut-off-kanji/)).

What is missing matters as much. He gave no retention figure, no seconds-per-rep, no new-cards-per-day for MCDs; Ken Seeroi's "how many MCD reps do you typically do a day?" went unanswered ([12 Free MCD Examples, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/)). His epistemic stance was stated plainly: "I don't know how to explain why stuff works or even how. I can't justify any of it theoretically… All I know is…there's stuff that I did and do and it works pretty awesome" ([If You Played Songs The Way You Read Books, 2013-02-23](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/if-you-played-songs-the-way-you-read-books-you-would-hate-music/)), and "OK, 'scientific' is a bit of a strong word… Probably the word we're looking for is 'empirical'" ([Long Live MCDs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds/)). The posts carrying the finer rules ("MCD 150: 50 Tips," "MCD 120: 20 Common Noob Questions," "The MCD Card Length Paradox," "Don't MCDs Make It So The Cards Are Too Similar?") were paid AJATT+ content and are not in the mirror. The launch post was also a sales page for the "MCD Revolution Kit," a context the Koohii forum noticed at once ("Pay me money and ill tell you what it is," pervygoat, 6 Mar 2012, [Koohii thread 9148](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309145250/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-9148.html)).

## Readers tested MCDs within weeks and the descendants dropped them

Testimony and guidance need separating, because the first is where the costs show up. On the launch threads themselves, readers reported leaving MCDs before most readers had heard of them. kalek (23 Feb 2012): "I returned to plain sentences too, after my ongoing MCD burnout since last November, and I'm loving it." フーラン (23 Feb 2012): "I took all the parts of MCD I loved (+1 effect, highlighting instead of clozed and more context) and applied it to my old sentence deck. MCDs just took too much time for me to review when I could have been reading all the books I was dying to read" ([10,000 Sentences Is Dead, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead/)). That is the targeted sentence card described ten years before Tatsumoto named it. ダンちゃん (29 Feb 2012), a graduate student writing in Japanese: "kind of funny seeing my praise of MCDs given that I don't actually use them any more!… I can read Japanese just fine these days… MCDs are possibly a very good way to break into an intimidating sino-Character language and build yourself up to an advanced level" ([Long Live MCDs, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds/)). Valerie (2 Mar 2012) raised the two review-side problems: "Am I supposed to read the entire card every time it comes up?… I feel like the fact that it's so long makes me feel…bogged down" and the spoiler, "when there's a really difficult particle somewhere near the end of the paragraph, but the answer keeps getting spoiled because that same particle appears in a really easy-to-understand spot earlier" (same thread). The strongest critique came from a Mandarin learner, Thomas Smith (11 Aug 2013): "Splitting Hanzi compounds into separate cards seriously screwed everything up… 1) When reading, I'd fail to recognise words I'd reviewed, because split cards had trained me to view them separately… 2) SRS scheduling got mangled by 'false passes'. Eg upon hitting 名######## I should have written 子, failed… But… I met the card ########字 just beforehand, and 'passed' with 字… 3) When outputting, I'd constantly not know whether to produce 成果 or 果成… Clozing words instead of parts of words has eliminated all 4 of the above problems… My failure rate on SRS has increased by about 5-10%. But that's not a bad thing, it's a genuine reflection of my level. TL;DR Clozing compounds one Kanji at a time >>> weak. Clozing words as words >>> strong" ([12 Free MCD Examples, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/)). Khatz never replied. Positive testimony clustered on specific gains: sargon, quoted by Khatz, "MCDs have exposed a weakness with particles that I didn't know I had"; 名前 (7 Mar 2012), a beginner, "the amount of cards I failed went down dramatically" after switching to micro-MCDs with Japanese and English on the front; JustinB on Spanish por/para ([Long Live MCDs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds/)). By 2015 one reader had "about 21000 right now which seems possibly excessive. I delete all the time but I'm still in the virgin cards" (Gabriel, 13 Feb 2015, same thread).

The Kanji Koohii forum ran its own trial in March 2012. KREVA posted his first MCD and reported "it took me nearly 25 minutes to make this card," then concluded "I'd much rather do that with a sentence or two worth of context as opposed to a paragraph." IceCream: "If they're that long, you'll simply learn the card… deleting one word out of a paragraph is probably the worst way possible to test your knowledge of it." oregum timed himself "reviewing sentences at a rate of over 300/hr… when I review, I simply glance over the answer field." howtwosavealif3, monolingual for years with a 539-card MCD deck, set the entry condition: "I dont' think you should do MCD till you go monolingual with your cards anyway… It involves outputting so it only makes [sense] that you have some level of japanese intuition," and clozes partially ("＿そる" → そそる). Nukemarine: "Works pretty good with grammar type sentences… Works GREAT with Subs2SRS type decks… One thing I'd recommend is not to look for paragraphs to teach you words. You want to learn words so that you understand the paragraph." syntoad described one sentence producing "around 10-30 cards"; howtwosavealif3: "I would make 2 cards… MAX" ([Koohii thread 9148, p.1](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309145250/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-9148.html), [p.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309145255/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-9148-page-2.html)). Jalup's 2014 article "Filling In The Blanks With MCDs" granted the pros ("forced 'output'," "words with three or more kanji," "particles") and the cons ("so much content on the front… you wind up doing a lot of skimming"), and advised "I wouldn't suggest anybody shift exclusively to MCDs until they are at least at an intermediate level… people who are intermediates or higher should use MCDs to learn words that are 3+ kanji." Its commenters supplied the numbers: "I did it for about 500 sentences, which blew out to 1000 cards… Seemed like I was seeing the same content everyday"; and a user with 1,367 MCD notes (4,674 cards) beside a 10,000+ J-J sentence deck found the format "excel[s] at… anything grammar related, like particles or conjugations… compounds of three or more kanji," but "my card… with the cloze around 想… was now actually also testing whether I knew the (presumably subtle) difference between 思想 and 思考… making clozes of stuff you only half understand is much riskier" ([JALUP, 28 Jul 2014, with comments](https://web.archive.org/web/20160617231039/http://japaneselevelup.com/filling-blanks-with-mcds/)). A 2017 Koohii 30-day cloze trial ended the same way: "while very effective, it's too time consuming. Not the review itself, but the card creation… there are too many similar words with too many similar sounds, so that even simple hints don't always work"; the replacement was the sentence with the target in kana on the front, "Maybe it's 10% less effective than cloze-deletion, but it's 90% faster" (cophnia61, [Koohii thread 14428](https://web.archive.org/web/20190309101737/https://forum.koohii.com/thread-14428.html)).

The guides that followed codified the retreat. MIA's Japanese Quickstart (2020) restated the sentence-card era without the word MCD anywhere: "A sentence card is an SRS card with a target-language example sentence on the front, and whatever is needed to understand the sentence on the back… it's important that the sentence on the front be a one-target (1T) sentence"; "Once you're past the basic vocab phase, putting full English translations on the back of cards is not recommended… it's best to stick to just putting the English definition of the target word/structure"; "full kanji with no furigana"; sentences "between 3 and 7 words long"; "If you felt like you understood the sentence well, you don't have to read the back of the card"; leeches at five lapses, "just get rid of them"; "we don't recommend ever exceeding 30 cards per day… the SRS is simply a supplement" ([MIA JP Quickstart, Dec 2020 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20201220134528/https://massimmersionapproach.com/table-of-contents/stage-1/jp-quickstart-guide/)). Refold (2026) keeps the mining rule ("Sentences with 1 (or maybe 2) unknown words… Don't mine: Sentences with 3+ unknown words"), the deletion ethic ("Words aren't rare Pokémon… A bad card isn't your fault; it's the card's fault"), lowers new cards to "5–10," adopts FSRS, says "You can entirely ignore the 'Easy' and 'Hard' buttons," and never mentions cloze; its JP1K deck is word-front ("Step 1: Recall the word's reading… Step 2: Recall the word's meaning. Then flip the card over") ([Refold, Learning words with Anki](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/learning-words-with-anki); [Sentence Mining](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/sentence-mining); [JP1K](https://refold.la/decks/buy/fundamental-vocabulary-to-learn-japanese)). Tatsumoto, who claims the AJATT name ("Following Khatzumoto's retirement in 2023, Tatsumoto took the reins as his successor"), is the most explicit rejection: "there is always an infinite number of answers for any given 'fill in the blank' question. For this reason cloze cards stop working once the intervals grow big enough. You either memorize the card itself… Or you just guess… I can't imagine a good application for MCDs and cloze cards in general when learning languages," and on the old blog, MCD "hasn't received much adoption over the years due to being too difficult and complicated" ([What's AJATT?](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/whats-ajatt.html); [What do you think of using MCDs…](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/what-do-you-think-of-using-mcds-to-train-production.html); [Resources](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/resources.html)). His TSC is the フーラン hybrid: bold the target, grade only on it, "On average, it takes 20 seconds to answer a sentence card," and the back needs only the definition ([Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)). Animecards lists cloze among formats "either useless or helpful only in specific situations," argues "anime cards are superior" because "Review data suggests anime cards can be reviewed 2 to 4 times faster than sentence cards" (no data shown), and demotes the sentence to the back "only as a reminder if you fail the card" ([Animecards, Anki: Card Types](https://animecards.site/ankicards/)). Shoui (TheMoeWay) credits AJATT for "The mindset, the pragmatic approach," puts word and sentence on the front but "rarely ever read[s] the sentence," grades on "the MEANING, and then the reading," uses PassFail and deletes his decks yearly ([Shoui method](https://learnjapanese.moe/shouimethod/)).

What survived everywhere is a short list: immersion as the engine with the SRS as supplement; native sentences from one's own reading; one unknown per card (i+1 renamed 1T); no furigana on the front; the monolingual transition; delete freely. What was dropped everywhere is MCD as a default and "massive" context, which reverted to 3–7 words (MIA) and then to a single word with the sentence as support (Animecards, Lapis default, JP1K). What survived as a niche is equally stable across 2012, 2014 and 2017: cloze for particles and conjugations, for 3+-kanji compounds, for production, and for keeping subs2srs context alive. Those are also the only cloze cards modern note types still ship as options (JPMN's cloze card type, Lapis's `IsAudioCard`). No one in this lineage measured anything; "many in the Japanese community have shifted to MCDs" (Jalup 2014) and "hasn't received much adoption" (Tatsumoto 2022) are both partisan assertions.

## Every current template splits the back into read-on-pass and tap-on-lapse

The 2012 commenters were already hand-building what the note types later automated. ライトニング's monolingual card: front 「屁を放出することを放屁と言う。」, back the sentence with readings in 「」 after each word, then J-J entries for 屁, 放出 (two senses copied) and 放屁 ("If there are multiple definitions, I copy both"). 魔法少女☆かなたん: front 「地球が太陽のまわりをまわる周期は約一年だ。」, back the full kana reading with ・ separators and "the definition of any words that I feel I need. In this case, it was only 周期… I usually look for the specific definition that fits and add only that." ブライアン: "You copy the definition from a (online/software) dictionary… Writing your own is far too error prone and too slow" ([Part 4, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/what-is-it-about-these-mcds-part-4-the-active-output/)). lapis-modified states the modern rule in one paragraph: "Essential: Word, Reading, Meaning. Non-essential: Picture, Sentence, Audio. When reviewing and passing the card, we only pay attention to the essential information. When lapsing a card, we use the non-essential information to remind ourselves. This allows us to review cards very quickly while maintaining high review quality" ([lapis-modified README](https://github.com/friedrich-de/lapis-modified)). Lapis implements it structurally: the back runs frequency rank, word with ruby furigana beside a pitch display and audio buttons, picture, sentence (furigana on hover by default), then "a single definition box… with edge-prev / edge-next tappable edges" to page through dictionaries in the order SelectionText → MainDefinition → Glossary, and `MiscInfo` inside a collapsed `<details>` ([Lapis src/back.html](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/src/back.html); [user_settings.md](https://github.com/donkuri/lapis/blob/main/docs/user_settings.md)). JPMN routes dictionaries into `PrimaryDefinition` (visible), `SecondaryDefinition`, `ExtraDefinitions`, `AdditionalNotes` and `ExtraInfo` (all collapsed by default, toggled by keys 8/9/0/[), and its `AdditionalNotes` is where "the sentences surrounding the mined sentence" go, "in a collapsible field to reduce vertical space… for any info that is not crutial to understanding the tested content" ([JPMN fieldref.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/fieldref.md); [ui.md](https://github.com/Aquafina-water-bottle/jp-mining-note/blob/master/docs/docs/ui.md)). Kiku adds grouped, paginated contexts and a "Kanji Web: Explore your other notes that use the same kanji, same reading, same expression" ([Kiku features.md](https://github.com/youyoumu/kiku/blob/main/apps/docs/mds/features.md)). Tatsumoto's prescription matches: "The back of the card contains everything you need to understand the target word: Readings and pitch accents; Example sentence; Definitions; Audio; and so on," and after the first learn "if you are able to recall the meaning well then you move on to the next card without listening to the audio or reading the sentence on the back" ([Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)); "It is okay to have furigana on the back of your cards. Any information on the back should help you understand the target word and not be in the way" ([Going monolingual, 2024-03-28](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)); pitch "on the back of your cards, but don't take it into account when grading yourself" ([How should I grade… pitch accent?, 2022-12-20](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-should-i-grade-cards-once-i-add-pitch-accent.html)).

No current template has a synonyms or antonyms field; the only synonym information is the ⇔ and [類語] cross-references that ride along inside a verbatim 大辞泉/大辞林 entry, and TheMoeWay's FAQ redirects near-synonym questions to the dictionary and immersion: "You need to use a J-J dictionary such as the 大辞林 to find out the difference. If you're not good enough to read a J-J definition yet then don't worry about it because you can also find out the difference through immersion" ([TheMoeWay FAQ](https://learnjapanese.moe/faq/)). No current template prints static kanji etymology or mnemonics on a vocabulary back; "kanji family" has been re-implemented as a query over the learner's own notes (JPMN kanji hover: "shows you if you have seen the kanji in previous cards or not"; Kiku Kanji Web), and Tatsumoto's fix for a kanji you keep misreading is more cards, not more back: "I make several TSCs with different words sharing the kanji (悠々 / 悠長 / 悠久)" ([Tatsumoto, furigana post](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/i-forget-kanji-readings-in-sentences-should-i-use-furigana.html)). And no guide recommends several visible example sentences on one back; Tatsumoto says hunting extra sentences per sense "is not efficient. Don't do it" and makes a second card only "When a word comes up in your immersion that you already learned, but it is used to express a different meaning" ([How to approach words with multiple meanings?, 2023-01-16](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-approach-words-with-multiple-meanings.html)). On English, the line runs from Khatz's "pollute" through Tatsumoto's "Don't add English definitions to your cards. Having both English and Japanese definitions is acceptable only during the transition period… copy a definition from a bilingual dictionary as a last resort" ([Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)) to Shoui's later mixed use ("Even if I have bilingual dicts in Yomitan, I am completely unphased by needing to read a monolingual definition") and Refold's hedged "Why It (Might) Matter… If you don't want to make the transition, then don't!" ([Refold, Monolingual Transition](https://refold.la/roadmap/library/monolingual-transition)). The grading rule that makes dense backs cost nothing on a pass is TheMoeWay's: "if the definition on the back made you understand the sentence better, then grade it as a Fail. If not, grade it as a Pass" ([TheMoeWay FAQ](https://learnjapanese.moe/faq/)). No source measures review time for dense versus minimal backs; the only quantified claims (20 s per sentence card, "2 to 4 times faster," 300/hr) are about the front.

## What this means for a Bunki N2–N1 card

**Default card type: targeted meaning retrieval, with MCD as a gated preset.** This resolves decision D1 in favour of the "読んで思い出す" preset for 語・表現・文法 and keeps the MCD preset as a deliberate choice, which is what the review register already leaned toward. The reasoning is not that MCDs were refuted; nobody tested them. It is that every cost the community documented (25-minute creation, 10–30 siblings per source, "seeing the same content over and over," false passes between siblings, blanks that admit several fillers or test an unintended contrast like 思想/思考) falls on exactly the kind of deck this learner has: 323 self-mined N2–N1 words with one encounter each, where 322 of 323 MCD anchors are written passages rather than native hits. The one thing Khatz never abandoned, "there is only a single point of comparison/failure," is satisfied equally by a bolded target in a sentence ([Long Live MCDs](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-is-dead-long-live-mcds/)), and he said so himself in 2007: "narrow your grading down to the part of the sentence that you were aiming to learn" ([How to Score Yourself on Repetitions](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-yourself-on-repetitions/)). The strongest pro-MCD evidence is for gains this learner may actually want (particles, conjugations, collocations, long compounds, output), so the MCD preset should be scoped to those: blank whole words or bounded chunks, never single kanji of a compound, following Thomas Smith's four failure modes ([12 Free MCD Examples, comments](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/12-free-mcd-examples/)); constrain the blank to a collocation partner (懸念を［　］抱く-type) or supply a reading/gloss hint so the answer set is small, because an open blank "stop[s] working once the intervals grow big enough" ([Tatsumoto](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/what-do-you-think-of-using-mcds-to-train-production.html)); accept reviewed alternatives rather than training source reconstruction; and cap siblings per source at three, which is Khatz's own ceiling ("Only 3 Clozetexts At A Time," "Stop when you've done this for three words"), with same-source siblings dispersed across days so one sibling cannot spoil the next (Valerie's complaint; Thomas Smith's false passes). Khatz's 2017 divide-and-conquer card is the right model for the one MCD the learner should keep by default: when a word is already known by meaning but not by reading or by kanji, a card that clozes the reading and a separate card that clozes the kanji, each "easier without making it too easy" ([New MCD Card Format](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/a-new-mcd-card-format-for-japanese-even-lazier-and-more-effective-than-before/)). An MCD-first choice remains defensible for this learner if they value passage practice and output over review speed; the data to decide that is the personal comparison the review register proposed, not anything in the literature.

**Context size on the front.** Khatz's late verdict is the usable one: "one-word cards are bad. Overlong, one-page cards are also bad" ([If Your SRSing Is Boring](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/if-your-srsing-is-boring-then-youre-doing-it-wrong/)). For N2–N1 news and academic words the 2008 beginner cap of 30 characters is too tight and the 2014 "headline and three paragraphs" is the thing readers refused to reread; a clause that fixes the sense, with the full source passage one tap away on the back, is where フーラン, KREVA ("a sentence or two worth of context"), MIA (3–7 words, "a complete clause") and Tatsumoto all landed. The front stays locked, exactly as the learner asked: no furigana, no English, no tap-to-define, because "it's not like you are trying to test your knowledge of the answer section" is a statement about the back only, and MIA and Tatsumoto both forbid furigana on the front.

**The reveal, tier one (read on every pass).** Target word, reading as furigana over the whole sentence (the learner's request; sanctioned by Khatz 2007 and Tatsumoto 2024), pitch accent shown but ungraded (Tatsumoto; JPMN default; Kaishi), and one Japanese definition: the dictionary entry copied verbatim, with the sense used in the sentence selected or bolded, never rewritten and never meant to be recalled word for word ("A definition is just one of many possible ways to describe the meaning… Having a general idea of the meaning in your head is enough," [Tatsumoto, How to review and grade TSCs?, 2022-12-26](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/how-to-review-and-grade-tscs.html)). Prefer learner-friendly dictionaries for the primary slot (旺文社国語辞典, 明鏡, 新明解 per Shoui; Khatz's own criterion was "short and to the point") with 大辞林/大辞泉 as the comprehensive fallback. Word audio where licensable; Tatsumoto notes sentence audio makes word audio unnecessary ([Discussing various card templates](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/discussing-various-card-templates.html)).

**The reveal, tier two (one tap away).** The English gloss of the target, the full-sentence translation, other dictionaries, the kanji family, synonyms, extra passages and the source. On English specifically: PR #116 asked for a visible meaning and the brief asks for "a click and a little work"; the tradition settles this by level, not taste. For a learner at N2–N1 studying words he mined himself, the defaults from Khatz ("pollute"), MIA (target definition only, no translation), Tatsumoto ("acceptable only during the transition period") and Shoui's own mixed use all point to the same compromise: the Japanese definition reads first, a short English gloss of the target sits behind a single tap and a per-deck "always show" switch honours the earlier request, and the sentence translation is never on reveal ("Never look at the English translation, ever… Reading just the definition of the target word should be enough to understand the entire sentence. If that's not the case, the sentence is not 1T," [Tatsumoto, 2022-11-30](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/should-i-put-english-translations-on-bilingual-tscs.html)). The kanji family should be built the way JPMN and Kiku build it, as a list of the learner's own enrolled words sharing the kanji or the reading, with 同/読/漢-style markers, not as etymology or mnemonics, which no vocabulary template prints and which Tatsumoto replaces with sibling words. Synonyms should be limited to the cross-references already inside the copied entry plus the learner's own enrolled near-synonyms; a free-standing synonym list has no precedent in any guide and TheMoeWay treats the distinction as a dictionary and immersion task. Extra passages belong in a collapsed "surrounding context" panel (JPMN `AdditionalNotes`, Kiku context pages) and, more usefully, as the source for a later sibling card once the anchor is stable, which is the anchor-then-diversify policy of the earlier report and Tatsumoto's "another card" rule for a new sense.

**Tap-to-define and add-to-list on the back.** This is the most AJATT-native feature on the learner's list. Khatz: "You will probably have to do a lot of looking up, such that your answer area may contain definitions of definitions. That's super!" ([10,000 Sentences: How](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/10000-sentences-how/)); Momoko: "Whenever I look up a word in my Japanese-Japanese dictionary, I enter the example sentence. Then I look up a word that's new in that sentence (or from the definition)" ([Why I'm in Love with my Japanese-Japanese Dictionary, ≤2007-07-25](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-i%E2%80%99m-in-love-with-my-japanese-japanese-dictionary/)); Tatsumoto: "If you encounter unfamiliar words in the definitions, put them aside. Come back and learn the words after you make your regular cards" ([Going monolingual](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/going-monolingual.html)). Two guardrails come from the same sources. Khatz's cut-off: "If you understand what a word means from its kanji (as you surely will), then just stop/break/cut off the recursion process. If the recursion keeps going and you still don't understand what you set out to understand, then skip to something easier" (Khatz comment, 2007-07-20, [Why Monolingual Dictionaries Are Worth Your Time](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-monolingual-dictionaries-are-worth-your-time/)); and DADRA's "0. Don't Add… If in doubt, don't add anything to the SRS," which argues that a tap on the back should capture the word to the 覚え list with its sentence, not enrol a card on the spot. In practice this makes the back a mining surface and the review a session in which the learner can act on recursion without paying for it in reviews.

**Grading.** Two buttons, Again and Good, graded on the target's meaning (and reading only when the card's task is reading), with Hard and Easy absent: MIA ("stick to Good/Again"), Refold ("You can entirely ignore the 'Easy' and 'Hard' buttons"), Shoui ("I always used PassFail"), and Khatz's own practice, which was 0–5 in name but "Don't be too harsh… Grade cards on a per rep basis… Two? Take the top. Many? Move to middle," which in effect is pass/fail with a bias to pass ([Surusu scoring](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-score-reps-in-surusu/); [Heuristics](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-scoring-heuristics/)). Use TheMoeWay's criterion as the on-screen rule: Again if the back made you understand the target better. Grade only the target: Khatz 2007, "the part of the sentence that you were aiming to learn"; Khatz 2012, "you only have to get the hidden text right"; Tatsumoto, "if you accidentally forget some part that is not highlighted, it shouldn't be taken into account when grading." Pitch shown, never graded (Tatsumoto; JPMN `PAShowInfo` off by default). For MCD cards grade the blank alone, and when a reviewed alternative filler is supplied, count it as Good and offer a repair, rather than failing the learner for not reproducing the author's word.

**Deletion and leeches.** Give deletion a one-tap, reversible place in the review loop, because Khatz's rule is to delete while reviewing ("I virtually always do my deleting while reviewing… just start deleting and don't even bother stop until you hit a card that makes you want to do reviews," [SRS Precedence Rules](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/srs-precedence-rules/)) and the descendants agree ("If a card is giving you trouble, delete it or suspend it," Refold). Set a leech threshold between MIA's 5 and Tatsumoto's 4–6, then run a repair ladder in this order: swap the sentence or passage for the next-ranked one (Tatsumoto: "Swap the card… Introduce a second card with the same target word… Edit the card and swap the sentence," [i-can-only-remember-word-due-to-context](https://tatsumoto-ren.github.io/blog/i-can-only-remember-word-due-to-context.html)), add a Lapis/Animecards-style hint "as a last resort," then delete or suspend. Expect deletion rates that would alarm a conventional app ("at least 5% and as much us 25~50% of the little that does get entered into the SRS, sucks and should be deleted," [Why The Way We Read Sucks, Part 2](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/why-how-we-read-sucks-and-how-to-fix-it-part-2/)) and allow bulk culls of never-reviewed cards, which is what Surusu's "Cull virgin cards" did, while never offering a whole-deck reset ("Total deletion bad"). Keep the two existing deck styles as presets over one collection with one queue, as the review register recommended; Khatz's own "Darwinian Game" (run both, add to both, keep the one that does not slip) is the right way to let this learner settle D1 with his own data ([Darwinian Game](https://alljapaneseallthetime.com/blog/how-to-make-big-srs-changes-the-darwinian-game/)).

**On "maximum punch."** Khatz's answer to density was never a denser card; it was more cards from the same source with one fail point each, "9 cards with 1 unknown each… the same massive context," plus "great responsibility to delete them." The community's answer was a layered back nobody reads on a pass. Put together, a Bunki card packs punch by making one retrieval unambiguous on the front, by reading exactly four things on reveal (target, reading, pitch, one Japanese sense), by placing everything orthogonal (English, other dictionaries, kanji family, synonyms, surrounding passages, source) one tap deep where it costs nothing on a pass and everything useful on a lapse, and by letting a tap on the back spawn the next card rather than lengthening this one.

## What remains unknown

Nobody has run a controlled comparison of MCDs against sentence or targeted cards, in Japanese or any language; Khatz's claims are self-described as empirical and anecdotal, Thomas Smith's "5-10%" is the only numeric effect reported and it concerns abandoning single-kanji clozes, and the modern guides' speed figures (20 s per sentence card, "2 to 4 times faster") carry no data. Khatz's paid materials (MCD 101/102/150/120/200, "The MCD Card Length Paradox," "Don't MCDs Make It So The Cards Are Too Similar?," "MCD Confusion Blues," MonoTran, QRG, SilverSpoon, Nutshell) and the AJATT+ forum are not in the mirror, so any finer rules on hints, context length, similarity between siblings and daily counts are unknown; the three 2018 "[SRS/MCD Bait]" posts are password-protected. Khatz never defined "partially correct" for an MCD, never gave a leech threshold, a seconds-per-rep or a new-cards-per-day figure for MCDs, and never replied to the word-fragmentation critique or to the minimum-information objection. Exact AJATT publication dates are unrecoverable; all dates here are comment-derived bounds. On the community side, reddit (r/ajatt, r/LearnJapanese), Discord logs, Koohii thread 9148 page 3 and thread 9249, Matt vs Japan's videos and his 2021–2023 reasons for tolerating vocab cards, Refold's own Japanese note type, Jalup's authoring rules for simplified J-J definitions, Kumasensei and LingQ were unreachable or second-hand, as flagged in the notes. No measurement exists of review time for dense versus minimal backs, of the interference between same-source sibling cards under FSRS, or of whether tap-to-define on the back changes retention; these are the questions the Bunki deck can answer for this one learner if it logs task, assistance and lapse data per card, which the review register already made a prerequisite.

## Conclusion

The history reads differently once the bylines are right. AJATT did not move from sentences to MCDs and then get overturned by its successors; Khatz moved from testing a whole sentence to testing one blank because long sentences had too many fail points, and the community moved from one blank to one bolded target because long passages had too much reading per rep. Both steps served the same rule, one failure point per card, and the step the community refused was the one Khatz actually sold, massive context on the front. The format that survived, and that Bunki should default to, is the one a reader described in a comment three weeks after the launch: highlight instead of blank, keep the context, grade one thing.

For an app, the useful residue of the MCD era is not the passage but the attitudes around it: that a source should yield several small cards rather than one heavy one, that siblings need capping and dispersing, that cards are disposable and deletion is a rep, and that the back is a workspace for recursion rather than a text to be read. The 2017 furigana reversal is the clearest signal of where Khatz himself ended up: separate easy cards for reading and for kanji, generated from one entry, each "easier without making it too easy." That is a design for a card generator, not for a card, and it is the part of his late practice that an N2–N1 learner mining his own words can still use.
