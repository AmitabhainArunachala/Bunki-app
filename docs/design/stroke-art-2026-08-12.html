筆順の五案

five art directions for the stroke-order page · 永字八法にて

一 · 墨と朱sumi & vermilion on kōzo

The living stroke is brushed in 朱 vermilion — the teacher's ink — then settles into 墨. Small seal-red numerals wait at each stroke's origin. The quietest of the five: calligraphy class at dusk.

二 · 岩絵具mineral pigments, one per stroke

Each stroke keeps its own nihonga pigment — 藍, 弁柄, 緑青, 金茶, 京紫 — laid translucent like watercolor, so the order of writing becomes the painting's own stratigraphy.

三 · 紺紙金泥gold ink on indigo sutra paper

The Heian sutra tradition: gold ink on deep-dyed indigo. Finished strokes cool to old gold; the living stroke burns brighter. Pairs naturally with the app's 夜 world.

四 · 筆勢the brush's momentum, kasure & comet

Dry-brush strokes that fray at the edges, a vermilion brush-tip comet riding each line as it draws, and earlier strokes receding into sepia — the page remembers the ORDER as depth in time.

五 · 春霞spring nihonga with sunago gold dust

A seasonal painting that happens to be a kanji: strokes in 若竹 and 萌黄 greens over 桜鼠, gold flecks scattered in the paper, a red 落款 seal in the corner. The most decorative — a festival version.
